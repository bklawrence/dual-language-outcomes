Tulsa Public Schools unveils growth strategy with the goal of expanding by 1,200 new seats
Oct 30 2026 12:18 PM

On Thursday, Tulsa Public Schools Superintendent Dr. Ebony Johnson presented a district-wide growth strategy aimed to expand enrollment by more than 1,200 seats in some of the district's most sought-after programs: elementary immersion and dual language, secondary virtual instruction and magnet programs, and career tech instruction. The changes will take effect beginning in the 2027-28 school year.

The growth strategy continues the work of aligning Tulsa Public Schools’ programs and buildings with its current enrollment reality by responding to family demand and rapidly moving students off of waitlists and into classrooms.

“We believe growing enrollment where, and how, families want it is the best way to serve young people,” said Dr. Johnson. “I am committed to guiding TPS into the future with a plan that answers the call from students and families by offering more options and increased access to in-demand offerings.”

The multi-pronged growth strategy will include the following initiatives that respond to demand while maximizing space by putting in-demand programs where they can grow:

Expanding programs families want most:

  • Expansion of immersion and dual language programming: Zarrow International School (pre-K-5) will move to the larger Hoover building, adding at least 65 seats. Felicitas Mendez International (pre-K-5) will move to the larger present-day Anderson Elementary campus, adding at least 220 dual-language seats. 
  • Career Tech: More than 300 TPS high school students are currently on the waitlist for Tulsa Tech. With Central slated to become home of Oklahoma’s first public polytechnic high school, hundreds of students will gain access to free career certifications as they work toward earning their diplomas.

Responding to what students desire:

  • Virtual learning: TPS online programs for grades 6-12 will grow by 1,100 seats, clearing the waitlist and opening seats for additional enrollment. Students will have a new home at the Central virtual and career learning complex, with in-person support, a cafeteria, a student lounge, health and wellness services, a library, and college and career counseling.
  • Hybrid learning: A new hybrid learning program at Booker T. Washington High School and Edison Preparatory High School (grades 9-10) will serve qualifying students with highly specialized pursuits – such as elite athletes and performing artists – or for those who prefer a rigorous online model. Students will stay connected to their high school community and earn a diploma from their home school. Programs are set to launch with 35 students at each site.

“These steps are just the beginning of redefining what it means to be a school district that serves the unique needs of every learner,” said Dr. Johnson. “We have heard what students and families want, and we are responding.”

Enrollment begins in January for all programs across the district. You can find more information about enrollment options by visiting https://www.tulsaschools.org/enrollment.
