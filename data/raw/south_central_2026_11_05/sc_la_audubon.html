What makes Audubon's French Program Unique?


In an historic agreement with France in 1990, Audubon Charter School was the FIRST SCHOOL IN LOUISIANA to receive an accreditation to teach the French national curriculum. 

Our French program is fully accredited through the Agency for French Education Abroad (AEFE) and is also designated by the Louisiana State Department of Education as a Certified World Languages Site. 



What does attending an accredited French program mean? Rather than a French Immersion program, our school offers an authentic French learning experience using the French national curriculum that is taught by native French speakers. Students receive full credit for their courses in meeting both Louisiana state standards AND French national standards, enabling them to transfer to any French educational institution in the world. 



Not only do students in our French program score on or above the French national average on the French national exam (the DELF), but they also consistently score higher than their peers on Louisiana state standardized tests. Their English test scores are consistently in the top 10% statewide. Students continue to the best secondary schools in the area.

French Language Instruction (So, is it all in French?)

In lower elementary grades, we focus on providing 70-90% of instruction in French to create parallel vocabulary and the foundations of bilingualism. In PreK, Kindergarten, and 1st grades, students begin learning French through repetition of words and sounds. In the 2nd grade, students become more familiar with transitioning between French and English in thinking, reading, writing, and speaking. It is common to hear students start to pepper their English with French expressions or to hear a student speaking “franglais,” a mixture of French and English. Students in 2nd grade begin daily study of English Language Arts but the rest of their academic coursework remains in French.

In 3rd grade, students are expected to be fluent in French and all coursework is taught in French. Students also begin studying Mandarin Chinese with support from the Confucius Institute. We start exposure to Mandarin Chinese in elementary school for two reasons: Math computation is embedded in elementary language use and reinforces our math instructional priority, and it allows our students in the French program to develop a stronger ear for tonal languages.

Starting in 3rd grade, 70% of instruction is in French and 30% is in English. Our students learn to write timelines, outlines, paragraphs and essays in both languages. Math, Social Studies, Science, French Language Art and all other core curricular subjects (except English/Language Arts) are taught in French and students will be tested in these areas in French.
