Boundaries and School Closures

School Closures Approved by Board of Education

The Olathe Public Schools Board of Education has approved closing five elementary schools and one middle school at the end of the 2026-27 school year: 

  • Black Bob Elementary 
    • This recommendation includes moving the Spanish Immersion program from Black Bob Elementary to Arbor Creek Elementary. The program at Manchester Park Elementary would remain in place. 
  • Heritage Elementary 
  • Scarborough Elementary 
  • Tomahawk Elementary  
  • Walnut Grove Elementary 
  • Indian Trail Middle 

We know seeing any name of one of our schools on this list would be difficult because of how much our schools mean to the communities they serve.  

This recommendation came after extensive review of enrollment, finances, facilities and other factors. Our district must respond to declining enrollment due to lowered birth rates and rising home prices, as well as budget pressure stemming from the underfunding of special education. Today, we serve approximately the same number of elementary students as we did in 2001, but operate nine more elementary schools. To protect the programming and opportunities our students and families expect, as well as our teacher and staff positions and compensation, we have reached a point where we must better align the number of schools we operate with the number of students we serve. 

Olathe Public Schools Boundary Map (PDF)

What Happens Next? 

The next phase of the facility alignment process will focus on developing new attendance boundaries for impacted schools and communities. Community boundary meetings will take place this fall. Specific dates, times, and locations will be shared in the coming days with all impacted school communities. Please note that boundary adjustments will affect both schools that are closing and some schools that will remain open as the district adjusts to changes in building capacity and student populations. 

Community input has been a foundational component of the district’s boundary process. At these meetings, staff and families can expect to see multiple potential boundary options, learn more about each proposal and provide feedback. District leaders will review all input and will take all feedback into consideration when making the final boundary recommendation to be presented to the Board of Education. 

Our goal is to have boundaries established by January 2027, giving families, students and staff several months to prepare for the transition, get connected to their new school and build relationships within their new community.

Why is the district addressing enrollment, finances, and school alignment now?

Olathe Public Schools has experienced a decline in enrollment since 2020 when we reached a peak of 30,000 students, and our district expects that trend to continue, with enrollment leveling off just above 25,000. As student enrollment declines, so do some of the resources the district receives from the state. At the same time, our district must continue managing rising costs and other financial demands while supporting students. 

Inadequate funding of special education by the state of Kansas adds significant pressure to our district’s operating budget. When those realities are combined with the fact that our district was built to serve more students than it has today, changes are necessary to better align schools and resources with current enrollment.

Currently, we serve approximately the same number of elementary students as we did in 2001, but we operate nine more elementary buildings. In order to continue to provide the student programs, opportunities and competitive compensation for staff that our community expects, we can no longer sustain the financial cost of running a larger footprint that does not accurately reflect our current enrollment. 

What does district alignment mean?

In this process, that means reducing our district’s school footprint to align the number of schools in our district with the number of students we serve today and into the future. This process includes reviewing student enrollment, school capacity, building use, school age and geographic attendance areas, among other factors.

Why are we discussing school closures after voters approved the $389 million bond?

The bond and potential school closures address two different needs in our district. 

Over the past year, we have openly shared information about declining enrollment with our Board of Education, staff, and community members during public meetings. During bond presentations and meetings with school staff, we explained that while the bond was necessary for facility improvements and long-term planning, we would also need to continue conversations about aligning the number of schools we operate with the number of students we serve. 

The video sent by Dr. Yeager was intended to help families who may not have followed every bond update, watched board presentations, or read school or district newsletters to understand that this work is part of our long-term planning. 

Why is enrollment declining when our community appears to be growing?

Several factors are contributing to this trend: 

  • Housing costs: The median home price in our district is now around $500,000, which makes it more difficult for young families to move into the area.

  • Lower birth rates: Birth rates across our region have declined, again, impacting the amount of young children in our district. 

  • Families having children later: Many people are waiting longer to get married and start families. 

If birth rates had remained at the levels we experienced during earlier growth periods, our district estimates we could have thousands more students enrolled today. 

How does declining enrollment affect our finances?

Public school funding is largely based on student enrollment. When enrollment declines, funding declines as well. 

In our district, a decrease of a little more than 2,000 students represents about $22 million less each year in our operating fund. 

At the same time, we are facing growing financial pressure related to special education funding, which requires significant support from our general education budget. Special education funding is currently not funded at the legally-required 92% by the legislature, which in turn forces the district to move millions of dollars from the general fund to cover the costs of these needed services.  

What steps has our district already taken to manage the budget?

We have already implemented several cost-saving measures, including: 

  • Reducing building and department budgets by 25% during the current school year 

  • Closely reviewing positions before filling them for next year to ensure we do not overstaff as we evaluate potential school consolidations 

  • Consolidating 30+ positions at district support buildings 

  • A continued 25% budget reductions in building and departments moving forward 

  • A continued 30% budget reduction in district-level departments. This approach is intended to limit the impact on classrooms and protect the day-to-day experience for students as much as possible. 

We are making every effort to manage these financial challenges while protecting the classroom experience and minimizing the impact on students. 

Why are so many of the consolidation sites located within the southern portion of the district?

The neighborhood school model that Olathe implemented during peak enrollment growth peaked in the southern region. This has resulted in a high school saturation within the southern portion of the district, where a large majority of the district’s elementary schools are located. 
 
As enrollment evened out and additional buildings were constructed, the district moved away from the neighborhood school model in areas like the northwest corner of the district, resulting in less school saturation and less need to align building number with enrollment in those areas. 

Are homeschooling and private school enrollment contributing to the district’s enrollment decline?

No. For several years, we have studied the factors contributing to changes in enrollment. We have heard assumptions from community members that some families are choosing to homeschool or attend private schools. However, according to the most recent small-area census data, 89.34% of school-aged students living within our district boundaries are enrolled in Olathe Public Schools. In 2010, 86.96% of school-aged students living within our district boundaries were enrolled in Olathe Public Schools. This percentage has stayed consistent. 
 
Both internal and external research show that there are simply fewer children living within our district boundaries, which is the most significant factor contributing to the decline in enrollment. This trend aligns with the national decline in birth rates, while rising local home prices also make it more difficult for families with small children to move into our community. Together, these factors are changing the number of school-aged children in our district. 

What are the cost savings of closing all five proposed elementary schools and one middle school?

Closing the five proposed elementary schools and one middle school is conservatively estimated to save the district more than $12 million annually. These savings would come from day-to-day building operations and utilities, technology equipment and replacement cycles, ongoing maintenance and grounds services, repairs to building infrastructure and systems, as well as staffing efficiencies achieved through attrition and retirements.  

These savings would provide the opportunity to have long-term fiscal sustainability in our school district, reinvest in our staff, and protect the programs and opportunities our students and families value. Proceeds from the potential sale of closed buildings would also help replenish district reserves. Together, these steps are intended to strengthen the district’s long-term financial sustainability while keeping our focus on providing the best possible experience for students. 

Cost Savings Breakdown: 

  • Operating a building efficiently and safely = $960,000
  • Ensuring technology works and is equitable = $460,000
  • Maintaining our buildings and grounds = $80,000
  • Repairing the buildings' infrastructure and systems = $2,500,000
  • Staff compensation and benefits through attration and retirements = $8,000,000
  • Total Savings = $12,000,000

Why close all proposed buildings next year rather than a phased approach?

The decision to implement the proposed closures at the end of the 2026-27 school year considers several factors, including the district’s budget outlook, staffing needs and the long-term sustainability of our schools. This timeline allows the district to begin realizing the financial savings associated with consolidation in the 2027-28 school year, while also giving our staff greater clarity and allowing us to prioritize critical classified staffing shortage areas. It also allows us to thoughtfully plan staffing and resources at schools that will welcome additional students, rather than attempt to staff buildings that are expected to close. 
 
Just as importantly, this timeline allows us to focus on creating a positive and supportive transition for all students. Establishing new attendance boundaries at one time, rather than phasing in changes over multiple years, gives families greater clarity and allows the district to plan staffing, transportation, programs and student supports together. 
 
While we know transitions can bring uncertainty, our goal is to use this time to prepare welcoming school communities where every student can feel connected, supported and ready for a successful start to the 2027-28 school year. 

Why are my taxes going up if we are closing buildings?

The district’s decision to close/consolidate schools and the change in the tax rate address two related but different financial needs.  
 
Olathe Public Schools receives funding based largely on the number of students we serve. As enrollment has declined, our funding has also decreased. However, we are still operating a school system built to serve a much larger number of students. In addition, the state continues to underfund special education.  
 
Last year, the district used its remaining reserves to help cover the gap between our revenues and the cost of operating our current system. 
 
Closing/consolidating schools will help reduce those ongoing costs, but those savings will not begin until the schools close at the end of the 2026-27 school year. In the meantime, operating costs continue to increase. The district also needed to increase its special liability levy due to costs associated with active lawsuits. 
 
For these reasons, the district was unable to lower the tax rate to the revenue-neutral rate this year. The approved increase is minimal at 0.147 mills and is the district’s first mill levy increase since 2017-18.  
 
Ultimately, school consolidation is part of the district’s longer-term effort to better align our expenses with declining enrollment and use taxpayer dollars responsibly. 

Why were the six proposed buildings recommended for closure?

Schools were evaluated using several factors, including enrollment, building capacity, age and condition, future maintenance costs, transportation, location, potential boundary adjustments and school programming, among others. No single factor determined whether a school was recommended for closure. During the Sept. 3 Board of Education presentation, the following rationale was provided as the primary reasons for each building's recommendation, although other factors may have also contributed to the recommendation: 

  • Indian Trail Middle
    • Declining enrollment in southeast Olathe
    • Smallest middle school this year
    • Building condition/needed improvements
    • Multiple adjoining schools with space for additional students
    • 100% of the Facility Alignment Steering Committee recommended Indian Trail as their top choice for a middle school closure
  • Black Bob Elementary
    • Declining enrollment in southeast Olathe
    • Building age/condition/needed improvements
    • Proximity of other schools under their functional capacity
    • Top 5 closure recommendation of the Facility Alignment Steering Committee
  • Heritage Elementary
    • Building age/condition/needed improvements
    • ADA compliance issues/floor plan
    • Site challenges sitting on a high school campus
    • Top 5 closure recommendation of the Facility Alignment Steering Committee
  • Scarborough Elementary
    • Building age/condition/needed improvements
    • Smaller number of classrooms
    • Proximity of other schools under their functional capacity
    • Top 5 closure recommendation of the Facility Alignment Steering Committee
  • Tomahawk Elementary
    • ​​​​​​​Building age/condition/needed improvements
    • Proximity to other schools under their functional capacity (Briarwood)
    • Future projected enrollment decline
    • Top 5 closure recommendation of the Facility Alignment Steering Committee
  • Walnut Grove
    • ​​​​​​​Lack of students within 1/2 mile of school (very few student walkers)
    • Building age/condition/needed improvements
    • Proximity of other schools in the northeast under their functional capacity
    • Top 5 closure recommendation of the Facility Alignment Steering Committee

Do all six recommended building closures align with the steering committees recommendations?

Yes. All six proposed building closures reflect the recommendation of the Facility Alignment Steering Committee's final feedback. Steering committee members were polled at the conclusion of each meeting to provide input on their building recommendation closures at that time to help narrow the scope of elementary schools under consideration. Understandably, these recommendations fluctuated meeting-to-meeting based on additional study of data and conversation among the group. 

The six proposed building closures reflect the final recommendations of the Facility Alignment Steering Committee following the Aug. 4, 2026 meeting. At the conclusion of the final steering committee meeting, members were asked to complete a survey ranking their top six buildings recommended for closure. Results from the survey are available in the Facility Alignment Steering Committee Presentations and Resources section of this page.. Their responses were scored using the following rubric: Rank 1 (top choice) = 6 points; Rank 2 = 5 points; Rank 3 = 4 points; Rank 4 = 3 points; Rank 5 = 2 points; Rank 6 (last choice) = 1 point.

While the steering committee's final recommendation was for the six schools identified, it was ultimately the responsibility of district administration to make the final recommendation on building closures.

Below are the results of the overall survey scores:

  • Walnut Grove: 120 points
  • Black Bob: 75 points
  • Scarborough: 65 points
  • Heritage: 56 points
  • Tomahawk: 52 points
  • Havencroft: 28 points
  • Countryside: 14 points
  • Bentwood: 5 points
  • Brougham: 4 points
  • Green Springs: 1 point
  • Briarwood: 0 points

How will closing buildings save money and where does the district currently spend its operating dollars?

Our people are by far our biggest investment. The majority of our operating expenditures go directly toward instruction and teacher salaries, with additional dollars supporting other employees who serve students every day. 

As enrollment declines, we have made it a priority to protect our programs, services and staff our students rely on as much as possible. That means looking closely at other areas where we can reduce ongoing costs. As a reminder, we are committed to ensuring all impacted employees are placed in positions within the district if the closures are approved. 

Our next-largest operating expenditure is operations and maintenance. Every school we operate carries ongoing costs, from utilities and technology to maintenance and repairs. 

Today, we're operating a school system built for more students than we serve. Did you know that we currently have nearly the same amount of K-5 elementary students as we did in 2001, but we operate nine more elementary school buildings? Reducing our building footprint would better align our facilities with current and projected enrollment while reducing those ongoing expenses.

What is the Enrollment and Facility Alignment Task Force, and what did it do?

The task force was the first step in the community-led alignment process. This 125-member group included a parent and a staff member from every school, along with district and community representatives. 

The primary focus of the task force was to review district enrollment data, school capacity, building use, school age and geographic attendance areas, etc. to make a recommendation on the appropriate number of schools our district needs based on student enrollment. The task force met for three, two-hour sessions in the Spring of 2026. View all task force presentations in the "Presentation and Resource" section of this web page.

What happened after the task force?

After the task force completed its initial work, a smaller steering committee was formed. The steering committee was made up of parents and staff from the original task force. This group focused on identifying the specific buildings to be considered for closure, with the goal of bringing a recommendation to the Board of Education at the September meeting. 

The task force helped inform how many schools may need to be reduced, and the steering committee will helped identify which specific buildings should be considered for closure.

What is the Facility Alignment Steering Committee, and what did it do?

The steering committee was the second step in the alignment process and convened over the summer after the task force completed its work. This smaller group included both parent, staff member and community representation from the original task force and had representation from across the district.

The primary focus of the steering committee was to review district enrollment data, school capacity, building use, school age and geographic attendance areas, etc. to make a recommendation on the specific elementary and middle schools the district should consider for closure. The steering committee met for four, two-hour sessions from June-August, with the goal of making a recommendation to the Board of Education in September. The September Board of Education meeting will take place Sept. 3, 2026 at 5:45 p.m.

What is the timeline for this process?

The task force was formed in Spring of 2026 and met three times from April to May. At the June Board of Education meeting, Deputy Superintendent Dr. Jim McMullen presented the task force's feedback on the number of schools the district should consider reducing.

Beginning in late June, the district formed a smaller steering committee that convened four times from June-August and was tasked with making a recommendation on specific buildings for closure. The steering committee recommendation to the Board of Education will occur at the Sept. 3, Board of Education meeting, which begins at 5:45 p.m. 

Following board action of recommended school closures, the district will proceed with any related boundary decisions, visiting with all impacted communities to provide additional information on future boundary adjustments. It is our goal to have new attendance boundaries approved by January 2027 so that families have ample time to prepare for the 2027-28 school year at their new school. 

How were the proposed buildings selected?

Final recommendations were based on parent, staff and community member input from the Facility Alignment Task Force and Facility Alignment Steering Committee. The Task Force, made up of a parent and staff member from each school, met three times in the spring and evaluated district enrollment trends, history and projections, as well as school building capacity and room utilization to make recommendations on the number of buildings the district should consider reducing. 

The Steering Committee, a smaller group made up of members of the Task Force, dove deeper into data, assessing building age and condition, projected facility maintenance expenditures, estimated property value, percentage of students who utilize bus transportation by building, school saturation by geographic location, programming within the buildings and variety of other factors. 

What is the timeline for school closure?

Schools will remain open for the remainder of the 2026-27 school year. Beginning in the 2027-28 school year, the proposed buildings would no longer be open. It is our goal to have new attendance boundaries approved by January 2026 so that families have ample time to prepare for the 2027-28 school year at their new school. 

Where will my student go to school?

Pending Board approval, district administration will begin working on updated boundaries in October 2026, including community information sessions, to identify new boundaries and schools for the closed buildings, as well as any additional boundary adjustments for buildings that will remain open. At the board meeting, it was recommended that the Spanish Immersion program be relocated from Black Bob Elementary to Arbor Creek Elementary. The Spanish Immersion program at Manchester Park Elementary will remain in place. 

When will new boundaries be determined?

Pending Board approval, district administration will begin working on updated boundaries in October, including community information sessions, to identify new boundaries and schools for the closed buildings, as well as any additional boundary adjustments for buildings that will remain open. District administration anticipates proposing new boundary adjustments for Board consideration at the December 2026 Board of Education meeting. 

What will happen to the buildings that close?

Buildings that are no longer used for their original purpose—for example, a school impacted by consolidation—could be repurposed, sold, or demolished, with the land potentially sold. State law requires that if a school building is sold, the state legislature be given the first right of refusal.  

For the 2027-28 school year, two of the elementary schools will serve as temporary sites to house students from Central Elementary and Northview Elementary as new buildings are constructed on their current sites as part of Bond 2026.

April 24, 2026 

As the Olathe community evolves and there are fewer young children in our boundaries, the school district is working to thoughtfully adapt while keeping students at the center of every decision.  

Olathe Public Schools, like many districts statewide and across the country, is experiencing declining enrollment alongside continued financial strain, including underfunding of special education at the state level. In response, the district is beginning a process to better match the number of school buildings with current and future student enrollment.  

The Facility Alignment Task Force is leading the initial phase of this effort, with each school represented by one staff member and one parent. The group met for the first time last week to review enrollment trends and gain a clearer understanding of school funding. Two additional meetings are scheduled this spring, after which the task force will recommend how many schools the district will need to remain sustainable in the years ahead. The group will not determine which specific schools may be affected, but its work will help shape those future decisions.

March 27, 2026

Our community is changing, and our district must respond with care, honesty and a continued focus on students.

Like districts across the state and nation, our district is facing declining enrollment, a trend expected to continue, along with ongoing budget pressures, including insufficient state funding for special education. As shared in Dr. Yeager’s recent video message, these challenges require action. The district must align the number of schools with current and projected enrollment through a consolidation process.

A task force, with one staff member and one parent from each school, will lead the first phase. The group will review facility use and enrollment data to determine the number of schools needed for long-term sustainability. While it will not identify specific buildings, its work will guide future decisions. This is the first step in a three-phase study, and community input will be critical throughout.

District leaders are committed to transparent, clear and consistent communication throughout this process. Learn more and find answers to frequently asked questions on the district website.

May 20, 2026

Last week, the Facility Alignment Task Force held its final meeting. As a reminder, the task force included one staff member and one parent representative from each school, ensuring voices from across the district were part of the conversation and planning process of school consolidation. The task force's primary focus was to learn about district enrollment and forecasting data, and use the knowledge they acquired to help inform a recommendation on the number of buildings the district needs moving forward to best align with student enrollment.

In their final meeting, the task force broke out into groups to review data and make a recommendation on the number of elementary and middle schools that best fit enrollment projections moving forward. Next, district leaders will present the task force's recommendations to the Board of Education at the June board meeting. Following that presentation, a smaller steering committee made up of task force members will convene this summer to take a deeper look at the recommendations and begin the work of determining which schools will ultimately be consolidated.

One of the participants shared, "Thanks for the opportunity to learn more. While these discussions are hard to have I am reassured that it will be handled as best as it can be."

We want to sincerely thank all of the task force members for their time and thoughtful feedback throughout this process. We recognize this has not been an easy topic to discuss, but we deeply appreciate the willingness of members to have honest conversations about the need for the district to align for the future. The feedback and perspectives shared by the group have been invaluable and will play an important role in guiding future decision-making.

There will be more information to come in the fall. If you'd like to stay engaged throughout this process, please visit our School Alignment webpage on the district website.

June 25, 2026

This spring, the Facility Alignment Task Force, made up of one staff member and one parent representative from each school, met three times review district finances, enrollment projections and building capacity data to help inform Olathe Public Schools’ school consolidation planning process.

At the June Board of Education Meeting, Deputy Superintendent Dr. Jim McMullen presented an overview of the Task Force’s work and recommendations. View the full presentation on the district’s YouTube Channel.

Survey results showed that 100 percent of participants believed the district needed to reduce its elementary footprint, with the largest groups recommending the closure of three or four elementary schools and at least one middle school. High schools are not expected to be affected by the alignment process based on enrollment projections.

Next Steps

A steering committee made up of task force members will meet four times this summer to further evaluate the recommendations and begin identifying schools that may be considered for consolidation. The steering committee met for the first time this week.

A recommendation on specific school closures will be made to the Board of Education at the September board meeting, with decisions around updated boundaries to follow. Please note, it is possible that your school could be impacted even if it is not identified for consolidation or closure. Buildings that do not close may have their boundaries adjusted to accommodate student populations from buildings that have been reduced.

Additional information will be shared this fall. Community members interested in following the process can learn more on the district website.

August 28, 2026

As shared with families earlier this week in a direct email, Olathe Public Schools has reached a point where we must reduce the number of schools in our district to better align with our enrollment and continue to be good stewards of taxpayer dollars. Aligning facilities to our enrollment provides significant operational cost savings for the district, allowing us to reinvest dollars into student programs and opportunities, as well as competitive staff compensation to continue to provide the world-class educational experience and strong work environment that our community expects.

District leaders will share the recommendation for specific school closures during a presentation at the public Sept. 3 Board of Education meeting. You can watch a livestream of the meeting on our district’s YouTube channel. Please know that additional in-person meetings and opportunities to connect will be available in the month of September and beyond.

Why are we doing this?

  • Declining Enrollment: Olathe Public Schools, like most districts across the state and nation, are facing a continued decline in enrollment due to declining birth rates and increased home prices.

  • Facility Alignment: We are operating a system designed to serve more students than we have. Currently, we serve approximately the same number of elementary students as we did in 2011, but we operate nine more elementary schools.

  • Cost Savings to Reinvest in Students & Staff: Closing buildings provides significant operational cost savings for the district, allowing us to reinvest dollars into student programs and opportunities and staff compensation to continue to provide the world-class educational experience and strong work environment that our community expects.

For more information on the task force or steering committees, previous presentations, communication sent and answers to Frequently Asked Questions, please visit our school alignment website.

September 25, 2026

Olathe Community,

Throughout the month, our community has engaged in conversations about school alignment and the recommendations being considered by the Board of Education. We know these conversations have brought a range of emotions and perspectives, and we are grateful to everyone who has taken the time to attend the meeting, ask questions, share feedback and engage in this process. We have been adding more information and answers to your frequently asked questions all month long. Learn more on our School Alignment webpage on our district website.
 

Oct. 1 will mark an important moment in this process as the Board of Education considers the recommendations for school closures. If the closures are approved, we would immediately schedule boundary meetings so that our families can give us their feedback on different scenarios. Community input has been a longstanding foundation of our boundary process and we will continue that tradition pending any school closures. Community feedback has played an integral role in shaping previous boundaries, at times helping to generate new and different boundary options from those originally proposed. Regardless, we recognize the gravity of these decisions, and we are committed to being there for our students, staff and families every step of the way.

Although that conversation has been heavy on our minds this month, our district has also had plenty to celebrate. We had 19 seniors named National Merit Semifinalists, which is such an accomplishment for them, their loved ones and their teachers. Congratulations to you all! We also honored the 25th anniversary of September 11 and our third grade mock trial went international on Constitution Day! There are so many wonderful things happening in every corner of our district, as always. This newsletter contains all of that – and more.

Thank you for your partnership with your child's education.

Your very proud superintendent,

Dr. Brent Yeager
