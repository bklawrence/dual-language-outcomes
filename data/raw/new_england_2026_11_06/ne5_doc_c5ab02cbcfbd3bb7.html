New dual language school on the horizon for Pawtucket students

  • By LUZJENNIFER MARTINEZ Valley Breeze Deputy Editor luzjennifer@valleybreeze.com

    LuzJennifer Martinez

    Deputy Editor

PAWTUCKET – A proposal from the Rhode Island Education Collective for a new dual-language charter school to serve students from Providence, Pawtucket, and Cranston is under charter review with the Rhode Island Department of Education.

Founder and CEO Victor Capellan said that the school, which will be named De La Comunidad Bilingual (Of The Bilingual Community), will be a K-12 school with a full service community model, which he said he hopes will open by September 2027 if approved.

“It will be able to provide wraparound services by creating a network of providers that support the school,” Capellan told The Breeze. “But the big idea is around increasing dual-language, or English and Spanish in this case.”

As the former superintendent of schools in Central Falls, Capellan, said he and his colleagues looked at how dual-language school schools were doing in Washington, D.C., Boston, and New York.

He added that De La Comunidad would be a good model to add additional seats in Rhode Island.

“A few of the dual-language schools that we have right now in the state which are doing fairly well, (include) the International Charter School, Nuestro Mundo, and Leviton in Providence,” Capellan said.

He added that the proposal was submitted by the Collective in July in response to a request for proposals put out by RIDE during the spring.

The public comment period ended Monday, and according to Danielle Barron, senior director of leadership and development at the Collective, many families from Providence and Pawtucket showed up in support of the school.

Capellan helped launched Raices Dual Language Academy when he was superintendent, and he said that the interest from Central Falls families who wanted their children in the school was strong.

That interest is also evident in the number of families on the waiting list to get into the International Charter School, he said.

Capellan said RIDE will next take the time to review the application, and make the decision whether to approve it sometime in November or December.

“This proposal will aim to add a total of 628 seats,” he said. “It’s two tracks, an English and Spanish track, where the students will be doing a 50/50 model. Part of what we looked at was how do we create a geographically tight area where students can get to and from the school, and we’re hoping that the school is placed in Providence.”

Capellan said bringing students from Cranston into the school would support the English dominant side, while students from Providence and Pawtucket would bring more traction in both English and Spanish.

“Cranston has doubled in terms of their multilingual learners in the last few years,” he said. “Providence and Pawtucket continue to be places where families are looking for additional seats in charter schools, so it’s going to fill that need as well.”

The Collective started with the goal of ensuring that every child in R.I. has access to high quality education, according to Capellan, and began their cause by listening to what families were saying, surveying over 100 moms during Sunday night dinners.

“Through that, we developed the agenda of the work we are now doing and when we looked at what these moms shared, we thought, ‘how do we bring families, communities, and educators together to bring solutions, to challenge the status quo that is not working,’” he said.

During the last year-and-a-half since it was formed, the Collective has become focused on working with district public schools and charter public schools to increase the number of dual language programs, Capellan said.

Their work includes a Central Falls district-sponsored parent training program and instructional leadership in order to find innovative solutions and ways to do the work and be effective.

“We’re a small non-profit, but we’ve been able to do that work with successful funding through a few organizations for summer programs in Woonsocket, Central Falls, Johnston, Providence, and Newport,” he said.

“I think that this is a model that a lot of different schools should take a look at and do a little bit more research on, because we have seen that it not only serves multilingual learners, but it also serves English-dominant students who want to learn another language.”

Capellan confirmed that the school will allow non-Latino students to enroll as well, to provide dual-language learning opportunities to English-dominant students.

He noted how the school will fill a “very real need” for more institutions that affirm people’s cultures, languages, and identity, particularly during this time of unrest for the immigrant and Latino community.

“This school is looking for ways to provide wrap-around services to become a full-service school community model, for the community to work with schools to support students and families,” Capellan said.

“It’s not just for dual-language, but to provide full support to families that need support.”
