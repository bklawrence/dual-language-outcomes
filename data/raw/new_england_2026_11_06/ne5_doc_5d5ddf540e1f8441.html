Massachusetts Teacher Of The Year 2027: Mansur Buffins Named Semifinalist

Orchard Gardens K-8 School Instructional Coach Mansur Buffins has received one of the Commonwealth's highest honors for educators.

Massachusetts Teacher Of The Year 2027: Mansur Buffins Named Semifinalist

Mansur Buffins believes education should do more than prepare students for the next grade or the next test. Education also should empower young people to make a positive impact in their communities. That commitment has earned him recognition as a 2027 Massachusetts Teacher of the Year Semifinalist.

An Instructional Coach at Orchard Gardens K–8 School, Buffins is among seven educators selected as semifinalists by the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE), following a competitive process that began with 347 nominations from across the Commonwealth.

“I work in education in the tradition of excellent educators such as Dr. Carter G. Woodson and Dr. Mary McLeod Bethune, cultivating inspiring, empowering, and intellectually rigorous learning experiences that affirm young people and equip them with knowledge and skills to realize their aspirations and contribute meaningfully to their families and communities,” Buffins said.  

His path in Boston Public Schools began in 2022, when he joined the district as a substitute teacher while pursuing his master's degree in Education Leadership, Organizations and Entrepreneurship from Harvard Graduate School of Education. He went on to teach Humanities courses at Community Academy, where he spent several years working alongside students before transitioning into instructional coaching. He joined the Orchard Gardens K-8 community in September 2026.

Across these roles, Buffins has remained focused on the conditions that help students thrive: affirming relationships; high expectations; meaningful intellectual challenges; and the targeted support students need to succeed. His approach reflects the qualities celebrated by the Massachusetts Teacher of the Year program, which recognizes educators who advance student learning, embrace student-centered instruction, and demonstrate leadership beyond their classrooms.

Buffins’ semifinalist honor is a significant distinction and a moment of pride for Orchard Gardens and Boston Public Schools. Congratulations to Mansur Buffins on this well-deserved recognition and on the lasting impact of his work with students and educators.

BPS Student Poll Workers Step Up on Election Day, As Program Marks 10 Years

Students are participating in the Boston Election Department’s Student Poll Workers Program enjoy a hands-on, behind-the-scenes look at the electoral process and also help with all aspects of Election Day.

BPS Student Poll Workers Step Up on Election Day, As Program Marks 10 Years

While voters across Boston head to the polls to vote November 3, 2026, Boston Public Schools (BPS) students will play an important role in making Election Day happen in the City. As student poll workers, they help voters navigate the process, support polling place operations, and gain a firsthand understanding of how democracy works at the local level.

The day also marks a milestone for the City of Boston Department of Elections and BPS: the Student Poll Worker Program is celebrating its 10th year of operation, having launched in 2017. Over the years, the partnership has created a meaningful way for young people to contribute to their communities before they’re old enough to vote.

Open to students who will be at least 16 years old by Election Day, the program offers paid work and a chance to take on real responsibilities. Depending on their assignments, students might serve as greeters, interpreters, or inspectors, helping ensure voters have a welcoming and accessible experience at polling locations throughout the city.
 
The students’ experience goes beyond a single day on the job. Working alongside election staff and community members offers a closer look at the care, coordination, and teamwork required to run an election. Students can build communication and problem-solving skills while seeing how civic participation takes shape in their own neighborhoods.

Their contributions matter. A smooth election depends on the people who help voters find their way, answer questions, and keep polling places running. By stepping into these roles, BPS students are helping their neighbors exercise a fundamental right while learning what it means to serve the public.

As the program celebrates a decade of connecting students with civic life, the November 3 election is another opportunity for Boston’s young people to make a difference.

BPS Recognized for September Terrific Tray

DESE recognizes BPS for the September Terrific Tray, a statewide competition celebrating school meals featuring locally sourced Massachusetts ingredients and highlighting reimbursable, high-quality student-served meals.

Terrific Tray is a statewide competition run by Massachusetts Farm to School and the Department of Elementary and Secondary Education's Office for Food and Nutrition Programs, celebrating school meals that feature locally sourced Massachusetts ingredients. It highlights reimbursable, student-served meals made with foods from local farms, dairies, and fisheries across the Commonwealth.

We're thrilled to share that BPS was recognized this year! From Boston's local waters to the farms of Western Mass, our winning tray showcases some of the best local foods the Commonwealth has to offer.

This past June, BPS served a hyper-local menu featuring a New England Fish Sandwich from Red's Best, house-made tartar sauce, kale & carrot salad, local black beans from Joe Czajkowski Farm in Hadley, fresh strawberries from Nourse Farm in Whately, and Hood milk. This recognition celebrates our Harvest of the Month and how BPS Eats continues to prioritize high-quality, local proteins and produce for our students.

BPS serves more than 8 million meals per year, including breakfast, lunch, after-school meals, and summer meals. The district continues to reimagine school meals by providing high-quality, freshly prepared, nutritious food that supports students' focus in the classroom while also reflecting the diverse cultures and communities across our schools.

State of the Schools 2026: Meet Deaf Student Journalist Carlos Rodriguez

The Horace Mann School for the Deaf & Hard of Hearing Grade 12 scribe will serve as a student journalist at Mayor Michelle Wu’s 2026 State of Schools address.

State of Schools 2026: Meet Deaf Student Journalist Carlos Rodriguez

Jean Carlos Rodriguez will have the scoop on the latest news that will impact the next generation of Boston students.

The Horace Mann School for the Deaf & Hard of Hearing Grade 12 student will serve as a student journalist Monday, October 19, at the second annual State of the Schools Address. Rodriguez will be part of the press scrum that will take place following Mayor Wu’s speech.

Rodriguez is one of the founding members of the Horace Mann school newspaper, The Cougar Times. He covers stories on a variety of topics – ranging from news to human-interest – that impact members of the Horace Mann community. In addition to his editorial roles, he mentors students who are in their first year on the newspaper staff.

“I love journalism because it's wonderful to hear other people's stories and share them,” said Rodriguez. “Also, it's a great way to call out issues/problems or to bring exposure to something.”

The 2026 State of the Schools event will highlight the City of Boston’s ongoing work to make Boston Public Schools (BPS) the first choice for families. Mayor Wu and BPS Superintendent Mary Skipper will share updates on progress across the district, and outline academic and partnership priorities for the year ahead.

Mayor Wu will deliver the 2026 State of the Schools Address on Monday, October 19 at UMass Boston. The event will begin at 6:30 p.m., and the program will be streamed live on Boston.gov. 

“As a BPS mom, I’m determined to continue investing in our schools and school communities so we make Boston Public Schools the first choice for all our families. As we continue to build on our progress in student achievement, graduation rates, and advanced coursework, we are focused on expanding opportunities and investing in our young people, educators, and families,” said Mayor Michelle Wu. “At our second annual State of the Schools address, we will report out on our work, highlight our school communities, and bring partners together to make Boston the best city for families.”

“The progress we continue to make as a district reflects our shared commitment to delivering high-quality learning experiences that strengthen outcomes for students across Boston,” said Boston Public Schools Superintendent Mary Skipper. “We are excited to once again host the State of the Schools, bringing our community together to recognize the dedication of our educators, the engagement of our families, and the many partners who contribute to our students’ success. Alongside the City of Boston and Mayor Wu, we will continue this work to ensure every student has access to the rigorous, culturally affirming education they deserve.”

Instructional Programs

Dual Language / Two Way Bilingual Program

In this program model, about half of students in the classroom are speakers of the program’s partner language (e.g. Spanish) and the other half are English speakers, taught by qualified teacher(s). Instruction is provided in both languages with the goal that students will become bilingual or biliterate. The program entry grade levels are K1 to 2 and 9 to 11. Students between grade levels 3 and 8 must be assessed by the school in the partner language to enter the program. ML students also receive English Language Development (ELD) instruction, which is designed to teach English to ML students.

American Sign Language Program

Horace Mann School for the Deaf and Hard of Hearing

The Horace Mann School (HMS) is a K0-12 American Sign Language (ASL) and English dual language program providing an asset-driven education for deaf and hard of hearing children. Horace Mann School provides a Parent Infant Program (PIP) for families of deaf and hard of hearing children from birth to age 3, ensuring that these students enter school with the early language foundation necessary for academic success. K0 through 12th grade students attending Horace Mann School engage with a rigorous standards-based curriculum that aligns with the current district and state frameworks preparing them for postsecondary success. Its language-rich environment allows students to learn through immersion in a community of strong language models and develop fluency in both American Sign Language and English. Horace Mann School empowers students as Deaf individuals to be lifelong learners and innovative thinkers with diverse linguistic, ethnic, and cultural identities.

Learn more about the Horace Mann School for the Deaf and Hard of Hearing

Haitian Creole Program

Mattahunt Elementary School

In September 2017, Boston Public Schools launched the Toussaint L’Ouverture Academy at the Mattahunt Elementary School, the first Two-Way Immersion Haitian Creole Dual-Language Preschool Program in the country. The Toussaint L’Ouverture Academy is a Haitian Creole Dual Language program that serves students in grades K1-6. We provide an opportunity for students to achieve high levels of bilingual and biliterate proficiency and sociocultural competency through rigorous Haitian Creole and English dual-language instruction. The team at the Toussaint L'Ouverture Academy believes in the potential of every student and fosters a love of learning and a sense of responsibility in students to position them to realize their immense potential. We do this by providing rigorous and meaningful learning experiences - experiences that are grounded in students’ interests, contexts, and cultures and in the environment and community that surrounds and girds the school.

Learn more about the Mattahunt Elementary School.
 

Cabo Verdean Creole Program

Lila G. Frederick

The Lila G. Frederick is the first and only Cabo Verdean Creole/ English program beginning in K1 starting September 2026. The Lila G. Frederick will be the new home to the merged John Winthrop Elementary School and Roger Clap Elementary Schools. Situated in Upham's Corner, the Lila G. Frederick seeks to promote and upload the pillars of bilingual education— bilingualism & biliteracy, sociocultural competency, and high academic achievement. Ensuring community partnership and participation, the school will also serve as a Community Hub School.

Vietnamese Programs

Âu Cơ Preschool

Âu Cơ Preschool was the first and only Vietnamese bilingual, bicultural preschool program in Massachusetts. Starting in September of 2019, Âu Cơ PreSchool joined in partnership with Boston Public Schools to become a Universal Pre-K program. Âu Cơ provides Vietnamese dual-language curriculum to the students, teaching the same curriculum as students would learn in Boston Public Schools, in both English and Vietnamese. Students who graduate from our Universal Pre-K program will have priority registration for K2 in selected BPS schools.

Learn more about the Vietnamese Program at Âu Cơ Preschool.

Mather Elementary School

Mather Elementary became the home of the first and only Vietnamese Dual Language Program in Massachusetts in 2021. The program follows a research-based dual language model that supports students to learn academic content in both Vietnamese and English. It fosters the development of bilingualism and biliteracy while also affirming students’ cultural identities, building confidence, and strengthening the connection between families and schools.

With high-quality curriculum and instruction, a team of well-trained and dedicated bilingual teachers, and strong family/community engagement, the Vietnamese Dual Language Program is a vibrant multilingual multicultural learning space where students grow academically, stay connected to their heritage, and gain bilingual skills to thrive in a global economy.

The program is adding one grade per year and currently in SY25-26 serves grades K1-5.

Learn more about the Mather Elementary School.
 

Mandarin Program

Josiah Quincy Elementary School

The Josiah Quincy Elementary School launched a bilingual program that enrolls Chinese language Heritage speakers (Mandarin, Cantonese, Toishanese) and English dominant speaking students. Learning is in English for most subjects including English as a Second Language. Mathematics is taught in Chinese (Mandarin), and students take Chinese (Mandarin) language classes. The Bilingual Education Program starts in K1 and continues through 5th grade. The school is projected to cover grades K1-5th grade for the 2031-2032 school year.

Transitional Bilingual Education

Transitional Bilingual Education or “TBE” is a program where multilingual learners learn content primarily in the partner language and then transition to learning more of the content in English. English as a Second Language or “ESL” is taught by a licensed teacher in the classroom or in a pull-out model and is adjusted to the level of the student. When students complete a foundational level of proficiency in English, they transition to an inclusive program where they are integrated with English speaking peers. The goal of a TBE program is English acquisition. This program is for students in all grades.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What languages are available in BPS dual language programs?
  • Are dual language programs the same as two-way immersion programs?
  • Are there any recommendations to enroll my child in a dual language program?

What languages are available in BPS dual language programs?

Currently, BPS offers dual language programs in Spanish/English in selected elementary schools and in one high school. We also offer Haitian Creole/English programming at the Mattahunt Elementary School. In addition, we also have an American Sign Language (ASL)/English dual language program at the Horace Mann School for the Deaf. BPS subsequently added dual language programs in Vietnamese/English at Âu Cơ Preschool and Mather Elementary School. More recently, BPS will launch a Cabo Verdean Creole/English program the Lila G. Frederick is expected to launch in Fall 2026.

Are dual language programs the same as two-way immersion programs?

Yes, dual language programs as defined in BPS are also known as two-way immersion programs.

Are there any recommendations to enroll my child in a dual language program?

It is recommended that students enter the dual language programs at K0-K2. The child must be proficient in either English or the target language of the program (for the Haitian Creole, Spanish, and Vietnamese dual language programs). In some cases, a new student who has proficiency in the target language (Haitian Creole, Spanish, or Vietnamese) may be enrolled into a DLP beyond grade 1.

Enrollment in the ASL Dual Language program at the Horace Mann School for the Deaf and Hard of Hearing is available for students who are deaf or hard of hearing and does not require proficiency in ASL or English.

Consult your local dual language program school for details and enrollment procedures.

SEI - Sheltered English Immersion

Sheltered English Immersion or “SEI”  is an instructional program for English learners that has two components - sheltered content instruction and ESL instruction. Nearly all of the classroom instruction is in English, with appropriate linguistic supports for students who are learning the language. Teachers use specific techniques to make content learning in Math, Science, and Social Studies accessible to multilingual learners at all levels. English as a Second Language or “ESL” is taught by a licensed teacher in the classroom or in a pull-out model and is adjusted to the level of the student. In Boston, this type of SEI program is delivered in an inclusive setting where multilingual learners are integrated with English speaking peers throughout the school day. This program is for students in all grades.

Newcomer Program

In BPS, Newcomers are multilingual learners who have been born outside of the United States who have been in the United States for 12 months or less and have a WIDA Screener level of 1.0-2.0. Families of newcomer students who enter Boston Public Schools may choose a specially designed program known as a newcomer program. Newcomer programs are an initial and temporary placement with students moving into SEI classrooms within 1-2 years of entering the program.

Program Information

Purpose of Newcomer Programming 

The Newcomer Program is designed to support newly arrived multilingual learners and their families by providing a structured, supportive environment that integrates grade-level content instruction with targeted English language development and social-emotional supports. The program emphasizes a culturally responsive approach that addresses students’ academic, linguistic, and social integration needs as they transition into the United States and the Boston Public Schools system. Upon completion of the Newcomer Program, students transition into one of the district’s language development pathways: Sheltered English Immersion (SEI), or Dual Language programs.*

Students with Limited or Interrupted Formal Education (SLIFE)

Students with Limited or Interrupted Formal Education (SLIFE) are those who are learning English and have had their education interrupted or have had limited formal education before joining the district. SLIFE typically come from households where a language other than English is spoken, have educational gaps from their home country, and are at least two grade levels behind in reading and mathematics. They may have attended school in the U.S., but may have gaps in language and literacy due to ineffective or absent instruction. They are often at risk of leaving school and may require intensive support.

Program Information

Students with Limited or Interrupted Formal Education (SLIFE) are those who are learning English and have had their education interrupted or have had limited formal education before joining the district. SLIFE typically come from households where a language other than English is spoken, have educational gaps from their home country, and are at least two grade levels behind in reading and mathematics. They may have attended school in the U.S., but may have gaps in language and literacy due to ineffective or absent instruction. They are often at risk of leaving school and may require intensive support.

In BPS SLIFE students are all newcomer multilingual learners who:

  • Newcomer;
  • Age 9 to 22;
  • Entered a US school after grade 2 or exited the US for 6 months or more;
  • Has no formal schooling or has interrupted formal schooling, that is, two years behind or more than his/her peers, or, has consistent, but limited formal schooling;
  • Functions two or more years below grade level in native language literacy relative to typical peers; OR functions two or more years below expected grade level in numeracy relative to typical peers.

The primary goal of the SLIFE Program is to offer newly arrived multilingual students who have experienced limited or interrupted education a nurturing and supportive environment. Within this program, they receive intensive English language instruction, grade-level content instruction with targeted English language development and social-emotional supports. The program emphasizes a culturally responsive approach that addresses students’ academic, linguistic, and support in their native language. Additionally, they benefit from supplementary linguistic, cultural, and academic support aimed at bridging educational gaps resulting from their academic background. The program is also designed to facilitate a smoother transition into the United States and the Boston Public Schools system. Upon completion of the SLIFE Program, students move into various educational paths available at their school setting, including Sheltered English Immersion, Transitional Bilingual, and Dual Language programs.

Out-of-School Time and Summer Programming

The Office of Multilingual and Multicultural Education (OMME) Supplemental Services team provides out-of-school time and summer programming that expands access to high-quality opportunities for language development, academic learning, and enrichment for multilingual learners across Boston Public Schools.

  • Program Information

Program Information

Grounded in research-based practices for high-quality out-of-school time and summer learning, OMME’s programming supports a diverse range of multilingual learners, including:

  • Newcomers
  • Students with Limited or Interrupted Formal Education (SLIFE)
  • Long-Term English Learners (LTELs)
  • Multilingual learners with Individualized Learning Plans (ILPs) or IEPs
  • Multilingual learners not currently receiving district multilingual program services
  • Multilingual learners who benefit from additional opportunities for language development and academic enrichment

Our Approach and Partnerships

OMME offers a wide range of out-of-school time and summer learning opportunities for multilingual learners through multiple program models and partnerships. These opportunities include school-based Summer Learning Academies (SLA) and programs offered in partnership with 5th Quarter community-based organizations.

Across all program settings, OMME designs learning experiences that affirm students’ identities and backgrounds, support language development, deepen academic learning, and build on home languages as valuable assets for belonging and connection. Together, these school-based and community-based opportunities expand access to engaging learning experiences that foster school connectedness, support social-emotional development, strengthen language growth, and promote academic achievement.
