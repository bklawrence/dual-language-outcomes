K-5 Dual Language Transition

On June 26, 2025, the North Shore School District 112 Board of Education voted to consolidate the K-5 Dual Language Program at Oak Terrace Elementary School and close Red Oak Elementary School. This page serves as a hub for updates and information throughout the two-year transition process.

Page last updated on October 21, 2026

Current Status

On October 20, 2026, Paridad Education Consulting presented the findings of its independent evaluation of North Shore School District 112's K-5 Dual Language Program to the Board of Education.

The evaluation highlighted strong academic achievement, including English reading and math growth that exceeded national comparison benchmarks, along with the commitment of teachers, students and families to bilingual education. Paridad also identified opportunities to strengthen Spanish instruction and recommended maintaining the district's 50/50 instructional model while ensuring that classroom instruction more consistently reflects that balance.

The district is reviewing the findings and recommendations. The administration will present its initial response and next steps at the November 10, 2026, Committee of the Whole meeting.

Meanwhile, preparations continue to bring the full K-5 Dual Language Program together at Oak Terrace Elementary School beginning in 2027-2028. This includes student and family transition activities, facility improvements and continued collaboration between the Red Oak and Oak Terrace school communities.

Task Force Presentation - May 19, 2026

English

The Approved Plan

The transition to one K-5 Dual Language site began in August 2026, when all incoming Dual Language kindergarten students started attending Oak Terrace Elementary School. The full K-5 Dual Language Program will come together at Oak Terrace in 2027-2028, with Red Oak Elementary School closing after the 2026-2027 school year.

The Early Childhood program will return to Green Bay School in the 2027-2028 school year.

Why the District is Moving to One K-5 Dual Language Site

The decision to move to one K-5 Dual Language site was driven by a significant and ongoing decline in enrollment, particularly among Spanish-speaking English learners. Spanish-speaking English learners are essential to maintaining the balance of the district’s two-way immersion model.

A single-site model is intended to strengthen the long-term sustainability of the program, support balanced classrooms, and provide a more consistent experience for students and families. The goal is to preserve the core pillars of the dual language program: bilingualism and biliteracy, high academic achievement, and sociocultural competence.

The phased transition is intended to sustain and strengthen the K-5 Dual Language Program while supporting students, families and staff throughout the process.

Why Oak Terrace Was Selected

Oak Terrace was selected as the K-5 Dual Language site because it has the physical capacity to house the full program, is closer to a larger concentration of Spanish-speaking families, and has received substantial facility investments through the district’s Long-Range Facilities Plan.

Oak Terrace is also the district’s newest elementary school building and has several features that support the transition, including ADA accessibility, updated mechanical systems, small group rooms and prior site improvements. The building was originally constructed to serve 600 students and is expected to remain under that capacity when the full K-5 Dual Language program is housed there in 2027-2028.

The district is also planning additional improvements at Oak Terrace to support the transition, including updates to learning spaces, furniture, food service, outdoor play areas and other areas identified through the Task Force and facilities planning process.

To support continuity for students and families, current Red Oak Principal Chelsey Maxwell will join Oak Terrace as co-principal beginning in the 2027-2028 school year.

Timeline

  • Dual Language Task Force convened and completed its work.
  • Family engagement events were held for Oak Terrace and Red Oak families.
  • Paridad Consulting began an independent review of the K-5 Dual Language Program.
  • Planning continued for Oak Terrace facility improvements and outdoor play spaces.

2026-2027

  • All Dual Language kindergarten students attend Oak Terrace.
  • Red Oak and Oak Terrace will continue transition activities for students, staff and families.

  • October 20, 2026: Paridad Education Consulting presented its independent evaluation findings and recommendations to the Board of Education.

  • November 10, 2026: The administration is scheduled to present its initial response and next steps at the Committee of the Whole meeting.

  • Planning and preparation will continue for the full K-5 transition.

2027-2028

  • The full K-5 Dual Language program will be housed at Oak Terrace.
  • Red Oak Elementary School will close after the 2026-2027 school year.
  • The Early Childhood program will return to Green Bay School.

Dual Language Task Force

The Dual Language Task Force was formed to help guide the transition to one K-5 dual language site at Oak Terrace. The group included parents/guardians, staff, administrators and district leaders, and was organized into three subcommittees: Student Success, Family Engagement, and Facilities and Operations.

The subcommittees completed their work and developed recommendations to support the transition. The full Task Force reviewed draft materials in April, and the district shared an update with the Board of Education in May 2026.

The Task Force’s work will help guide the district’s next steps in 2026-2027, including student and family transition planning, staff collaboration, facility improvements, outdoor play space planning and ongoing communication with families.

Independent Program Evaluation

Paridad Education Consulting completed an independent evaluation of the district's K-5 Dual Language Program between March and August 2026. The evaluation included classroom observations, academic performance data, curriculum reviews and feedback from teachers, students, administrators and families.

The evaluation identified several program strengths, including strong English language development, academic achievement in reading and math, positive classroom environments and high-quality Spanish instructional materials.

Paridad also identified opportunities for improvement and made recommendations in four primary areas:

  • Instructional Model: Maintain the district's 50/50 model while ensuring students receive a more consistent balance of instruction in English and Spanish.
  • Curriculum and Instruction: Strengthen coordination between English and Spanish instruction and protect instructional time for subjects taught in Spanish.
  • Biliteracy Assessment: Develop additional ways to measure students' Spanish language proficiency and literacy growth alongside their English development.
  • Class Size: Establish a planning target of 20 students per classroom, with a recommended maximum of 24 students and at least two sections per grade level.

These recommendations will help inform the district's long-term planning for the Dual Language Program. No final decisions have been made regarding their implementation.

The administration will present its initial response to the evaluation at the November 10 Committee of the Whole meeting. The district will continue sharing information as next steps are determined.

Family Engagement

In February, the district hosted three family engagement events for Oak Terrace and Red Oak families as part of the K-5 dual language transition planning process. Families toured Oak Terrace, learned more about proposed building and playground planning, connected with the Oak Terrace PTO, Red Oak PTO and BPAC, and provided feedback to help guide the next phase of work.

Feedback gathered through these events, surveys and Task Force discussions will help shape transition activities, facility planning and family communication during the 2026-2027 school year.

Oak Terrace Facility and Outdoor Space Planning

As part of the transition, the district is preparing improvements at Oak Terrace to support students, staff and families when the full K-5 Dual Language Program comes together in 2027-2028.

A new universally designed playground opened at Oak Terrace in August 2026, following extensive input from students, families and staff at both Oak Terrace and Red Oak. Additional planning includes improvements to classrooms, shared learning areas, staff spaces, the library, food service, the front entrance and outdoor play areas.

Design and approval work for the remaining improvements will continue this fall, with major interior renovations and additional site work planned for summer 2027. Updates will be shared as plans are finalized.

What Is Changing For The 2026-2027 And 2027-2028 School Years?

In the 2026-2027 school year, all Dual Language kindergarten students are attending Oak Terrace Elementary School.

In the 2027-2028 school year, the full K-5 Dual Language Program will be housed at Oak Terrace. Red Oak Elementary School will close after the 2026-2027 school year, and the Early Childhood program will return to Green Bay School.

The Dual Language Task Force has completed its report, and the district’s work now shifts to implementation. This includes transition activities between Red Oak and Oak Terrace, continued family communication and Oak Terrace facility planning.

Will Oak Terrace Have Enough Space For The Full K-5 Dual Language Program?

Yes. Oak Terrace was designed for 600 students and has 85,000 square feet of learning space. The district expects the building to remain under capacity when the full K-5 Dual Language Program is housed there beginning in 2027-2028.

Oak Terrace also has several features that support the transition, including larger classrooms, small group learning spaces, ADA accessibility and mechanical systems that are in good condition. During Phase 1 of the Long-Range Facilities Plan, the district invested more than $6 million at Oak Terrace for heating and cooling systems, roofs, site work and plumbing.

As part of the planning process, Wight & Co. completed a gap analysis comparing Oak Terrace to the district’s recently renovated K-5 schools. That work helped identify recommended updates to better align Oak Terrace with other modernized elementary buildings in District 112.

What Improvements Are Planned At Oak Terrace?

The recommended Oak Terrace project includes interior renovations and site improvements to support the full K-5 Dual Language Program beginning in 2027-2028.

The proposed work includes classroom updates such as new storage, flooring, LED lighting, acoustics, window shades and flexible furniture. It also includes improvements to specialized spaces such as art, music, band, orchestra and small group instruction rooms; updates to the Library Media Center; staff and security improvements; a wellness/respite room; a new food service servery; and site improvements to support pedestrian safety.

Some areas are not included in the current recommended scope. Bathroom remodels, corridor flooring and corridor lighting are deferred. The multipurpose room and gym will remain largely intact, except for the servery addition and a new PE office. Outdoor learning spaces previously considered for the front of the school are also deferred.

What Is The Timeline For Oak Terrace Improvements?

The new playground at Oak Terrace opened in August 2026.

Final design work, bidding and Board approval for the broader Oak Terrace project are expected in fall 2026. Major interior renovations and remaining site work are planned for summer 2027. Some work is expected to continue into the 2027-2028 school year, with final completion of the servery and remaining site work expected after the start of the school year.

The broader recommended renovation and site-improvement plan has an estimated total cost of $11.95 million and is planned to be funded through Phase 3 cash reserves.

How Is The District Addressing Traffic, Drop-Off And Pick-Up?

Traffic flow and safety were major areas of focus during the Task Force process. Director of Safety and Security Solvei Jurmu completed a traffic management report after concerns were raised by the Facilities and Operations Subcommittee.

The report found that Prairie Avenue currently shows stable, low-risk traffic behavior. The review included traffic accident and violation data from January 1, 2024, through November 20, 2025, and did not identify congestion-related safety issues that would prevent Oak Terrace from accommodating the transition.

The report also notes that Early Childhood will move out of Oak Terrace beginning in 2027-2028. That change will eliminate midday Early Childhood arrival and dismissal at Oak Terrace and reduce the number of daily arrival and dismissal periods. The district will continue to review arrival and dismissal procedures with school staff and the Highwood Police Department as implementation moves forward.

What Outdoor Play Space Improvements Are Planned?

The new universally designed playground at Oak Terrace opened in August 2026. Its design was shaped through staff presentations, family engagement events, Facilities and Operations Subcommittee meetings, parent presentations and student feedback from every grade level at both Red Oak and Oak Terrace.

The district is also reviewing improvements to the blacktop play area on the east side of the school. The concept includes relocating the gaga pit, redesigning the basketball area, adding space for activities such as kickball and four-square, adding swings and incorporating a shade structure. Because the broader outdoor play space project is extensive, the remaining work is expected to be phased.

How Will Red Oak And Oak Terrace Students And Families Come Together Before The Full Transition?

The Family Engagement Subcommittee recommended continued opportunities for Red Oak and Oak Terrace students and families to connect before the full transition in 2027-2028.

During the 2025-2026 school year, the two school communities came together through several activities, including a mariachi band event, Family Skate Night, a joint PTO meeting, a Chicago Wolves outing and a Dual Language Family Fun Fair.

During the 2026-2027 school year, the district will continue working with school leaders, PTOs and BPAC to support transition activities, family communication and opportunities for connection before the full K-5 Dual Language Program is housed at Oak Terrace.

What Will Happen To Red Oak Traditions, Artwork And Symbols?

The Task Force report recommends carrying forward traditions and activities that families value from both Red Oak and Oak Terrace.

The Red Oak rocket mascot will be retired when the school closes. Oak Terrace will keep the monarch butterfly mascot, which holds cultural meaning connected to migration, hope, strength and resilience.

The district will also bring meaningful Red Oak elements into the Oak Terrace campus. The “We All Live Here” mural from Red Oak will move to Oak Terrace and be installed on an exterior wall near the existing mural. The report also recommends increasing student artwork throughout the updated Oak Terrace campus.

Students from both Red Oak and Oak Terrace are already helping create new murals for Oak Terrace in collaboration with artist Jason Watts. The murals will be installed following the school's renovations in fall 2027.

How Will The Combined School Be Led?

Beginning in 2027-2028, Oak Terrace will have a leadership model that includes co-principals and an associate principal. Current Red Oak Principal Chelsey Maxwell will join Oak Terrace as co-principal to help support continuity for students, families and staff.

This leadership model is intended to help bring the two school communities together and support a positive transition into one K-5 Dual Language school.

Has The District Decided Whether To Change The Dual Language Instructional Model?

No. The district has not made a final decision to change its instructional model.

In its October 20 presentation, Paridad recommended maintaining the district's existing 50/50 model, which is designed to provide equal instructional time in English and Spanish. The evaluation found that actual instructional time currently favors English and recommended adjustments to achieve the intended balance more consistently.

The district will review these recommendations and determine next steps. The administration will present its initial response at the November 10 Committee of the Whole meeting.

What Did Paridad's Evaluation Find?

The evaluation highlighted strong academic achievement, English language development and supportive classroom environments. It also identified opportunities to strengthen Spanish instruction, improve assessment of students' Spanish language development and better coordinate instruction across both languages.

Paridad recommended maintaining the 50/50 instructional model while improving its implementation. Additional recommendations addressed curriculum, assessment and class-size guidelines.

The full evaluation report, Board presentation and family summary are available in the Current Status section at the top of this page.

Does the Evaluation Change the Approved Transition to Oak Terrace?

No. The previously approved plan remains in place. The full K-5 Dual Language Program will come together at Oak Terrace in the 2027-2028 school year, and Red Oak will close after the 2026-2027 school year.

Paridad's recommendations focus on strengthening the educational program and will help inform instructional planning as the transition moves forward.

How Did Families, Staff And Students Provide Input?

Input was gathered through the Dual Language Task Force, subcommittee meetings, family engagement events, family surveys, staff surveys, school tours, PTO meetings, BPAC meetings and direct student feedback.

The Facilities and Operations Subcommittee reviewed Oak Terrace, Red Oak, Indian Trail and Ravinia to compare learning spaces and understand recent district renovation work. Students at both Red Oak and Oak Terrace also provided feedback on playground design.

The Family Engagement Subcommittee gathered feedback on school traditions, family needs and transition priorities. The Student Success Subcommittee helped shape the program evaluation process and supported the selection of Paridad Consulting.

Who Served On The Dual Language Task Force?

The Dual Language Task Force included nearly 60 parents/guardians, staff members, board members and administrators. The group was organized into three subcommittees: Student Success, Family Engagement, and Facilities and Operations.

The Student Success Subcommittee focused on instruction, resources, professional learning and the independent program evaluation. The Family Engagement Subcommittee focused on family communication, transition activities and school traditions. The Facilities and Operations Subcommittee focused on Oak Terrace learning spaces, outdoor areas, traffic, safety and facility needs.

What Happens Next?

The district administration will present its initial response to Paridad's recommendations at the November 10, 2026, Committee of the Whole meeting.

The district will continue preparing for the full K-5 Dual Language transition to Oak Terrace in 2027-2028, including facility improvements, student and family engagement opportunities, and instructional planning.

Updates will be shared with families as decisions are made and additional plans are finalized.
