Spanish (Two-Way) Dual Language

District 15’s Spanish Dual Language Program is a two-way language immersion program in which students receive literacy and content instruction in both Spanish and English beginning in kindergarten. The program includes native Spanish speakers as well as students who speak English or another language at home.

The program is offered at Jane Addams Elementary School, Kimball Hill Elementary School, and Winston Campus Elementary School and is available to incoming kindergarten students who are fully registered at their home school.

Instruction begins in kindergarten with 80% of the day taught in Spanish and 20% in English. Each year, Spanish instruction decreases by 10% while English instruction increases by 10% until Grade 3, when instruction is balanced equally between both languages. This 50/50 model continues through Grade 6.

The goal of Community Consolidated School District 15’s Dual Language Program is to support our learners in the achievement of academic excellence through a standards-based curriculum.

It is our mission for students to become bilingual and biliterate, and to develop cross-cultural competencies in preparation for their future as productive leaders in our ever-changing global community.

Incoming Kindergarten Students

Parents have the option of enrolling their incoming kindergarten student in the Two-Way Spanish Dual Language Program, which is housed at three of our schools—Jane Addams, Winston Campus, and Kimball Hill.

Priority consideration is given to students who reside within the attendance boundaries of the dual language host schools and to siblings of students currently enrolled in the program. District-provided transportation is available for students who live more than 1.5 miles from the host schools (bus routes for some students who live greater distances may exceed 30 minutes).

Middle School Dual Language

The AAPPL (The ACTFL Assessment of Performance toward Proficiency in Languages) test is a web-based proficiency- and performance-based assessment of standards-based language learned as defined by the World-Readiness Standards for Learning Languages.

The AAPPL assesses the language that learners have acquired and practiced within a classroom setting, providing evidence that points toward a learner’s proficiency level.

The AAPPL is made up of four parts that assess three modes of communication:

  1. Interpersonal Listening & Speaking (ILS)
  2. Interpretive Listening (IL)
  3. Interpretive Reading (IR)
  4. Presentational Writing (PW)

Each component (part) takes between 30 and 45 minutes to complete (or up to 60 minutes for PW) and can be administered in one sitting, or in multiple sittings. It is given during Language Arts class. 

The AAPPL incorporates video, audio, and other engaging tasks that are age- and level-appropriate, which provide authentic, real-life opportunities for learners to demonstrate what they can do in the language!

AAPPL score reports were sent home on May 29, 2026. Please view the Middle School Dual Language Assessment Update - AAPPL - May 28, 2026 for more information on the assessment, what this performance means, the 7th and 8th grade dual language plan, high school programs, and preparing your child for the Seal of Biliteracy. 

JOIN THE DLPAC

The Dual Language Parent Advisory Committee (DLPAC) consists of parents whose students are enrolled in the district's dual language program at Jane Addams, Kimball Hill, and Winston Campus Elementary schools. 

The committee meets at least 4 times a year and participates in the planning, operation, and evaluation of the dual language program. In addition, important topics related to education, the district, and the community are shared.

These meetings are conducted in English and/or Spanish depending on what is requested.

Dual Language Culture Camp!

Grade K-5 students currently enrolled in our Dual Language program at Jane Addams, Kimball Hill, or Winston Campus come together for one week to explore and ENJOY Spanish-speaking cultures from different regions of the world at our Dual Language Culture Camp. Students participate in literacy and cultural experiences while also developing the third pillar of Dual Language education, which is socio-cultural competence.

WHEN: July 13-16, 2026, 1-4 p.m.

WHERE: Jane Addams Elementary School, 1020 E. Sayles Drive, Palatine

COST: The camp is free and transportation will be provided for those in need.
