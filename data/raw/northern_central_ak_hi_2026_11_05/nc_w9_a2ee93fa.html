Hmong International Academy

Overview

Hmong International Academy is a Community School built in 1999. Hmong International Academy does have air conditioning in the building and does have its own kitchen. The building does have a Safe and Welcoming Entrance, as defined by Capital Planning, Construction, and Maintenance. The building is 123,293 square feet in size and has a design enrollment of approximately 797 students.

Between school years 2014 and 2023, Minneapolis Public Schools has invested $2,533,777 in capital expenditures for the building. In 2017, a facilities condition assessment classified the building as being in fair condition. The Operations Division will be conducting another facilities condition assessment in calendar year 2025.

Hmong International Academy had an enrollment of 260 students as of 01 October, 2024, with approximately 82.0% of those students qualifying for Free or Reduced-Price Lunch benefits. Note that families are still submitting Applications for Educational Benefits, so this proportion may change over the course of the school year. Total enrollment is approximately 33% of the building’s designed enrollment, making the current building use underutilized [‘underutilized’ is defined as enrollment totaling less than 70% of the design enrollment].

Building Use

At direction of the Board of Education, in October 2024 district staff performed a physical space study of all District schools. This included a walkthrough of the buildings that included many different stakeholders – Board directors, principals, teachers, central office staff, custodians, and others. Guided Discussion

A guided discussion consisting of three questions was held with those who participated in the walkthrough. The major themes are included below, as are more detailed summaries of walkthrough participants’ answers.

  • Question 1: In what ways does the physical environment in this building best support learning?
  • Question 2: In what way does the physical environment in this building interfere with how we would like learning to happen today and in the future?
  • Question 3: What should the district know or appreciate about the physical environment of this building?

Question 1: In what ways does the physical environment in this building best support learning?

Hmong International Academy benefits from having newer, pod-based classrooms that allow for departmentalized teaching, which minimizes transition time and helps keep classes close together. Common spaces within the pods facilitate group meetings, while bathrooms in classrooms, including those for the Developmental and Cognitive Disabilities program, are beneficial for younger students. The school has separate exits to parking lots for different student groups, a circle drive for parent pickup, and accessible entrances for students with mobility challenges. The gym and cafeteria provide plenty of room for the whole school to gather, and the school’s location on a public park fosters a sense of community. The school has a small, fenced-in playground for younger children, green space around the building, and ample staff parking. The library is a standout feature with its beautiful windows and new carpet, while a new sensory room and support spaces in each pod ensure students can access additional support close to their classrooms.

Question 2: In what way does the physical environment in this building interfere with how we would like learning to happen today and in the future?

Hmong International Academy is located near busy streets, which leads to safety concerns due to traffic around the building. Additionally, the numerous doors on the first floor create a security challenge, making it difficult to monitor access effectively. The science labs, while present, are not well-suited for the current age group of students, as they no longer align with the needs of the building’s student population.

Question 3: What should the district know or appreciate about the physical environment of this building?

The building faces challenges with its heating and cooling systems, as some rooms can be very hot while others are very cold, creating an uncomfortable environment. There are also ceiling leaks that need attention. Access to the small playground is a safety concern, as there is no badge access to re-enter the building, and propping doors open creates security risks. Finally, the security of the parking lots is a concern, particularly when the school is not in session.

Overall Discussion Themes

  • Great pod-based arrangement of grades and classrooms

  • Separate, accessible entrances for the beginning and end of day

  • Beautiful library surrounded by windows and a view of a public park

  • Traffic and neighborhood safety concerns

  • HVAC is inconsistent throughout the building
