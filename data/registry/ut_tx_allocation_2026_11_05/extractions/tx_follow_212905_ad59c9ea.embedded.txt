Our bilingual education program helps students build strong academic skills in their first language while gradually and confidently gaining mastery of the English language. Texas Education Code §89.1201 defines of bilingual programs, including dual-language immersion and transitional bilingual education, is to help Emergent Bilingual (EB) students develop literacy and academic skills in both English and their home or partner language. These programs use content-based instruction to build proficiency in listening, speaking, reading, and writing while ensuring mastery of grade-level skills in language arts, math, science, and social studies so students can participate equitably in school. Texas Education Code 29.055 defines the program of instruction “English as a Second Language” as “a program of intensive instruction in English from teachers trained in recognizing and dealing with language differences.” The program goal is to enable Emergent Bilingual students to become competent in the comprehension, speaking, reading, and composition of the English language through the integrated use of second language methods. Bilingual Programs OUR GOAL Students will be literate in English and another language OUR TEACHERS Are literate in English and another language OUR INSTRUCTION Is in students' native language, and as students gain proficiency in English, their instruction in English increases Dual Language One-Way Immersion This program focuses on EBs attaining biliteracy— meaning functional literacy and fluency in oral and written forms—in both English and the home language over a six to seven year time frame before entering an English-only instructional environment. Dual Language Two-Way Immersion This program is administered to EB and non-EB (with English home language) students, with the goal of attaining biliteracy over a six to seven year timeframe. This program is only offered at our Pre-K through 8th grade Birdwell Dual Language Immersion School. Students must apply to attend this campus. ESL Programs Students will be English literate Are English literate Predominantly in English English as a Second Language (ESL) programming meets the most basic requirement for educational language services. Instruction in ESL programs is predominately in English, with the main instructional goal of English acquisition. Teachers in ESL programs are English literate and are certified to teach English learners. However, they do not have to be proficient in a language other than English. Although Spanish is the primary language spoken by most of our students, we also serve students who speak 23 other languages, including Arabic, Hindi, Indonesian, Portuguese, Vietnamese, and many others. ESL services are offered through two program types: ESL Content-Based Serves students who are identified as having “limited English proficiency” through English-only instruction with a designated full-time teacher. Instruction in ESL Content based classes merge learning English with specific content like mathematics, science, or social studies. ESL instruction is presented along with supplementary content instruction in non-language subject areas, including math, science, social studies, and other subjects. If entering the program during or after first grade, students remain in the program for a minimum of two to five years before being eligible to exit. Pull-Out ESL The pull-out ESL program offers English-only instruction exclusively for English Language Arts, not other content areas. Students in the program are removed or “pulled out” of mainstream instructional environments to receive ESL pull-out. If entering the program during or after first grade, students remain in the program for a minimum of two to five years before being eligible to exit. Quick Links FAQs The Dual Language One Way Program is a bilingual academic program where students learn in both English and Spanish. It utilizes the students’ native language for academic instruction 50 percent of the time, and English instruction for the other 50 percent. Students in this program are native Spanish speakers and receive the same curriculum as the general education program, through enrichment methods and strategies. If the Home Language Survey indicates a language other than English, students are assessed with an oral language proficiency test and an English norm-referenced test. The results of the assessment then determine the student’s program placement based on criteria set by the state. The student’s daily oral face-to-face communication for personal and social purposes is known as Basic Interpersonal Communication Skills, or BICS. It takes two years of exposure to English to develop a relatively high degree of English communicative skills. The level of English proficiency needed for academic learning (reading and writing) is referred to as Cognitive Academic Language Proficiency, or CALP. CALP takes from five to seven years to develop. Although your child is orally fluent in English, they may still not be academically prepared to compete in an all-English classroom.
