Students in dual language programs are presented with the social and cognitive benefits of bilingualism. They gain a second language, a broader vocabulary, and multiple views of the world (Cazabon, Lambert & Heise-Baigorria, 2002). In order to acquire these benefits, the program sets out to accomplish the following goals: • Develop high levels of proficiency in Spanish and English. • Help students achieve grade level academic performance in Spanish and English. • Raise self-esteem for all children. • Take advantage of the optimal window of learning a second language during the primary years. • Increase the use of foreign language among monolingual groups. • Give students an upper edge in high school, college, and the workplace. • Develop bilingualism, biliteracy and biculturalism. • Develop positive cross-cultural attitudes and behavior. 5 What Does the Research Say About Dual Language? Multiple benefits exist for acquiring a second language during the primary years. Some of these benefits include: • Foreign language study has shown to increase listening skills, memory, and a greater understanding of one’s own language. (Lapkin, et al., 1990) • Students who acquire advanced levels of proficiency in second languages often experience certain cognitive and linguistic advantages when compared to monolingual students. (Cummins, 1981; Lambert, Genesee, Holobow & Chartrand, 1993) • Foreign language learners consistently outperform control groups in core subject areas on standardized tests, often significantly. (Armstrong & Rogers 1997, Saunders 1998, Masciantonio 1977, Rafferty 1986, Andrade, Kretschmer & Kretschmer 1989) • Early foreign language study gives children unique insight into other cultures and builds their cultural competency skills in a way that no other discipline is able to do. (Curtain & Dahlberg 2004) • The resulting benefits to self-image, self-esteem, and satisfaction with school experience are enormous. Evidence from several studies show language students to have a significantly higher self-concept than do non-language students. (Masciantonio 1977, Saunders 1998, Andrade, et al.1989) 6 History of Dual Language Programs in the U.S. Dual language programs first appeared in the U.S. over forty years ago. Dade County Public Schools in Miami, Florida was the first district to implement a Spanish-English dual language program in 1962. In the 1970’s programs spread into Washington D.C.; Chicago, Illinois; and San Diego, California. Today, there are more than 370 dual language programs nationwide. Spanish is the most popular target language with 94% of the programs using Spanish and English as the languages of instruction. Programs are found in many different communities throughout the country. Large urban districts, middle class suburban districts, as well as small rural districts all have found success and value in implementing dual language programs. “I can help my parents translate when they talk to some of our neighbors.” – Fallon Ash, student Is the Dual Language Program Right for Your Family? The dual language program truly incorporates the entire family. Students learn language during the day, while parents are encouraged to learn language by night. Children who have a love of learning, age-appropriate language development, and support and encouragement from home will find success in the dual language program. Your child is a good candidate for the dual language if he or she: • Is developmentally on target in his or her native language. • Expresses an interest in different people, places, and things. • Wants to become fluent in Spanish and English. The program is a good fit for families that: • Value other cultures – the people, art, history, etc. • Support learning a second language and encourage practicing the language. • Are willing to participate in school projects and activities. • Are willing to commit to at least five years of program participation. There are many reasons families choose to participate in the dual language program: career opportunities, multicultural appreciation, and/or the ease of becoming bilingual at an early age. Parents, however, are the key to the success of any program. All families who enroll in this program show a commitment to support their child at school and at home. 8 Curriculum and Teaching The dual language staff are trained in second language acquisition and deliver a curriculum that is aligned to state and national standards. Foreign language and English language learner standards are also incorporated. Assessments are given throughout the program to track academic and language achievement. Differentiated instruction is incorporated into daily lesson plans to meet the needs of all students. All dual language teachers receive specialized training in Guided Language Acquisition Design (GLAD). The GLAD model provides teachers with strategies that promote second language acquisition, academic achievement, and cross-cultural skills. This research- based program is a United States Department of Education, Project of Academic Excellence, which enhances language acquisition while promoting multicultural interactions. Secondary Dual Language Students have the opportunity to continue the dual language program at the middle and high school levels. Students continue their study of the Spanish language through Spanish literacy and content classes in science and social studies. 9 FAQs Why should I consider exclusively in the target (Spanish) language. Los estudiantes en el programa de lenguaje dual son presentados con los beneficios sociales y cognitivos del bilingüismo. Múltiples beneficios existen al adquirir una segunda lengua durante los primeros años de escuela. Algunos de los beneficios de aprender una segunda lengua incluyen: • El estudio de una lengua extranjera ha demostrado un aumento en las habilidades de escuchar, memoria y un mayor entendimiento de la lengua de uno mismo. (Lapkin, et al., 1990) • Los estudiantes que lleguen a niveles avanzados de competencia en segundos idiomas a menudo experimentan ciertas ventajas cognitivas y lingüísticas, en comparación con los estudiantes monolingües. (Cummins, 1981; Lambert, Genesse, Holobow & Chartrand, 1993) • Los aprendices de lenguas extranjeras superan constantemente los grupos de control en las materias principales en los exámenes estandarizados a menudo de manera significativa. (Armstrong & Rogers 1997, Saunders 1998, Masciantonio 1977, Rafferty 1986, Andrade, Kretschmer & Kretschmer 1989) • El estudio temprano del idioma extranjero da a los niños una visión única de otras culturas y construye sus habilidades de la competencia cultural de una manera que ninguna otra disciplina es capaz de hacer. (Curtain & Dahlberg, 2004) • El beneficio resultante a la propia imagen, autoestima y satisfacción con la experiencia de la escuela son enormes. La evidencia de varios estudios demuestran que estudiantes de idiomas tienen una significativamente mayor concepto de su propia persona que los alumnos no lingüísticos. (Masciantonio 1977, Saunders 1998, Andrade, et al. 1989) 18 Historia de Los Programas de Lenguaje Dual en Los EE.UU. Los programas de lenguaje dual aparecieron por primera vez en Los Estados Unidos hace más de 40 años. El distrito escolar del condado Dade en Miami, Florida fue el primero en implementar un programa de lenguaje dual en español-inglés en 1962. En los años 70 los programas se esparcieron por Washington D.C., Chicago, Illinois, y San Diego, California. Hoy en día existen más de 370 programas de lenguaje dual en toda la nación. Español es el idioma más popular de adquisición con un 94% de los programas usando español e inglés como las lenguas de instrucción. Los programas se encuentran en muchas diferentes comunidades del país. Grandes distritos urbanos, distritos suburbanos de clase media tanto como en pequeños distritos rurales han encontrado éxito y valores en implementar programas de lenguaje dual. 19 “Puedo ayudar a mis padres a traducir cuando hablan con algunos de nuestros vecinos. “ – Fallon Ash, estudiante ¿Es el Programa de Lenguaje Dual una Buena Opción para su Familia? El programa de lenguaje dual involucra a toda la familia. Los alumnos aprenden una lengua durante el día, mientras que a los padres se les motivará aprender la lengua extranjera en la noche. Los niños que tienen un deseo de aprender, que tienen un nivel de lenguaje apropiado para su edad, y el apoyo y motivación en casa encontrarán éxito en el programa de lenguaje dual. Su niño es un buen candidato para el programa si es que él o ella: • Se está desarrollando apropiadamente en la adquisición de su lengua materna. • Expresa un interés por diferentes personas, lugares y cosas. • Quiere llegar a ser una persona bilingüe en español e inglés. El programa de lenguaje dual es bueno para las familias que: • Valoran otras culturas: las personas, el arte, la historia, etc. • Respaldan el aprendizaje de una segunda lengua y motivan practicar la lengua. • Están dispuestos a participar en las actividades y proyectos de la escuela. • Están dispuestos a comprometerse por lo menos 5 años de participación en el programa.
