This report provides the specific information required by law for the 2024-25 school year (paid during FY 2026). Chapter 115, Subchapter VII, Wisconsin Statutes provides partial, yet critical, funding for English Learner (EL)1 students in the state whose language education needs must be met by the districts in which they are enrolled. It is the only state funding source for students classified as English Learners and supports districts in providing instructional services that foster language proficiency development and leverage students’ linguistic assets for academic learning. It should be noted this report will use the term English Learner (EL) rather than Limited English Proficient student, the term used in state statute. This follows the precedent set in the reauthorization of ESEA, as amended by the Every Student Succeeds Act. Language Programs and Locations In 2024-25, 384 local education agencies (LEAs) in Wisconsin enrolled one or more English Learner students, totaling 55,772 English Learner students enrolled in public and independent charter school districts statewide.2 This represents 6.9% of the total student enrollment in public and independent charter schools that year. Fifty-eight3 districts applied to receive state bilingual- bicultural categorical aid for the 2024-25 school year. Those districts served 25,427 ELs who were eligible for state aid.4 This equates to 45.6% percent of all English Learners enrolled Wisconsin public and independent charter school districts receiving bilingual-bicultural services that were partially reimbursed by the state. Students from nine language backgrounds were supported by the state bilingual-bicultural aid program: Arabic, Chinese, Hmong, Korean, Mandingo, Pashto, Somali, Spanish, and Swahili. 1 An English Learner (EL) student is defined in federal law as a student whose proficiency in English impacts their academic performance. This instruction can be delivered by a teacher with a bilingual license or, for students who are not Spanish speakers, by a teacher with an English as a Second Language (ESL) license and a bilingual aide. EL students not receiving bilingual services must be served by other types of language instruction educational programs, such as English as a Second Language (ESL) program models. Per Wisconsin Statute 115.95, only students served in bilingual programs are eligible for state BLBC categorial aid reimbursement. Report on the Status of Bilingual-Bicultural Education Programs in Wisconsin 2 Program Costs for Categorically Aided District Programs When the bilingual-bicultural state aids program was first established, the appropriation from the state legislature was divided evenly among districts so that each received the same percentage of reimbursement for its approved expenditures. In 1999, Wisconsin Act 9 (the biennial budget) set aside $250,000 from the bilingual-bicultural appropriation. This set-aside was to be divided proportionally among school districts participating in the bilingual-bicultural aid program whose enrollments in the previous school year were at least 15 percent English Learners. The remainder of the appropriation was divided evenly among all the districts according to their approved total budgets. The same formula continues through the current fiscal year. Eleven districts were eligible for this set-aside in 2024-25: Abbotsford, Arcadia, Beloit, Darlington, Delavan-Darien, Green Bay, Madison, Milwaukee, Sheboygan, Waterloo, and Whitewater. Program costs vary from district to district due to several factors, including, but not limited to, the following: • Number of students served; • Program and service delivery model, such as in-class vs. pull-out programs, or self-contained programs (e.g., dual language immersion); • Staff/student ratio; • Amount of instructional contact time; • Instructional resources provided (e.g., texts, primary/home language materials and assessments); • Growth of program and the need to purchase additional materials • Variety of languages spoken by students and families in a district; • English-language proficiency level of students and range of proficiency levels among students in a classroom or in a school; • Amount of previous schooling of English Learners; and • Outreach and services to EL students and families, immigrant children and youth, or refugee status of students and families. It is important to note that, even if a school district has English Learners and a bilingual program, it does not necessarily mean that all ELs in the district are enrolled in that program. There are several reasons why this could be so: the bilingual program may be targeted at specific grade levels; the bilingual program may be offered at some schools in the district, but not others; or there could be a bilingual program for one language group in the district that meets the statutorily set threshold numbers, but not for students speaking other languages. When the funding source was first established in 1975, it reimbursed school districts for a substantial percentage of their allowable costs. However, the percentage of approved costs being reimbursed by state aid has significantly decreased since that time. The amount allocated to BLBC state aids was reduced in FY10 and FY12 and was then flat funded until FY23. During that time, the reimbursement rate fell to a low of 7.7 percent in FY23. As part of the 2023-25 state budget, the appropriation was increased by $1,500,000 for FY24 and FY25. As a result, the reimbursement rate increased slightly to 9.15 percent in FY24. The reimbursement rate fell to 8.55 percent in FY25 due to an increase in the number of districts participating in the program. The 2024-25 biennial budget maintained the increased funding level established in the previous budget but did not include any additional funding. The total amount available for reimbursement for FY 2026 was $10,089,800 and reimbursement rate was 7.92%. The current BLBC reimbursement rate covers a small portion of the actual costs incurred by districts. Additionally, due to the requirements of the state statute, some districts are not eligible for any reimbursement or receive reimbursement for only a portion of the EL students in the district. As a result of this, as well as the fact that no other state-level funding exists for English Learner students, districts do not receive any funding to support language programming for approximately half of identified ELs in the state. This means that the vast majority of costs associated with providing these required services must be paid for with local funds.
