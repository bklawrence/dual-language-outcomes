Welcome to District 54 Dual Language and Immersion Programs Dual Language and Immersion are programs of choice that are available for all students in District 54. Dual Language classes at the elementary level are composed of approximately 50% English or other language proficient students and 50% Spanish or Japanese-proficient students. Chinese Immersion is a one-way immersion experience where the majority of students have no prior experience or proficiency in the Chinese language. Dual Language and Chinese Immersion teachers, together with the English partner teachers, foster high academic achievement by teaching the two target languages through content. These programs seek to develop academic and cultural competence and bilingualism for students from kindergarten through eighth grade. District 54 has partnered with the Spanish Embassy and is honored to have our Spanish Dual Language Schools recognized as International Spanish Academies. Program Goals In addition to the district goals, District 54 also has language goals. The goals are for students in these programs To perform academically at grade level in two languages, commensurate with their monolingual English-speaking peers; To use two languages comfortably and effectively in social situations appropriate for their age level; To communicate effectively through reading and writing in two languages at a level appropriate for their age; and To demonstrate an appreciation of cultural diversity and cross-cultural competence across a variety of social situations. Language Proficiency Below is information regarding the language proficiency targets for each grade level. This information is aligned with the American Council on the Teaching of Foreign Languages and reflects the continuum of language learning from Novice through Advanced levels. The end of year targets were determined after reviewing best practice and national expectations for dual and immersion programs as well as District 54 student language achievement data over time. Language learning is an investment of time and effort. At initial levels, students move quickly from single words to phrases but as students grow and are able to use language in challenging academic contexts, more growth is required to reach higher levels. According to the College Board, students who are showing evidence of Intermediate Mid to Intermediate High proficiency are likely to function successfully in an Advanced Placement (AP) course. Kindergarten: Novice Low 1st Grade: Novice Mid 2nd Grade: Novice High 3rd & 4th Grade: Intermediate Low 5th & 6th Grade: Intermediate Mid 7th & 8th Grade: Intermediate High Kindergarten & 1st Grade: Novice Low 2nd Grade: Novice Mid 3rd & 4th Grade: Novice High 5th & 6th Grade: Intermediate Low 7th & 8th Grade: Intermediate Mid The information below summarizes how students process and comprehend spoken language and written text across each proficiency level band. Novice Low (N1): Recognizes a few memorized words, phrases, letters, or characters when heard, spoken or read. Novice Mid (N2, N3): Understands familiar words, phrases, and simple sentences in spoken language and short learned texts. Novice High (N4): Identifies main topics and simple sentences in short spoken messages, everyday conversations, and simple texts. Intermediate Low (I1): Captures main ideas of short, simple presentations, overheard conversations, and familiar written texts. Intermediate Mid (I2–I4): Easily understands main ideas in presentations, daily conversations, and texts related to school, personal interests, and daily life. Intermediate High (I5): Understands main ideas and key supporting details in organized speech, stories, and written texts across various time frames and genres. Advanced Low: Comprehends complex narratives, detailed speech, abstract concepts, extended arguments, and lengthy written texts across diverse genres and unfamiliar topics. The information below summarizes how students generate spoken communication and produce written text across each proficiency level band. Novice Low (N1): Communicates using single practiced words or phrases when speaking and copies familiar words, characters, or phrases when writing. Novice Mid (N2, N3): Communicates on familiar topics using memorized words and phrases when speaking and writes lists or memorized phrases. Novice High (N4): Exchanges basic information using simple sentences in speech and writes short messages and notes about everyday life. Intermediate Low (I1): Participates in conversations using simple sentences to ask and answer questions, and writes briefly about familiar topics using a series of simple sentences. Intermediate Mid (I2–I4): Speaks in connected sentences and organized presentations about school or personal topics, and writes connected sentences or simple paragraphs across various time frames. Intermediate High (I5): Delivers detailed presentations and talks in an organized way about events across time frames, and writes organized paragraphs on general, academic, and professional topics. Advanced Low: Expresses opinions freely and spontaneously on complex issues with detailed presentations, and writes extensive, well-organized text with significant precision.
