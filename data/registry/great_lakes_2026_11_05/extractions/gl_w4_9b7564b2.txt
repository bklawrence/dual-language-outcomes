K-5 Dual Language Transition On June 26, 2025, the North Shore School District 112 Board of Education voted to consolidate the K-5 Dual Language Program at Oak Terrace Elementary School and close Red Oak Elementary School. This page serves as a hub for updates and information throughout the two-year transition process. Page last updated on October 21, 2026 On October 20, 2026, Paridad Education Consulting presented the findings of its independent evaluation of North Shore School District 112's K-5 Dual Language Program to the Board of Education. The evaluation highlighted strong academic achievement, including English reading and math growth that exceeded national comparison benchmarks, along with the commitment of teachers, students and families to bilingual education. Paridad also identified opportunities to strengthen Spanish instruction and recommended maintaining the district's 50/50 instructional model while ensuring that classroom instruction more consistently reflects that balance. The district is reviewing the findings and recommendations. The administration will present its initial response and next steps at the November 10, 2026, Committee of the Whole meeting. Meanwhile, preparations continue to bring the full K-5 Dual Language Program together at Oak Terrace Elementary School beginning in 2027-2028. This includes student and family transition activities, facility improvements and continued collaboration between the Red Oak and Oak Terrace school communities. The transition to one K-5 Dual Language site began in August 2026, when all incoming Dual Language kindergarten students started attending Oak Terrace Elementary School. The full K-5 Dual Language Program will come together at Oak Terrace in 2027-2028, with Red Oak Elementary School closing after the 2026-2027 school year. The Early Childhood program will return to Green Bay School in the 2027-2028 school year. The decision to move to one K-5 Dual Language site was driven by a significant and ongoing decline in enrollment, particularly among Spanish-speaking English learners. Spanish-speaking English learners are essential to maintaining the balance of the district’s two-way immersion model. A single-site model is intended to strengthen the long-term sustainability of the program, support balanced classrooms, and provide a more consistent experience for students and families. The goal is to preserve the core pillars of the dual language program: bilingualism and biliteracy, high academic achievement, and sociocultural competence. The phased transition is intended to sustain and strengthen the K-5 Dual Language Program while supporting students, families and staff throughout the process. Oak Terrace was selected as the K-5 Dual Language site because it has the physical capacity to house the full program, is closer to a larger concentration of Spanish-speaking families, and has received substantial facility investments through the district’s Long-Range Facilities Plan. Oak Terrace is also the district’s newest elementary school building and has several features that support the transition, including ADA accessibility, updated mechanical systems, small group rooms and prior site improvements. The building was originally constructed to serve 600 students and is expected to remain under that capacity when the full K-5 Dual Language program is housed there in 2027-2028. The district is also planning additional improvements at Oak Terrace to support the transition, including updates to learning spaces, furniture, food service, outdoor play areas and other areas identified through the Task Force and facilities planning process. The Dual Language Task Force was formed to help guide the transition to one K-5 dual language site at Oak Terrace. The group included parents/guardians, staff, administrators and district leaders, and was organized into three subcommittees: Student Success, Family Engagement, and Facilities and Operations. The subcommittees completed their work and developed recommendations to support the transition. The full Task Force reviewed draft materials in April, and the district shared an update with the Board of Education in May 2026. The Task Force’s work will help guide the district’s next steps in 2026-2027, including student and family transition planning, staff collaboration, facility improvements, outdoor play space planning and ongoing communication with families. Paridad Education Consulting completed an independent evaluation of the district's K-5 Dual Language Program between March and August 2026. The evaluation included classroom observations, academic performance data, curriculum reviews and feedback from teachers, students, administrators and families. The evaluation identified several program strengths, including strong English language development, academic achievement in reading and math, positive classroom environments and high-quality Spanish instructional materials. Paridad also identified opportunities for improvement and made recommendations in four primary areas: Instructional Model: Maintain the district's 50/50 model while ensuring students receive a more consistent balance of instruction in English and Spanish. Curriculum and Instruction: Strengthen coordination between English and Spanish instruction and protect instructional time for subjects taught in Spanish. Biliteracy Assessment: Develop additional ways to measure students' Spanish language proficiency and literacy growth alongside their English development. Class Size: Establish a planning target of 20 students per classroom, with a recommended maximum of 24 students and at least two sections per grade level. These recommendations will help inform the district's long-term planning for the Dual Language Program. No final decisions have been made regarding their implementation. The administration will present its initial response to the evaluation at the November 10 Committee of the Whole meeting. The district will continue sharing information as next steps are determined. Feedback gathered through these events, surveys and Task Force discussions will help shape transition activities, facility planning and family communication during the 2026-2027 school year. Additional planning includes improvements to classrooms, shared learning areas, staff spaces, the library, food service, the front entrance and outdoor play areas. Design and approval work for the remaining improvements will continue this fall, with major interior renovations and additional site work planned for summer 2027. Oak Terrace was designed for 600 students and has 85,000 square feet of learning space. The district expects the building to remain under capacity when the full K-5 Dual Language Program is housed there beginning in 2027-2028. Oak Terrace also has several features that support the transition, including larger classrooms, small group learning spaces, ADA accessibility and mechanical systems that are in good condition. During Phase 1 of the Long-Range Facilities Plan, the district invested more than $6 million at Oak Terrace for heating and cooling systems, roofs, site work and plumbing. As part of the planning process, Wight & Co. completed a gap analysis comparing Oak Terrace to the district’s recently renovated K-5 schools. That work helped identify recommended updates to better align Oak Terrace with other modernized elementary buildings in District 112. The recommended Oak Terrace project includes interior renovations and site improvements to support the full K-5 Dual Language Program beginning in 2027-2028. The proposed work includes classroom updates such as new storage, flooring, LED lighting, acoustics, window shades and flexible furniture. It also includes improvements to specialized spaces such as art, music, band, orchestra and small group instruction rooms; updates to the Library Media Center; staff and security improvements; a wellness/respite room; a new food service servery; and site improvements to support pedestrian safety. Some areas are not included in the current recommended scope. Bathroom remodels, corridor flooring and corridor lighting are deferred. The multipurpose room and gym will remain largely intact, except for the servery addition and a new PE office. Outdoor learning spaces previously considered for the front of the school are also deferred. The new playground at Oak Terrace opened in August 2026. Final design work, bidding and Board approval for the broader Oak Terrace project are expected in fall 2026. Major interior renovations and remaining site work are planned for summer 2027. Some work is expected to continue into the 2027-2028 school year, with final completion of the servery and remaining site work expected after the start of the school year. The broader recommended renovation and site-improvement plan has an estimated total cost of $11.95 million and is planned to be funded through Phase 3 cash reserves. Traffic flow and safety were major areas of focus during the Task Force process. Director of Safety and Security Solvei Jurmu completed a traffic management report after concerns were raised by the Facilities and Operations Subcommittee. The report found that Prairie Avenue currently shows stable, low-risk traffic behavior. The review included traffic accident and violation data from January 1, 2024, through November 20, 2025, and did not identify congestion-related safety issues that would prevent Oak Terrace from accommodating the transition. The report also notes that Early Childhood will move out of Oak Terrace beginning in 2027-2028. That change will eliminate midday Early Childhood arrival and dismissal at Oak Terrace and reduce the number of daily arrival and dismissal periods. The district will continue to review arrival and dismissal procedures with school staff and the Highwood Police Department as implementation moves forward. The new universally designed playground at Oak Terrace opened in August 2026. Its design was shaped through staff presentations, family engagement events, Facilities and Operations Subcommittee meetings, parent presentations and student feedback from every grade level at both Red Oak and Oak Terrace. The district is also reviewing improvements to the blacktop play area on the east side of the school. The concept includes relocating the gaga pit, redesigning the basketball area, adding space for activities such as kickball and four-square, adding swings and incorporating a shade structure. Because the broader outdoor play space project is extensive, the remaining work is expected to be phased. The Task Force report recommends carrying forward traditions and activities that families value from both Red Oak and Oak Terrace. The Red Oak rocket mascot will be retired when the school closes. Oak Terrace will keep the monarch butterfly mascot, which holds cultural meaning connected to migration, hope, strength and resilience. The district will also bring meaningful Red Oak elements into the Oak Terrace campus. The “We All Live Here” mural from Red Oak will move to Oak Terrace and be installed on an exterior wall near the existing mural. The report also recommends increasing student artwork throughout the updated Oak Terrace campus. Students from both Red Oak and Oak Terrace are already helping create new murals for Oak Terrace in collaboration with artist Jason Watts. The murals will be installed following the school's renovations in fall 2027. Beginning in 2027-2028, Oak Terrace will have a leadership model that includes co-principals and an associate principal.
