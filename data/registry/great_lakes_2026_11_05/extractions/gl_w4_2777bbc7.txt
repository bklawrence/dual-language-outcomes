{{schoolName}} Dual Language Immersion (DLI) Contact Us Allison Woods Assistant Superintendent for Exceptional Learners and Whole Child317-869-4405 Kim Park Director of Multilingual Learners and Dual Language 317-869-4799 The Dual Language Immersion (DLI) program is committed to the following: To provide valuable skills to students To develop both native language and second language To broaden students’ understanding of the world To develop listening, speaking, reading, and writing skills in English and Spanish To obtain high academic achievement Dual Language is a type of bilingual program where two languages are used for instruction. Curriculum concepts are taught in English and Spanish. Native English speakers, native Spanish speakers, and emergent bilingual children learn together throughout the day. Dual Language Program Benefits Social Benefits The program environment embraces diversity and bilingualism. The program encourages all students to maintain a strong connection to their heritage and culture. Cognitive Benefits Native Spanish speakers are able to improve and maintain Spanish while learning English. English speakers can take advantage of the opportunity to learn a second language at an early age, making them more likely to achieve native-like fluency. Academic Benefits Many studies have demonstrated that Dual Language is the most effective program model for students acquiring English to succeed in school. In a global society, a Dual Language education prepares students for college and future careers Three Pillars of Dual Language Our program is built on three fundamental goals designed to prepare students for success in a global society: Bilingualism & Biliteracy Students learn to speak, read, write, and understand both program languages fluently, using them together to strengthen their overall communication skills. High Academic Achievement Students are expected to meet or exceed grade-level standards in all core subjects and special courses across both program languages. Sociocultural Competence We foster an environment of equity and inclusion, valuing diverse backgrounds and home languages while teaching students to advocate for themselves and others. Students learn to read and write in both languages. Dual Language teachers follow district expectations for each grade level. Teachers will utilize the Indiana Academic Standards and curriculum maps provided by the district. Teachers will collaborate to ensure fidelity and coherence between the languages of the program. Curriculum-based assessments will be administered in the appropriate language of instruction throughout the course of the school year. State assessments will be administered when designated by the Indiana Department of Education (IDOE) Avant STAMPe Grade 2 - 6 students will participate in the annual STAMP language proficiency assessment. This test measures students’ learning proficiency in Spanish in the language domains: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing. Program Eligibility and Enrollment MSD of Warren Township currently offers Dual Language programs only at Pleasant Run Elementary School and Stonybrook Intermediate Middle School. Upon enrollment into those buildings, students may be considered to participate in the Dual Language program based on available space and district placement considerations. Parents are required to complete a Dual Language Program Application and participate in an information parent meeting. Parents and students should be committed to the program from K–8th Grade. Program Withdrawal Every effort will be made to support a child’s success in the Dual Language program. Should a parent or the school have concerns regarding a student’s progress, a meeting should be held to discuss any concerns and potential interventions. The meeting should include a building administrator, classroom teacher(s), and the parent/guardian. Parents or the school may withdraw the student from the DLI program after collaboration and sufficient evidence has been gathered. The Dual Language Program Withdrawal Form must be completed and maintained locally. Certificate of Multilingual Proficiency (CoMP) Indiana Certificate of Multilingual Proficiency is an award made by a participating school corporation, charter school, or accredited non-public high school designating on a student’s transcript that the student has attained a high level of proficiency, sufficient for meaningful use in college and a career, in one or more languages in addition to English. Requirements for Students: English: Students must earn at least eight credits in English Language Arts. Students must pass a graduation qualifying exam (SAT, ACT, ASVAB) Multilingual Learners ONLY: Achieve a 5.0 or higher on WIDA ACCESS or exit ML services via Additional Evidence Review Spanish: Students must earn six credits in a single World Language (WL) through coursework. If a student enrolls already proficient in a WL, a score of Intermediate High on an approved WL assessment can serve as demonstration of proficiency in lieu of course work. The student would then receive six WL credits. Assessments can be taken at any time during their high school career. Students must pass an approved external assessment in WL at an Intermediate High level of proficiency.
