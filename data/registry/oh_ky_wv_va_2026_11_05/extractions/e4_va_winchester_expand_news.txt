New Administrators, Expanded Language Program, and School Construction Vote Ahead for WPS By newsdesk@thewinchestergazette.com on August 10, 2026 Winchester, Virginia — Winchester Public Schools is preparing to welcome students back for the 2026-27 school year with new administrators, an expanded language program, a planned elementary school renovation, and a bond referendum expected to appear on the November 2026 ballot. The division announced the updates in a Facebook post on Monday that included a video message from Superintendent Dr. Jason Van Heukelum. “We’re so excited to welcome all of our students and teachers and staff back to the Winchester Public Schools for a start of another great year here in Winchester,” the division wrote in the post, adding that a slate of changes made over the summer will roll out alongside the new school year. New school leadership Van Heukelum said the division has several new principals and assistant principals taking over school leadership roles this year. Many of them, he said, previously worked in Winchester Public Schools as teachers, and some are graduates of Handley High School. “Now they’re coming full circle, having made a career here in Winchester and now will be leading many of our schools,” Van Heukelum said. Dual language expansion John Kerr Elementary School will launch a new dual-language Spanish immersion program this year. Van Heukelum said the addition means every elementary school in the division now offers Spanish dual immersion. “Completing the full circle and making sure that every student in Winchester has access to Spanish language dual immersion in every single one of our elementary schools,” he said. Garland R. Quarles Elementary renovation The division is also planning a renovation of the Garland R. Quarles Elementary School campus, a project Van Heukelum said is still a couple of years away. He said the division has spent the last six months working with architects and a steering committee to develop a vision for the site. A central goal of the renovation is to bring fifth graders back into elementary schools across the city, restoring a full kindergarten-through-fifth-grade model. Fifth grade was moved to the middle school campus more than a decade ago as what Van Heukelum described as a temporary measure. “We want to keep our kids as young as possible, as long as possible,” he said, adding that the shift of 10-year-old fifth graders to middle school “was never intended to be permanent.” School construction referendum Van Heukelum pointed to new Virginia legislation allowing localities to levy a 1% sales tax dedicated to school construction as a possible funding option for Winchester and other Virginia communities. He said the division has identified more than $130 million in capital needs over the next 10 years, including the Garland R. Quarles renovation as well as routine maintenance such as HVAC systems, roof repairs and athletic field turf across the district. Van Heukelum said more information will be released about a bond referendum expected to go before voters in November 2026. “Be looking for more information about the bond that will be coming out and will be on the ballot in November 2026,” he said.
