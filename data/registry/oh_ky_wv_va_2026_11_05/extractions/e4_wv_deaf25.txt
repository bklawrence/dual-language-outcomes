THE WEST VIRGINIA 2025 SCHOOLS FOR THE DEAF AND THE BLIND About Us Welcome to the West Virginia Schools for the Deaf and the Blind (WVSDB)! Since 1870, the WVSDB has been a place where students who are Deaf, Hard of Hearing, Blind, Visually Impaired, or Deafblind are empowered to thrive academically, socially, and emotionally. Located in Romney, WV, we are a state-operated public school serving students from birth through age 23 across all 55 counties. Our comprehensive services include on-campus programs for elementary through high school, residential and day options, and statewide outreach through early intervention and school-age support. Designed for students who live too far to commute daily or who benefit from the additional support of a residential setting, the program extends learning beyond the classroom through the development of life skills, social interaction, emotional health support, and extracurricular engagement. Sports and Physical Activities The West Virginia Schools for the Deaf and the Blind (WVSDB) offers a variety of athletic programs designed to keep students active and healthy. Sports such as goalball, swimming, track and field, e-sports, soccer, and basketball are available to help students develop physical fitness, teamwork, and discipline. Affiliated with the Eastern Athletic Association for the Blind (EAAB) and the Eastern Schools for the Deaf Athletic Association (ESDAA), students travel across the Eastern U.S. to represent the WVSDB in regional and national competitions, fostering sportsmanship, school pride, and lasting connections with peers from other specialized schools. Clubs and Organizations The WVSDB is proud to offer a variety of student-led clubs that encourage creativity, collaboration, and leadership. Students can get involved in organizations such as the American Sign Language (ASL) Club, Robotics Club, Drones Club, Rocketry Club, and Girl Scouts; each providing a fun and supportive environment to explore personal interests and gain valuable life skills. This program is designed for young adults between the ages of 18 and 23 who are Deaf, Hard of Hearing, Blind, Low Vision, or are Deafblind. The CAP program offers flexible pathways, including educational and vocational tracks. Students receive individualized support in career readiness, independent living, and academic development, while gaining real-world experience through job training and community engagement. Educational Pathway Participants in the Educational Pathway may be dually enrolled in a postsecondary institution, either online or locally, or planning to enroll the following year. vocational pathway Participants in the Vocational Pathway aim to transition directly into the workforce without pursuing a formal education or training program. WVSDB Outreach The WVSDB Outreach is proud to extend its mission beyond our campus through a comprehensive Outreach Program that serves students, families, and educators across the state. Through the WVSDB Outreach, we provide support for children from birth to age 21 who are Deaf, Hard of Hearing, Blind, Low Vision, or Deafblind, regardless of their school placement. Our Outreach Coordinators work with local schools to provide direct instruction, professional development, consultation, and resources. Low-incidence services include audiology, assistive technology, early intervention, literacy, psychological support, transition planning, the Lending Library, and statewide training. These services support students, their families, and educators across West Virginia. Training & Professional Development Workshops and technical assistance for general educators, speech-language pathologists, special education teams, and administrators Direct Services for Students WVSDB Outreach may provide direct services in a student’s home county on a case-by- case basis.
