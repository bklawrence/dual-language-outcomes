Jefferson County Public Schools (JCPS) demonstrated steady academic progress during the 2025–26 school year, marked by reading gains across all school levels, higher postsecondary rates, higher graduation rates, and a significant reduction in the district’s lowest-rated schools, according to Kentucky Summative Assessment (KSA) data. The data released Thursday highlight a continued drop in schools receiving "Red" status—the state’s lowest performance tier—falling to 33 schools this year, down from 41 last year and 54 in 2023–24 (20% and 39% reduction, respectively). "We are encouraged by our reading gains and the steady drop in our lowest-performing schools," said JCPS Superintendent Dr. Brian Yearwood. "At the same time, overall proficiency remains below district aspirations. Our data clearly show where we must confront persistent achievement gaps with urgency. We are responding with stronger instruction, targeted coaching, and direct resources where our students need them most." Key Assessment Highlights Reading Growth: Proficiency improved across all school levels: elementary reached 38% (up from 36%), middle school rose to 36% (up from 35%), and high school increased to 35% (up from 34%). High School Success: Postsecondary readiness climbed to 89.3, while the district graduation rate reached 89.4%. English Learner (EL) Progress: The district earned "Blue" ratings (the state's highest level) in elementary and middle schools, and a "Green" rating in high schools. Mathematics Need: Math proficiency remained relatively stable across grades at 32% for elementary, 29% for middle, and 27% for high school, highlighting an area requiring accelerated improvement and district focus. Federal Accountability Designations Federal designations show both progress in turning around underperforming schools and areas requiring more intensive support. Comprehensive Support and Improvement (CSI): A total of 38 JCPS schools were identified for CSI status in 2025–26, down from 41 in 2024–25. Following the closure of King Elementary, 37 CSI schools currently remain. Eight schools successfully exited CSI status this year, while six schools were newly identified for CSI. Targeted Support and Improvement (TSI) & Additional Targeted Support and Improvement (ATSI): Twenty-two schools were designated for TSI (up from 9), while 16 schools were identified for ATSI (down from 18). Of the 38 total schools identified for TSI and ATSI, 32 were designated due to performance outcomes among students with disabilities. JCPS will use these federal designations to directly target resources and strengthen academic interventions for student groups with persistent achievement gaps. Customized Planning: Conducting needs assessments and Comprehensive School Improvement Plan (CSIP) reviews to establish specific priorities, particularly for newly identified CSI schools and schools in their third year of ATSI designation. Differentiated Support: Delivering targeted academic coaching, specialized professional learning, and instructional resources to CSI, TSI, and ATSI schools, with dedicated focus on strategies for students with disabilities. Instructional Oversight: Utilizing Focused Instructional Walkthroughs to monitor classroom implementation in real time and provide immediate feedback to educators. Core Academic Focus: Continuing the district’s Every Student Reads initiative, strengthening Tier 1 mathematics instruction and interventions, and deepening family engagement partnerships. Continuous Monitoring: Executing CSIP Implementation & Impact cycles to routinely examine student achievement data against instructional strategies and adjust district support as needed. While overall district accountability ratings remain in the “Orange” performance tier across elementary, middle, and high school levels, key indicators show growth toward reaching “Yellow” status and closing achievement gaps. “These results show clear momentum, but test scores are just one measure of our growth,” Yearwood said. “We’re celebrating our reading gains and improved school ratings while confronting our remaining challenges with urgency. Buses are prepped, cafeterias are stocked, and classrooms are ready: Jefferson County Public Schools (JCPS) kicks off the new school year on Thursday, August 6, pairing familiar daily routines with fresh district upgrades. Transportation JCPS begins the new school year with approximately 700 bus drivers in place. All 640 bus routes will be covered, including coverage for anticipated driver absences. This year, we will provide transportation services to about 48,000 students. Driver preparation is front and center heading into the new school year. Alongside a brand-new routing system, the district added two additional paid practice days so drivers can master their routes. Drivers are actively refining schedules with compound supervisors and will complete three full practice "scrimmages" before doors open on August 6. Finding Bus Information: JCPS families can access their child's bus information two ways this year. As in previous years, families can continue relying on Bus Teller, found via the JCPS Transportation Portal at jcps.me/transportation. Bus Teller allows parents and guardians to easily view their child's bus number, bus stop location, and estimated pick-up and drop-off times but does not provide real-time tracking or live estimated arrival time information. My Ride K-12: A second tool to track their student's school bus, which is new for the 2026–27 school year is the My Ride K-12 app. This new tool allows guardians to view their child's real-time bus location and view updates directly on their phones such as sub bus numbers. For full details and setup instructions, visit: jefferson.kyschools.us/article/2895007 or go to JCPSfirstday.com and look for the MyRideK-12 button. Stay Informed: Families should continue to check My Ride K-12 or Bus Teller up to the first morning of school as routes are subject to change. Get Help: In addition to being able to call your school, the 485-RIDE bus hotline is open Monday through Friday through the initial days of school, with JCPS staff available to answer transportation questions. Transportation Verification Form: The June 8 deadline for guaranteed opening-day bus service has passed. Transportation requests to add new stop locations are currently paused through August 14 while drivers familiarize themselves with and practice their routes. New stop assignments will begin processing on August 17. Eligible families who have not yet completed the Transportation Verification Form may still submit a request on the Transportation Portal to receive transportation services in the next available route update. To ensure as many students as possible are accounted for, school teams are continuing direct outreach to registered families who haven't completed their verification forms. Technology on Buses: Every JCPS route-bus is equipped with real-time video streaming, enabling transportation officials to monitor onboard safety and respond immediately to any reported incidents or accidents. In addition, drivers will use mounted tablets for turn-by-turn route navigation. Practice Patience: Getting back into the school routine takes time and teamwork. JCPS asks parents and drivers for grace and extra caution on the roads during the first few weeks of school as bus drivers and families navigate new pickup times, traffic flows and daily drop-off procedures. Minor delays are expected during the first few weeks of school. While bus arrival time can be tracked in the My Ride K-12 app, we’re asking families to arrive about 20 minutes early to their stop the first few days to help buses run on time. Afternoon Kindergarten Bus Stops: Please remember that a guardian needs to be present at the bus stop in the afternoon in order for a driver to release a kindergarten student. Similar to the morning timing, if guardians are at the bus stop about 20 minutes before their scheduled time the first few days it will help buses run on time in the afternoon. After the first few days, checking MyRideK-12 will allow guardians to gauge typical arrival times. The assessment, approved by the Board on October 14, 2025, evaluated Jefferson County Public Schools’ (JCPS) fiscal administration and related governance activities that occurred during fiscal years (FY) 2022 through 2025. The objectives of the assessment were to: Identify systemic and situational factors contributing to the District’s budget shortfall. Assess the effectiveness of fiscal administration practices. Analyze roles, responsibilities, and oversight exercised by District leadership and the Board in financial governance. Benchmark JCPS’s practices against comparable peer school districts. Develop actionable recommendations to strengthen financial management and sustainability. Assess long-term solvency risks and mitigation strategies. The report identified 13 specific findings, with eight categorized as “High Priority,” requiring immediate attention. Primary themes include: Assigned monitoring and oversight: A lack of standardized framework for financial monitoring across the Central Office departments has led to inconsistent budget management and weakened accountability. Budget development and strategic alignment: Budget processes often relied on “rolling forward” prior year allocations rather than aligning spending with Board and administrative strategic priorities. Return-on-Investment (ROI) analysis for programs, projects, and initiatives has not been consistently integrated into budgeting decisions. Reliance on non-recurring funds: The District historically used temporary or nonrecurring funding sources, such as ESSER and contingency funds, to expand staffing and programs without establishing long-term sustainability plans. Budgetary decision-making authority and governance: Financial Services’ authority was limited in practice, with major budgetary decisions often made without a formal process for collective senior leadership review. Plante Moran proposed several high-priority recommendations for JCPS to consider, including: Updating Board policies: Updates should clearly distinguish the Superintendent's daily administration role from the Chief Financial Officer’s (CFO) budgetary control role to ensure proper checks and balances. Developing a multi-year strategic financial plan: This 3-5 year plan should translate the District’s strategic goals and Board priorities into measurable spending commitments. Formalizing budget ownership: Designate responsible budget managers for every Central Office department/division and develop written guidelines that outline budget development and monitoring expectations for all budget managers. “While this assessment highlights challenges in our past fiscal practices, it also provides us with an invaluable blueprint for our future,” said Superintendent Dr. Brian Yearwood. “We requested this independent review because our students and taxpayers deserve transparency. We are not just acknowledging these findings; we are using them to reshape how JCPS operates.” To ensure long-term fiscal responsibility, JCPS will implement the report’s recommendations, focusing on modernizing financial forecasting models and resolving identified operational deficiencies. LOUISVILLE, Ky. - In his first State of the District address, Jefferson County Public Schools (JCPS) Superintendent Dr. Brian Yearwood outlined his strategic vision for the future of the District, centered on financial stability and a renewed commitment to academic growth for the more than 94,000 students across JCPS. In a room filled with educators, community partners and students, Dr. Yearwood characterized the current state of JCPS as “resilience in action,” moving past historic financial uncertainty and toward his vision of a “destination school district.” The District’s aggressive response to a $188 million budget deficit was a cornerstone of his address. After eight months of listening sessions and 60 community meetings, the Superintendent detailed the comprehensive stabilization plan that has already identified about $132 million in savings. “Financial stability protects classrooms. It protects educators. It protects opportunity,” Dr. Yearwood said, emphasizing that these cuts were made to stop the bleed and protect the student experience. Population shifts and the future of our facilities The superintendent addressed the stark difference between the District’s current facilities and shifting student populations, noting 80% of JCPS buildings are more than 50 years old. He said the deferred maintenance backlog is estimated at $2.5 billion. Meanwhile, the student population in eastern portions of Jefferson County is growing, while the population in western portions of the county is declining. The District aims to align facilities with the realities of our community to ensure “educational equity for every student” through balanced enrollment and sustainable learning environments. Every Student, Every Year: a new academic promise Prioritizing his focus on academics, Dr. Yearwood introduced a new District-wide commitment to Every Student, Every Year. This promise ensures that every student achieves at least one full year of academic growth for every year they attend a JCPS school. This work is anchored in three commitments: Safety and Belonging: Ensuring students feel seen and valued. Academic Excellence and Growth: Supporting every student to reach their full potential. Care and Support: Educating the whole child through emotional and social support. Innovation through AI To prepare students for a changing world, Dr. Yearwood announced the completion of a migration to Google Workspace, integrating Gemini (Google’s advanced AI) into classrooms in a secure, enterprise-level environment. The initiative aims to use technology to remove barriers and empower teachers. In closing, Dr. Yearwood quoted Frederick Douglas, saying, “If there is no struggle, there is no progress.” The Superintendent then called on the community to stand with the District as it transitions from stability to momentum. “We are bigger than the deficit,” Dr. Yearwood concluded. “We are choosing progress over comfort, because this is not about a deficit.
