What is the purpose of this Booklet? 
 
The purpose of this booklet is to inform parents/guardians 
of students who are new to the FWISD and who speak or 
hear a language other than English in their home of the: 
 
• Bilingual and ESL programs offered by the FWISD for 
emergent bilingual (EB) students, 
 
• program goals and expectations to meet the affective, 
linguistic, and cognitive needs of EB students; and  
• assessment information and criteria for reclassification 
and program exit. 
 
The Student Placement Center (SPC) staff or the school 
will review content of this booklet with each parent/
guardian and provide them with a copy. Parents/guardians 
are encouraged to call SPC/school with any questions.

What are the goals of the Emergent Bilingual 
Programs? 
 
The goals of the Emergent Bilingual Programs are: 
 
• All students who enter the FWISD in grades PK-5 
placed in a DLI program reach full proficiency in both 
English and Spanish within five to seven years, pass all 
state-required academic tests, and develop sociocultural 
competence. 
 
• All students who enter the FWISD in grades PK-8 
placed in an ESL program reach full proficiency in 
English within five to seven years and pass all state-
required academic tests. 
 
• All students who enter FWISD in grade 9 or above 
placed in an ESL program meet graduation 
requirements in four years, including passing all state-
required academic tests. 
 
The Emergent Bilingual Programs envisions emergent 
bilingual students achieving high levels of language 
proficiency and content knowledge so that they will be 
prepared for success in college, careers, and community 
leadership. 
 
FWISD Programs for Emergent Bilingual Students

Dual Language Immersion (DLI) 
 
What is the Dual Language Immersion program?  
Dual Language Immersion is an educational approach that 
promotes biliteracy for all students. Students who speak a 
language other than English at home are more likely to 
underperform in key subjects like reading and writing and 
less likely than their peers to graduate from high school and 
enroll in college. 
 
However, parents themselves can advocate for an approach 
that may well empower their multilingual children. We 
believe the answer lies in FWISD's Dual Language 
Immersion program. 
 
Dual Language Immersion education holds the promise of 
giving students access to key 21st century skills. Some of 
the benefits include: 
• High Academic and Linguistic Proficiency in English 
and Spanish 
• Bilingualism and Biliteracy 
• High Earning Potential as Career Professionals 
• Increased Creativity and Problem-Solving 
• A foundation for building cross-cultured relationships 
• Sociocultural Competence 
 
Additionally, Dual Language Immersion education 
provides more opportunities for EB students to reach 
higher levels of academic achievement along with more 
positive motivation and a sense of identity.  
 
Families, businesses, and policy-makers increasingly place 
language, communication skills, and sociocultural 
competence among the desired outcomes of education.

Dual Language Immersion 
 
How are Spanish-speaking EB students enrolled in the 
Dual Language Immersion program? 
• Students in PK-5 grade who are Spanish-speaking and 
identified as emergent bilingual are recommended for 
placement in the FWISD DLI program.  
• Recommended placement is determined by the results 
of an initial language proficiency test.  
• Parents will be provided with information regarding the 
elementary school near their home that offers the DLI 
program (OW or TW).  
• Parents must approve their child's placement in the DLI 
program in writing. 
• Students who do not qualify for the Dual Language 
Immersion program based on language proficiency may 
apply to participate through the Two-Way Dual 
Language application lottery. 
 
What is the difference between the OW and the TW DLI 
models?  
One-Way (OW) 
• The FWISD One-Way Dual Language Immersion 
model consists of students that are predominantly 
Spanish speaking emergent bilingual and instructs in 
English and Spanish.  
 
Two-Way (TW) 
• The FWISD Two-Way Dual Language Immersion 
model consists of native Spanish speakers and native 
English speakers and instructs both groups in English 
and Spanish. 
 
During the 2022-2023 school year, FWISD implemented 
the DLI One-Way model in 62 elementary campuses and 
the DLI Two-Way model in 13 campuses (3 campuses have 
a combination of both programs).

Dual Language Immersion 
 
What methods of instruction will be used to teach in the 
Dual Language Immersion program?  
All DLI models will provide students with the following 
non-negotiables: 
• The DLI will be provided up to 5th grade. 
• Both Spanish and English will be used for instruction 
with no more than 50% in English each year. 
• The language of instruction will be clearly separated by 
content area.  
 
Students in the DLI program are instructed using the same 
curriculum as general education students. The only 
difference is that concepts are taught in two languages. 
Integrated into each of the core subjects on a daily basis are 
other types of dual language enrichment activities designed 
to build academic concepts and vocabulary, and the ability 
to speak, read, and write in both languages. 
 
How long will your child participate in the DLI program 
once placed? 
Until the 5th grade. 
 
Who will teach in the Dual Language Immersion 
program? 
In DLI program models, instruction is provided by 
bilingual-certified and/or ESL-certified teachers. Bilingual-
certified teachers and ESL-certified teachers are specially 
trained to provide instruction to EB students that aligns to 
the individual strengths and needs of each student. 
Bilingual-certified and ESL-certified teachers work to 
tailor instruction in the academic areas to ensure transfer of 
concepts and skills from Spanish to English and English to 
Spanish.

Dual Language Immersion 
 
What is the language of instruction of Dual Language 
Immersion?  
Students in the DLI daily receive instruction in both 
English and Spanish on a daily basis. 
• PK-K receive 80% Spanish and 20% English.  
• Grades1-2, receive 60% Spanish and 40% English.  
• Grades 3-5 receive 50% Spanish and 50% English. 
 
How does the Dual Language Immersion program help 
students with special needs? 
All FWISD programs operate within District guidelines to 
help identify and support students with special needs. An 
LPAC member is notified and attends all Admission/
Review and Dismissal (ARD) meetings for each student 
with special needs. All available resources are utilized to 
assist such students. 
 
Special Education and ESL teachers collaborate to support 
these students.

ESL (Elementary) 
 
What is the English as a Second Language (ESL) 
program? 
The ESL program is designed to serve students of all 
language backgrounds. The program serves students at all 
levels of English proficiency in grades PK–5. The goal of 
the ESL program is to ensure emergent bilingual students 
become proficient in listening, speaking, reading and 
writing in English using second language acquisition (SLA) 
methods. 
 
What methods of instruction will be used to teach in the 
ESL program? 
The teachers in the ESL program will use SLA methods 
which will teach students to understand, speak, read, and 
write in English while acquiring grade level content 
knowledge and skills. The curriculum for instruction is the 
same used in general education classrooms.  
 
What are the instructional goals of the ESL program? 
The goal is to ensure the student’s academic success in all 
subject areas at his/her grade level so that he/she may exit 
the language support program by the fifth year. 
 
What is the language of instruction for ESL? 
The language of instruction is English. 
 
Who will teach in the ESL program? 
Teachers who are ESL-certified.  
 
How will the ESL program meet the educational strengths 
and needs for your child ? 
By providing instruction based upon initial assessment 
results and adjusting instructional language support as 
student needs change.

ESL (Elementary) 
 
How will the ESL program help your child learn English, 
yet meet academic achievement standards for grade 
promotion? 
Schools designate a highly qualified teacher to teach 
English as a second language; the content area teacher is 
expected to be certified in ESL instruction. This allows for 
both the language and the academic learning to be 
accelerated. The campus Language Proficiency Assessment 
Committee (LPAC) oversees each student’s progress to 
ensure overall academic achievement. 
 
How long will your child stay in the ESL program? 
Students may exit from ESL programs when they pass the 
state-required academic English test and score “fluent” on 
an English language proficiency test. 
 
How does the ESL program help students with special 
needs? 
All FWISD programs operate within District guidelines to 
help identify and support students with special needs. An 
LPAC member is notified and attends all Admission/
Review and Dismissal (ARD) meetings for each student 
with special needs. All available resources are utilized to 
assist such students. 
 
Special Education and ESL teachers collaborate to support 
these students.

Transition ESL 
 
What is the Transition ESL program? 
This program is designed for students who are assessed to be 
nearly proficient in English but require moderate assistance in 
reading, writing, and/or oral language to be academically 
successful. In some schools, students at the Transition ESL/
English level are placed in separate classes (one or two classes a 
day); in other schools, students are placed in regular English/
reading classes and provided specialized help.  
 
What methods of instruction will be used to teach your child in 
the Transition ESL/English program? 
Transition ESL/English programs are generally for the advanced
-level ESL student, the methods used will mirror the methods 
used in programs for fluent-English speakers, with some 
accommodations: (1) there will be added linguistic and 
vocabulary instruction, (2) there will be greater interaction 
between student and teacher about reading and writing, and (3) 
there will be added time for completing work if needed.  
 
What are the instructional goals of the Transition ESL/English 
program? 
The goal is to ensure students develop the academic language 
skills needed to succeed in school and pass the tests required by 
the state for promotion and graduation from high school. 
 
What is the language of ESL/English program instruction? 
The language of instruction is English. 
 
Who will teach in the Transition ESL/English program? 
Teachers who are certified in English or reading (or both) and 
who are certified or trained in methods of teaching English and 
other subject-area classes to students who are learning English as 
a second language. 
 
How will the Transition ESL/English program meet the 
educational strengths and needs for your child? 
Transition ESL/English is comprised of two language arts

Newcomer ESL 
 
What is the Newcomer program? 
The program is designed for recent-arrival students in 
grades 6-12 who do not speak English. The program 
provides basic communicative and academic skills. In the 
event the program is not offered at your child's home 
school, transportation will be provided. 
 
What methods of instruction will be used to teach in the 
Newcomer program? 
The teachers focus on the development of English language 
skills and content-area knowledge. Students are taught 
more advanced English language skills. Teachers 
consistently provide support as students move through 
increasingly more rigorous concepts. 
 
What are the instructional goals of the Newcomer 
program?  
The overall goal is to foster English language development 
in all  subject areas and to mainstream the student to ESL 
after the second year or as soon as the student reaches the 
advanced level of language proficiency. In secondary, the 
goal is to help students continue developing the social and 
academic English needed for success in school. 
 
What is the language of instruction in the Newcomer 
Program? 
The language of instruction is English. 
 
Who will teach in the Newcomer Program? 
Teachers who are certified in the subject areas they teach 
and who are certified and/or trained in methods of teaching 
English to students who are learning English as a second 
language will provide instruction.  
 
How will the Newcomer program meet the educational 
strengths and needs for your child?  
Teachers will implement a strong content-based curriculum 
customized to each level of language proficiency and 
academic level of the students.

Newcomer ESL 
 
How will the Newcomer program help your child learn 
English, yet meet academic achievement standards for 
grade promotion or graduation? 
Grade level instruction, specialized texts and materials are 
provided. In elementary, an additional ESL teacher pushes 
in to provide additional language and academic support at 
least 3-4 times per week. In middle school, the classes will 
provide skills and concepts for grade promotion. In high 
school, credit toward graduation is earned in ESL, speech, 
social studies, math, and science. All courses teach skills 
and concepts that will prepare students to move into regular 
classes and pass the state-required academic tests. The 
campus Language Proficiency Assessment Committee 
(LPAC) oversees each student’s progress to ensure overall 
academic achievement. 
 
How long will your child stay in the Newcomer program? 
Students generally remain in the program 1 to 2 years and 
then are transitioned to ESL or the regular English 
program, depending upon achievement level. 
 
What is the expected rate of graduation from secondary 
school? 
All students are expected to graduate from high school in 
four years after entering grade 9. Students may find it 
necessary to take advantage of special opportunities, 
including tutoring and summer school, in order to reach 
this goal. 
 
How does the Newcomer program, help students with 
special needs?

LPAC member is notified and attends all Admission 
Review and Dismissal (ARD) meetings for each student 
with special needs. All available resources are utilized to 
assist these students. Special Education and ESL teachers 
collaborate to support these students.

International Newcomer Academy 
 
What is the International Newcomer Academy (INA)? 
The International Newcomer Academy is designed for 
recent arrival students in grades 6-9 who have been 
identified through assessment as non-English speaking. 
Transportation will be provided. 9th grade students must be 
age 15 or younger on September 1. 
 
What methods of instruction will be used to teach at INA? 
The methods used by teachers at INA are those which will 
help students understand, speak, read, and write basic 
English while they are learning concepts and skills in 
subjects such as math, social studies, and science.  
 
What are the instructional goals of the program at INA? 
The goal is to help develop the English language skills and 
content-area knowledge needed to successfully transition to 
a Newcomer program. 
 
What is the language of instruction at INA? 
The language of instruction is English. 
 
Who will teach your child at INA? 
Teachers who are certified in the subject areas they teach 
and who are certified or trained in methods of teaching 
English to students who are learning English as a second 
language will provide instruction.  
 
How will the program at INA meet the educational 
strengths and needs for your child? 
The program is focused solely on the needs of recent 
arrival students with emphasis on English-language 
development. Additionally, students who have special 
abilities, needs, and interest (i.e. are at an advanced level in 
math; need to learn basic literacy skills; have a particular 
talent in art, music, sports; etc.) are placed in special 
classes or groups for specialized instruction.

International Newcomer Academy 
 
How will the program at INA help your child learn 
English, yet meet academic achievement standards for 
grade promotion or graduation? 
The program is taught by teachers who accelerate the 
learning of English by providing intensive ESL classes and 
by focusing on both the language and the content of other 
courses so the student can learn both at the same time. 
Classes count toward grade promotion (or graduation if 
high school). Additionally, routine assessments are 
administered throughout the year to ensure growth and 
inform end-of-year decisions. The Language Proficiency 
Assessment Committee (LPAC) oversees each student’s 
progress to ensure overall academic achievement. 
 
How long will your child stay at INA? 
Most students remain in the program for two to four 
semesters. The two-semester period may be completed in 
May or in December, depending upon date of enrollment. 
When students complete INA, they go to a Newcomer or 
Success program (if applicable).  
 
What is the expected rate of graduation from secondary 
school? 
All students are expected to graduate from high school in 
four years after entering grade 9. Students may find it 
necessary to take advantage of special opportunities, 
including tutoring and summer school, in order to reach this 
goal.

Success 
 
What is Success?  
Success High School is designed for overage 9th-12th 
grade, ages 16-21 students. Success provides all courses 
needed for high school graduation. It also has a quarterly 
plan which allows students to earn credits in half the time 
required at other high schools. The “shortened” credit plan 
at Success is ideal for older students who do not have as 
much time to complete high school. 
 
What methods of instruction will be used to teach at 
Success?  The methods used at Success are those which 
help students expand their command of English while 
focusing on more sophisticated tasks and more mature 
subject matter. Students are taught in a class setting, in 
small groups, study skills guided and independent learning, 
and self-assessment provide the necessary support for the 
older student to reach personal education goals in an 
accelerated timeframe. 
 
What are the instructional goals of the program at 
Success? The goal is to teach students the English language 
and subject-area skills/knowledge needed to earn a diploma 
and enroll in higher education programs and/or to secure 
employment. 
 
What is the language of instruction at Success? 
The language of instruction is English. 
 
Who will teach your child at Success? Teachers who are 
certified in the subject areas they teach and who are 
certified or trained in methods of teaching English to 
students who are learning English as a second language 
will provide the instruction. 
 
How will the program at Success meet the educational 
strengths and needs for your child? Success will meet the 
educational strengths and needs of the students by 
providing (1) varying levels of English courses, (2) a range  
of academic and elective courses, (3) a selection of

Required Testing 
 
Students in DLI/ESL programs are required to take various 
achievement tests each school year. All emergent bilingual 
students take a test to assess their growth in English 
proficiency. This test is the Texas English Language 
Proficiency Assessment System (TELPAS). 
Students also take the State of Texas Assessments of 
Academic Readiness (STAAR) tests, as determined by 
program and/or grade level, to assess academic 
achievement level in subjects such as math, science, social 
studies, and language arts. Students in grades 9-12 must 
pass STAAR End-of-Course (EOC) tests. 
 
Students are assessed in both languages when applicable 
and in the language they can demonstrate knowledge when 
appropriate.

Emergent Bilingual Reclassification Criteria 
 
At the end of the school year, the LPAC will recommend 
reclassification to exit from DLI/ESL program or continue 
participation, if in a DLI program. The student must meet 
the criteria below: 
• 
Score Advanced High in each domain (listening, 
speaking, reading and writing) of TELPAS and; 
• 
Perform satisfactory on the State Standardized 
English Reading Assessment and;  
• 
A completed subjective teacher evaluation of 
the student’s readiness for reclassification. 
Note: 1. Students who received designated supports may 
not be considered for reclassification 
2. Parental approval for exit or continuation in 
program beyond reclassification must be 
obtained. 
  
Summer School for EB Students 
 
FWISD must provide summer school programs for EB 
students entering grades Kinder and 1st. 
 
Instruction focuses on language development and 
knowledge and skills to prepare them to be successful in 
grades Kinder and 1st.  
 
Enrollment is optional and parents must approve students 
to participate. If you are interested please complete a 
separate interest form. 
 
The Multilingual Education Department also provides 
summer school enrichment programs for EB students in 
grades 2-5, 6-8, and 9-12. Information about all programs 
are available at the campus or at the SPC in the Spring of 
each year.
