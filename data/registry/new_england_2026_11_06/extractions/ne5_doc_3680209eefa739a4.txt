Dual Language Program Information The Worcester Public Schools (WPS) offers Spanish-English dual language programs from preschool through high school. Dual language students learn content in both English and Spanish in integrated groups of students with diverse language backgrounds. The long-term goals of the program are academic achievement, bilingualism and biliteracy, and sociocultural competence. Students can be native English or Spanish speakers. If you have questions about the district’s dual language programs, please contact the Office of Multilingual Education at 508-799-3623. Worcester Dual Language Magnet Elementary (formerly Chandler Magnet) has a Two-Way Dual Language program from preschool through grade 6. The school is open to students citywide. There is a lottery admission process. Visit the Dual Language Program Registration page for full details and deadlines. Woodland Academy has a Dual Language program for kindergarten through grade 5. It is a neighborhood-admission school. (It is not open to students citywide.) Burncoat Middle and Burncoat High: Students who progress through the Two-Way Dual Language program in elementary school and would like to continue taking Dual Language classes can do so at Burncoat Middle School (grades 7-8) and Burncoat High School (grades 9-12). What Are Dual Language Programs? WPS currently has a two-way dual language program. Instruction and materials are provided in English and Spanish. Students make connections between languages and develop cross-cultural competency. Instruction is designed so students can access grade-level content and develop English and Spanish proficiency, and work toward the State Seal of Biliteracy. Learn more about the Massachusetts State Seal of Biliteracy. For students who speak English, Spanish, and/or different home languages. Integrates English Learners, former English Learners and native English speakers in the same class. Students begin in PreK and Kindergarten by learning 90% of content in Spanish and 10% of content in English, and transition each year to an even balance between the languages by third grade. They develop a strong positive bilingual identity and celebrate their cultures. Research has shown that “...children must continue developing thinking skills in their first language non-stop until at least age 12.” (Thomas & Collier, 2012, p. 49-50), and that developing language and reading and writing in Spanish will strengthen and transfer to greater success in English. Students who develop bilingualism and reading and writing in both languages have more success in entering a 4-year college and getting a professional job. The program prepares them to achieve the Massachusetts Seal of Biliteracy when graduating, a prestigious recognition of bilingualism they can show to colleges and employers. People who are proficiently bilingual show enhanced performance in a variety of cognitive tasks than are monolinguals. Students who have developed bilingualism, and are reading and writing in two or more languages, have more success in entering a 4-year college and getting a professional job after graduating. The program sets them up to achieve the Massachusetts Seal of Biliteracy when graduating high school, a prestigious recognition of their bilingualism that they can show to colleges and employers. Students in the Dual Language Program grow an appreciation for diversity and ability to respect and communicate in cross-cultural settings. The Dual Language Program is designed to build on students’ language abilities, which leads to transferring skills across both languages. Students who are classified as English Learners and are in Dual Language receive the same ESL services as students in English-only programs, in addition to the benefits of a Dual Language teacher who is skilled in developing both their Spanish and English at all moments of the day. Research has demonstrated consistently that English learners who stay in Dual Language programs for at least 5 years have higher English language and English reading test scores than English learners in any other program, including English-only, and that they become advanced beyond grade level in both English and Spanish.
