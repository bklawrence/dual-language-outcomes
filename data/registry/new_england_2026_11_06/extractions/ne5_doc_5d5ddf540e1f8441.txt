Transitional Bilingual Education Transitional Bilingual Education or “TBE” is a program where multilingual learners learn content primarily in the partner language and then transition to learning more of the content in English. English as a Second Language or “ESL” is taught by a licensed teacher in the classroom or in a pull-out model and is adjusted to the level of the student. When students complete a foundational level of proficiency in English, they transition to an inclusive program where they are integrated with English speaking peers. The goal of a TBE program is English acquisition. This program is for students in all grades. Donald J. McKay The Donald J. What languages are available in BPS dual language programs? Are dual language programs the same as two-way immersion programs? Are there any recommendations to enroll my child in a dual language program? Currently, BPS offers dual language programs in Spanish/English in selected elementary schools and in one high school. We also offer Haitian Creole/English programming at the Mattahunt Elementary School. In addition, we also have an American Sign Language (ASL)/English dual language program at the Horace Mann School for the Deaf. BPS subsequently added dual language programs in Vietnamese/English at Âu Cơ Preschool and Mather Elementary School. More recently, BPS will launch a Cabo Verdean Creole/English program the Lila G. Frederick is expected to launch in Fall 2026. Yes, dual language programs as defined in BPS are also known as two-way immersion programs. It is recommended that students enter the dual language programs at K0-K2. The child must be proficient in either English or the target language of the program (for the Haitian Creole, Spanish, and Vietnamese dual language programs). In some cases, a new student who has proficiency in the target language (Haitian Creole, Spanish, or Vietnamese) may be enrolled into a DLP beyond grade 1. Enrollment in the ASL Dual Language program at the Horace Mann School for the Deaf and Hard of Hearing is available for students who are deaf or hard of hearing and does not require proficiency in ASL or English. Consult your local dual language program school for details and enrollment procedures. Sheltered English Immersion or “SEI” is an instructional program for English learners that has two components - sheltered content instruction and ESL instruction. Nearly all of the classroom instruction is in English, with appropriate linguistic supports for students who are learning the language. Teachers use specific techniques to make content learning in Math, Science, and Social Studies accessible to multilingual learners at all levels. English as a Second Language or “ESL” is taught by a licensed teacher in the classroom or in a pull-out model and is adjusted to the level of the student. In Boston, this type of SEI program is delivered in an inclusive setting where multilingual learners are integrated with English speaking peers throughout the school day. This program is for students in all grades. Program Information Purpose of Newcomer Programming The Newcomer Program is designed to support newly arrived multilingual learners and their families by providing a structured, supportive environment that integrates grade-level content instruction with targeted English language development and social-emotional supports. The program emphasizes a culturally responsive approach that addresses students’ academic, linguistic, and social integration needs as they transition into the United States and the Boston Public Schools system. Upon completion of the Newcomer Program, students transition into one of the district’s language development pathways: Sheltered English Immersion (SEI), or Dual Language programs.* Newcomer Programs in Boston Public Schools Grade 6-8, 9-12 Brighton High School Boston International Newcomers Academy (BINcA) Charlestown High School TechBoston Academy The English High School New Mission Quincy Upper *The Newcomer Program is an option for families not a requirement. Families can choose to enroll initially in one of these programs. Program Information Students with Limited or Interrupted Formal Education (SLIFE) are those who are learning English and have had their education interrupted or have had limited formal education before joining the district. SLIFE typically come from households where a language other than English is spoken, have educational gaps from their home country, and are at least two grade levels behind in reading and mathematics. They may have attended school in the U.S., but may have gaps in language and literacy due to ineffective or absent instruction. They are often at risk of leaving school and may require intensive support. In BPS SLIFE students are all newcomer multilingual learners who: Newcomer; Age 9 to 22; Entered a US school after grade 2 or exited the US for 6 months or more; Has no formal schooling or has interrupted formal schooling, that is, two years behind or more than his/her peers, or, has consistent, but limited formal schooling; Functions two or more years below grade level in native language literacy relative to typical peers; OR functions two or more years below expected grade level in numeracy relative to typical peers. The primary goal of the SLIFE Program is to offer newly arrived multilingual students who have experienced limited or interrupted education a nurturing and supportive environment. Within this program, they receive intensive English language instruction, grade-level content instruction with targeted English language development and social-emotional supports. The program emphasizes a culturally responsive approach that addresses students’ academic, linguistic, and support in their native language. Additionally, they benefit from supplementary linguistic, cultural, and academic support aimed at bridging educational gaps resulting from their academic background. The program is also designed to facilitate a smoother transition into the United States and the Boston Public Schools system. Upon completion of the SLIFE Program, students move into various educational paths available at their school setting, including Sheltered English Immersion, Transitional Bilingual, and Dual Language programs.
