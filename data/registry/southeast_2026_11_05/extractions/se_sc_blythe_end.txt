David Ferrara/Staff By David Ferrara dferrara@postandcourier.com Facebook Twitter WhatsApp SMS Email Print Copy article link Save GREENVILLE — Struggling with low student enrollment and finding certified teachers, Blythe Academy is planning to scale down its French language immersion magnet program.The K-5 program, which runs alongside a Spanish immersion program at the school, is proposed to be replaced with a French studies class starting with next year's kindergarten students, according to a letter recently sent to parents."Although both programs have been successful, the French Immersion program has seen significant challenges that have impacted the quality of the program and the prospects for its future," principal Steven Sokohl said in the letter."While all of us — parents, students, Blythe teachers and administrators, and the District administration — are invested in this program and wish nothing more than to see it continue, the unfortunate reality is that it has become unsustainable in the long term." As long as the number of students enrolled can sustain the program, all students currently enrolled will be able to continue until the current kindergarten class exits fifth grade in 2029, Sokohl said.Sokohl cited low enrollment in Blythe Academy's French immersion program as part of the decision. Only about 12% of students at the school are enrolled in the French program, while the vast majority of students are enrolled in Spanish, enrollment data shows. Once students graduate from Blythe Academy's French immersion program after fifth grade, they can choose to continue into the Greenville Middle's French immersion program. However, only two students this year chose to do so, Sokohl said.Sokohl also said the difficulty in recruiting qualified teachers was a significant part of the decision. Since immersion programs require that teachers are certified in both the content area and the immersion language, the district has taken to recruiting from across the country and overseas, as well as starting a program to certify teachers on the job. The district's proposal to the Greenville County School Board would make it easier to hire a French teacher at the school because a French studies teacher would not require a certification in the language, Sokohl said. Even though Phillip has two master's degrees and his K-12 certification in French, Philipp does not have the other certification to teach core subjects.“The issue is getting the people that have the right certifications, for it to qualify for them to have the standard based instruction in the content area, but getting it in both languages,” Greenville ESOL coordinator Tanya Francha told The Post and Courier in April. Cecilia Carpenter, a student at a Greenville high school, said her diverse class of international students at Blythe Academy were provided an opportunity to learn French that they would not have otherwise had. "None of us were super rich and we all came from different backgrounds," Carpenter said. "Blythe was one of the only ways we were able to learn French."The proposal comes amid a statewide teacher shortage but rising enrollment in school language immersion programs across South Carolina. Greenville is one of three districts in South Carolina with a French immersion program. The others are Lexington One and Rock Hill. The French and Spanish immersion programs in Greenville are the longest-running ones in the state, according to data from the S.C.
