A child who arrives with no Spanish sits beside a child who speaks it at home, and each becomes the other's teacher. Our teachers are trained to make instruction understandable from the first day, through gestures, visuals, routines, and hands-on materials, so your child never has to understand every word to understand the lesson.Will my child's English fall behind?This is the question we hear most, and the research is consistent: scholars in well-implemented dual language programs perform as well as or better than their peers in English by the upper elementary grades, and the advantage tends to grow over time. English is never absent from our day; it is taught deliberately and expands each year. In the early grades you may notice a short adjustment period. That is normal, expected, and temporary.We don't speak Spanish at home. Can we still help with homework?You don't need to. Reading with your child in your home language builds the same vocabulary, comprehension, and background knowledge that transfers directly into their second language; that transfer is one of the best-established findings in bilingual education. We send family guides home in both English and Spanish, and our teachers will never ask you to teach content in a language you don't speak. Read with your child, ask questions, be curious. We'll handle the Spanish.Who teaches the Spanish portion of the day?All of our dual language teachers are credentialed, fluent in Spanish, and trained in immersion instruction. Each grade level has a language allocation tailored to its developmental needs.How do you measure progress in two languages?We assess in both languages, on a set schedule, and we share the results with you. We use mCLASS DIBELS 8 for early literacy in English and mCLASS Lectura in Spanish, with benchmark windows at the beginning, middle, and end of the year.
