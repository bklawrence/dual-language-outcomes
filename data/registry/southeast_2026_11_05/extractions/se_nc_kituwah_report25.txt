Library events reached over 1800 individuals this year, and staff distributed more than 1000 books. The Orange Shirt Day Walk was an event that had over 350 attendees. The Cherokee Recreation Program installed new flooring, outdoor signs, photo displays, playground equipment, and dehumidifiers in several gyms this year. Plans for more improvements at the John Crowe Complex are underway for next year. The program served over 600 youth in sports and over 180 participants in senior games. Our program also partnered with Casey Reagan and Smokey Mountain Judo to help build a new Youth Wrestling Program that is impactful. This year, we achieved record product sales, increasing our revenue to over $18,000. Additionally, our staff are implementing a new gym management software, “Gym Master,” which will provide members with online access to all services. The HELP Program—This year, our staff completed more than 2,500 service calls, primarily addressing plumbing concerns and general maintenance for the homes of elderly clients. The program also processed 153 major home repairs, including numerous HVAC installations and accessible walk-in showers. In addition, our team provided lawn care services to 422 clients, with an average of 26 cuts per season, and supported 55 clients with plowing services. The Cherokee Youth Center has reduced its waitlist twice and added 70 children over two years, now serving a record number of attendees. We have also partnered to bring in mental health professionals twice a week for staff and children, and nearly 2,000 community members have participated in our events and activities this year. New Kituwah Academy has upgraded the building this year with new playground equipment, flooring, furniture, cabinets, and an HVAC system in key areas. Staff have also completed over 26 training courses, including weekly language classes for both staff and parents. Kituwah Preservation & Education Program (KPEP) now provides three years of adult language learning, supported by over 20 fluent speakers. This year, we have expanded planning for language certifications and recognitions and will honor second language learners who achieve ATFAL Standard levels 3, 5, or Advanced (8). KPEP has also translated hundreds of community materials, including speeches, books, word lists, and curriculum for all ages. Qualla Boundary Head Start - We have provided vision, hearing, and dental services to all enrolled children. To boost recruitment, we're partnering with EBCI Communications for a recruitment video and other efforts. Our staff and families will gain more cultural knowledge and language training. QBHS turnover dropped by 10% last year—a major achievement.
