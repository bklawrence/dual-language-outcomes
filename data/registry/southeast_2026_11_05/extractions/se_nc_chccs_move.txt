Education CHCCS votes to split Glenwood Elementary's Mandarin magnet program WUNC News | By Liz Schlemmer, Eli Chen Published August 21, 2026 at 2:34 PM EDT Facebook Bluesky LinkedIn Email Eli Chen / WUNC News Glenwood Elementary parent Grace Wu speaks at the podium with her son at a CHCCS board meeting on Aug. 20, 2026. Chapel Hill-Carrboro City Schools' Board of Education took one step Thursday night in a larger plan to rezone students from across the district. District officials say this process is an effort to "rightsize" its schools due to falling enrollment.What did the school board decide on Aug. 20?The board voted to split Glenwood Elementary's STEAM2 Mandarin magnet program, with students in the Dual Language Immersion track moving to Rashkis Elementary. The other half of Glenwood's students in the world language track will likely move to Scroggs Elementary, but with the language track possibly ending. The board will decide what will happen to that track at its next meeting.The school board's decision didn't align with the suggestions from any of the groups of parents who spoke at the board meeting: not with parents asking for the decision to be delayed, not with several Rashkis parents lobbying to receive the world language track, nor with Glenwood advocates who urged the board to keep the Mandarin offerings intact even if the two tracks are split into separate schools.A common plea from parents of all three schools was to slow the process, and the school board did that in part, by delaying a decision on the future of Glenwood's world language track."I've heard a lot of people say they haven't been heard, and I want to say you have been heard, and the outcome might not have been what was desired," said board member Rani Dasi. "But there's been a lot of conversation, a lot of thought into this, and we're in a hard place." The district's Deputy Superintendent for Operations Al Ciarochi has stressed the urgency of these decisions because the school board is on a tight timeline to decide how to rezone students from all the district's schools over the course of this upcoming school year, in time to implement the changes in Fall 2027.Who voted against it? What concerns did they raise?The school board voted on two motions on Aug. 20. The first motion was to split Glenwood's Mandarin magnet program into two schools. Melinda Manning was the only board member to vote against this.Manning said that when the board voted to close Glenwood, she thought the two tracks would be separated, but that she had changed her thinking over the past two months."And I think that would be a big mistake, " Manning said. "To use a barbecue analogy, I think there's a special sauce, and I realize I'm probably on the losing end of this vote, but I think it's the right choice."The second motion was whether to move Glenwood's DLI track to Rashkis Elementary. This motion passed, with Melinda Manning and Rani Dasi voting against it.Then, board member George Griffin suggested that it might not be possible to move the world language track to Scroggs Elementary, which already has a Spanish world language program."This isn't going to be very popular, but I'm not sure it should be a magnet status," Griffin said. "Maybe we should suspend it for a year or two and really look at: do we want to have a magnet program that does Mandarin and also Spanish in the same school, and if so, what does that even really look like?"District officials had previously said they intended to keep any affected programs in the process of closing a school building, even before they ultimately chose in June to close Glenwood Elementary."I'm uncomfortable going back on our previous statements," said board member Meredith Ballew. "And I'm uncomfortable moving forward with a program just because we said we were going to, once we have more information." Eli Chen / WUNC NewsParents, teachers and other stakeholders mainly from Glenwood, Rashkis, and Scroggs Elementary Schools filled up most of the room at the CHCCS board meeting on Aug. 20, 2026. Why is it challenging to move Glenwood's Mandarin world language track to another elementary school as a magnet?Board members relied on the advice of a veteran world language teacher who spoke during the meeting's public comment period and had submitted a letter to the board describing the logistical issues with placing two world language tracks in the same school.Every elementary school in Chapel Hill-Carrboro City Schools has a world language program where students learn either Spanish, French or Mandarin as a specials class, like art or music. At Scroggs, the world language currently offered is Spanish.The district's lead world language teacher Shawna Catlett explained that while students leave their classroom to attend these classes, it serves as their classroom teacher's prep time. She said placing two world languages at one school would affect this protected time for teachers, and would also place an additional constraint on assigning students to classroom teachers.Why were Rashkis and Scroggs chosen as the schools to receive Glenwood students?Board member Barbara Fedders asked district staff to explain this recommendation, which staff and consultants from N.C. State University first presented at the board's meeting on July 16.Ciarochi said these two schools are among a list of underenrolled schools that have the capacity to accept more than a hundred students. Rashkis and Scroggs were recommended because they are located on the outside borders of the school district and along primary highways. Ciarochi explained that relocating a magnet program to those schools would be more efficient for later steps in the redistricting process."For me, the recommendation would be (to) put the program that historically has had the strongest numbers in the school that has the greatest challenges when it comes to redistricting, as far as getting the most kids into it," Ciarochi said. Ultimately, the message from the Rashkis community has been consistent. We are excited to welcome one of the Mandarin programs to our school."Meanwhile, Glenwood parents advocated for the Mandarin tracks to be supported and taught with fidelity. One parent argued the district was making a mistake by splitting one of the district's most popular magnet programs, that's been attracting families and driving enrollment at its school."If you want to grow an orchard, you do not go up to a healthy tree with a chainsaw," said Glenwood parent Kristen Said. "Programmatic growth deserves careful consideration, not a hacksaw and a prayer."Several Glenwood parents simply expressed their disappointment that their school community would soon be split."Small things matter," said Glenwood parent Grace Wu. "The teachers people remember, the friendships, the laugh(ter), the language spoken in the hallways, the children who found belonging there, the families who keep showing up."
