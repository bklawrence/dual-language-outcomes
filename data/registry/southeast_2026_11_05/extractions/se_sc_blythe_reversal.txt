David Ferrara/Staff By David Ferrara dferrara@postandcourier.com Facebook Twitter WhatsApp SMS Email Print Copy article link Save GREENVILLE — After several board members questioned a lack of communication between Blythe Academy school officials and parents, the Greenville County school board voted against a proposal by the school to phase out its French immersion program.The 8-3 vote on Nov. 14 came as the school says it has struggled to find certified teachers for the program and after Blythe parents and community members petitioned school officials to reconsider their decision.Several board members raised questions about why the school did not make its concern with finding French teachers public prior to the proposal. One said that doing so earlier might have brought enough attention to rally the community in finding a suitable teacher or getting the state's blessing to waive the certification requirements. Blythe Principal Steven Sokohl cited the difficulty of recruiting teachers with the proper certifications as a significant reason for scaling down the program, according to an Oct. 20 letter to parents at Blythe Academy.State regulations require that teachers of immersion programs are certified in both the content area — such as math and language arts — and the immersion language, South Carolina Department of Education spokesman Derek Phillips told The Post and Courier. Alternatively, a teacher can be certified in the content area and have earned a passing score on the certification assessment in the language, or vice versa, Phillips said. Blythe Academy has taken to recruiting teachers from across the country and overseas, as well as starting a program to certify teachers on the job, Sokohl said. Sokohl said that the proposal, which would have made the immersion program into a language class, would have made it easier to hire for since the teacher would not need both certifications.Community members had organized a petition to preserve the French program, which had more than 600 signatures as of Nov. 14. Greenville's is one of three districts in South Carolina with a French immersion program. The others are in Lexington One and Rock Hill.Rock Hill Schools began to pilot language-immersion programs in 2003 and the district has had great success, so much so that it opened a dedicated school in 2019, spokeswoman Lindsay Machak told The Post and Courier."Our French immersion is amazing, and we don’t have any trouble finding teachers," Machak said. More than 800 students in K-5 are enrolled in Spanish and French language immersion at Cherry Park Elementary, Machak said. Greenville News Parles-tu français? French immersion program scaling down amid teacher shortage, low enrollment By David Ferrara dferrara@postandcourier.com 'We have a demand for immersion in Spanish that we're not meeting.'Spanish remains the predominant immersion program at Blythe, with about 85 percent of students choosing Spanish from 2021 to 2023, enrollment data obtained by The Post and Courier show. The choice of a majority of Blythe parents to enroll their child in Spanish comes as Hispanic enrollment at the school has steadily risen over the past 16 years, according to enrollment data.Total enrollment at Blythe Academy has seen a slight decline over the years, but the number of Hispanic students enrolled rose from 5 percent in 2007 to 25 percent this year. When asked by the board whether French could be offered at schools other than Blythe, Sokohl said that scaling down the program at Blythe would make room for another Spanish class. "We have a demand for immersion in Spanish that we're not meeting. Thirty-four (students) would be at least one more class that frees up that space. We would prefer to have a more diverse language program," Sokohl said. "It would be nice if we had German, it would be nice if we had French, but we're struggling to find those teachers at the level where we must ensure that we provide it so students can matriculate to college." Not all Hispanic parents are enrolling their kids in Spanish. Abesita Gonnot is the parent of a third grader in Blythe's French program. Gonnot said that even though she is out of zone for Blythe, she chose it specifically because of the program.Gonnot said she found it difficult to find a French school in the area. In 2022, Michelin's French Bilingual School in Greenville, which primarily served its employees but also took public enrollment, closed.Soon after, Florence LaForge decided to fill the void. LaForge, principal and founder of The International School of Greenville, worked at the Michelin school for 10 years before it closed. Since The International School is private, its is not subject to the same certification requirements that make it difficult for immersion programs to find teachers, LaForge said.
