Dual Language Immersion Dual language immersion is not the same as a Spanish immersion program, where all instruction is delivered solely in Spanish. Our Dual Language Immersion (DLI) program provides both native English speakers and native Spanish speakers with academic instruction in two languages from pre-kindergarten through graduation. The DLI program starts in elementary school at Richfield Dual Language School (RDLS) and continues through Richfield Middle School and Richfield High School. It is also possible for students who did not start at RDLS or another Spanish immersion school to enroll in our DLI program at a later grade. They will need to take an assessment to show their Spanish reading and writing skills, and if they pass, they will be added to DLI. Many students do this every year and some of them have never attended school in Spanish before. Benefits of Dual Language Immersion DLI programs foster enhanced awareness of linguistic and cultural diversity. Students learn both languages simultaneously from one another, both socially and academically. Elementary School Middle School High School Dual Language Immersion at Richfield Dual Language School (RDLS) We offer a dual-language Spanish program for students in pre-kindergarten through grade 5. Students become bilingual and biliterate while mastering both Spanish and English. We use research-based best practices to teach languages at a high level while delivering academic content. Spanish is spoken 90% of the instructional day in pre-k, kindergarten and first-grade classrooms. Our highly-qualified and fully bilingual staff have diverse cultural and language backgrounds. No previous background in Spanish or English is required upon a student’s enrollment. Dual Language Immersion at Richfield Middle School Students take two classes per day taught in Spanish: Social Studies and Spanish Language Arts. The remaining five classes per day are taught in English. The DLI social studies course meets all of the state standards and mirrors the RMS social studies requirements. All 6th-grade students study Minnesota history. All 7th-grade students study U.S. history. All 8th-grade students study world geography. The DLI Language Arts course pushes students to analyze multiple genres of text through reading, discussions and higher-level writing prompts. A strong focus is placed on the production of Spanish both in speaking and writing to maintain skills gained at Richfield Dual Language School and propel students forward. DLI fosters a strong and safe learning community where students feel comfortable using their Spanish skills in discussions each day. Students display heightened critical thinking skills, resourcefulness and an excitement for learning. DLI students have access to all other programs and activities available at RMS, like Gifted & Talented, art, band, music, physical education, technology, etc. DLI language arts courses at the middle school and high school are aligned with the American Council of the Teaching of Foreign Languages (ACTFL) standards. The high school course is also aligned with Minnesota state standards for English Language Arts. Dual Language Immersion at Richfield High School DLI students in grades 9 and 10 take Spanish Language Arts and at least one other course in Spanish as part of the DLI program. Additional course offerings in Spanish are available in social studies and math for students in grades 9 and 10. Students receive honors-level credit for courses taken in Spanish. These courses are all rigorous academic courses that build upon students’ Spanish literacy skills gained at Richfield Middle School and earn credit toward graduation. Spanish Language Arts for grades 9 and 10 are aligned to the Minnesota state standards for English Language Arts, meaning students receive ELA credit towards graduation for taking these courses. However, if a student chooses to take English Language Arts as well, the Spanish Language Arts classes count as a world language elective credit. Students in grade 11 are required to take AP Spanish Language and Culture to remain in the DLI program but are highly encouraged to take more than one. Also in grade 11, students will take the AP Spanish Language and Culture assessment for an opportunity to earn a Seal of Biliteracy for their diploma. There are no requirements for DLI in grade 12. However, students are highly encouraged to take AP Spanish Literature, especially if they are considering continuing studies in Spanish at the university level. DLI language arts courses at the middle school and high school are aligned with the American Council of the Teaching of Foreign Languages (ACTFL) standards. The high school course is also aligned with Minnesota state standards for English Language Arts. Students in DLI have special opportunities to attend field trips locally and go on trips abroad, as well as hosting international students.
