Immersion and Multilingual Learners Learning another language opens doors for students. It builds communication skills, helps students connect with different cultures and prepare them for college, careers and life. At Spring Lake Park Schools, students have many ways to build language skills—from Spanish Immersion beginning in preschool to English learner support, world language classes and opportunities to earn bilingual recognition. Language learning opportunities Spanish Immersion (preK-12) offers students an opportunity to become bilingual and biliterate in Spanish and English. Support for English Learners with personalized instruction helps students build English skills while succeeding in their classes. World language classes in Spanish or German for students in grades 7-12. Bilingual seals and certificates recognize students who demonstrate proficiency in more than one language. Learning through Spanish Students learn reading, writing, math, science and other core subjects in Spanish. In kindergarten through grade 2, about 90% of instruction is in Spanish and 10% is in English. Formal English reading instruction begins in grade 2. As students learn and grow, they build strong skills in speaking, listening, reading and writing in both Spanish and English. A school where language comes to life Students learn from caring teachers and staff who are bilingual, and many are multilingual. Bilingual teachers help students hear and use authentic Spanish throughout the school day, creating a rich language-learning experience. Preparing students for the future The Spanish Immersion program continues through middle school and high school, giving students opportunities to strengthen their language skills, earn college credit and graduate bilingual and biliterate. Research shows that students in well-designed immersion programs perform as well as, or better than, their peers academically while gaining the lifelong benefit of speaking more than one language. Students who attend Woodcrest Elementary Spanish Immersion continue their bilingual journey at Westwood Intermediate School in grades 5 and 6. As students grow, they strengthen their skills in speaking, listening, reading and writing in Spanish while continuing to learn the same academic content as their peers. By the end of grade 5, most students communicate confidently in Spanish in everyday and academic settings. To personalize learning, we take time to learn about each student’s language background, experiences and strengths. During enrollment, families complete a Home Language Survey that tells us about the language(s) students use at home and outside of school. We also talk with families to better understand each student’s culture and language experiences. Based on what we learn, some students may complete an English language assessment (or screener) to help us understand their current English skills in reading, writing, speaking and listening. A student may be able to have conversations with friends and follow everyday directions but still need support with the more advanced language used for classroom learning. This information helps us provide the right support so every student can be successful. At Spring Lake Park Schools, we believe all teachers help students build language skills. Throughout the school day, students receiving English Learner services get support to develop the English skills they need to participate and succeed in their classes. Support may happen in a dedicated English Learner class or through instruction and support provided in the classroom with their peers. Students learn English while also learning grade-level content, building confidence and preparing for future success. Students who receive English Learner services take the WIDA ACCESS for ELLs 2.0 assessment each year. This test measures how students are developing their English skills in listening, speaking, reading and writing. The assessment helps teachers understand what each student already knows and what support will help them continue growing. Families receive their child’s ACCESS results in late summer or early fall. Score reports are available in many languages, and your child’s English Learner teacher can help explain the results and answer questions. Throughout the school year, teachers also look at classroom work, conversations and observations to understand each student’s progress and provide the right support. Families will receive updates throughout the year about their child’s growth and learning. Your child’s home language is a strength. Encourage your child to continue speaking, reading and writing in the language they know best. Using their first language helps students build strong communication skills and can support learning English and other languages. Families can support language growth by: Talking with your child about their day. Reading books together in your home language. Sharing stories, traditions and experiences. Encouraging your child to use both their home language and English. Every student’s language journey is different. Students receive English Learner services until they have developed the English skills needed to be successful in their classes. The Minnesota Department of Education sets the guidelines for when students no longer need English Learner services. Students continue receiving support until they meet the required scores on the WIDA ACCESS for ELLs 2.0 assessment in listening, speaking, reading and writing. After a student transitions out of English Learner services, we continue to monitor their progress for two years to make sure they are successful and have the support they need. Students who are not part of Spanish Immersion can begin learning Spanish or German starting in grade 7. In grade 7, students may choose an introductory Spanish or German class. Beginning in grade 8, students can take Spanish 1 or German 1 for high school credit. Students who continue studying the language through high school may have opportunities to earn college credit while building valuable language and cultural skills for college, careers and life. Students who are bilingual can earn special recognition for their language skills through Minnesota’s Bilingual Seals and World Language Proficiency Certificates. These awards recognize students who can speak, listen, read and write in a language other than English. Students who earn a seal or certificate may also be eligible to receive college credit at Minnesota State colleges and universities. Bilingual recognition celebrates the skills students have developed and can provide valuable opportunities as they continue their education and enter the workforce. To earn a seal or certificate, high school students must meet Minnesota’s English Language Arts graduation requirements.
