Beginning in elementary school and continuing through middle and high school, students develop strong academic skills while gaining proficiency in both Spanish and English, preparing them for success in an increasingly connected world. There are three pathways for students to develop bilingualism and biliteracy in Spanish and English. One-Way Spanish Immersion serves English-proficient students through instruction delivered primarily in Spanish. Two-Way Spanish Immersion brings together native English and Spanish speakers in a balanced learning environment where academic content is taught in both languages. Developmental Bilingual Education is designed primarily for students with a Spanish-language background and builds strong literacy skills in Spanish while supporting English language development. Each program begins in kindergarten and provides a pathway for continued language learning through middle and high school. One-Way Spanish Immersion The specialized language immersion program allows English-proficient students to become bilingual and biliterate in Spanish. Students attending the immersion program receive all classroom instruction in Spanish beginning in kindergarten. Through exposure to and practice with the language, students gain understanding and develop language proficiency. English specific skills are introduced beginning in 3rd through 5th grade. Parents of this program agree to read to their child in English for a minimum of 20 minutes a day. Students whose first language is not English must demonstrate proficiency in English prior to enrollment at Sonia Sotomayor. The application process occurs the year before students enter kindergarten. Students in One-Way Spanish Immersion continue their immersion programming in the Spanish immersion 6-12 model. Two-Way Spanish Immersion The goal of Two-Way Spanish Immersion is full bilingualism and biliteracy in both Spanish and English for English and Spanish-speaking children. Two-Way Immersion is a distinctive model of immersion education in which balanced numbers of native English and Spanish speakers learn together to read, write, speak and listen in both languages. All academic content is delivered equally across the two languages to students throughout the school day. Students work at the exact same academic level as traditional classroom students. They learn the same content to the same high expectations. The application process occurs the year before students enter kindergarten. Students in Two-Way Spanish Immersion continue their immersion programming in the Spanish immersion 6-12 model. Developmental Bilingual Education The Developmental Bilingual Education model is designed primarily for students who already have Spanish as part of their language background. English-dominant students may also enroll and are supported in the model. Spanish is the primary language of instruction in the early grades. In Kindergarten and 1st grade, students receive most of their academic instruction in Spanish, while also getting initial English literacy and language development instruction daily. This strong foundation in Spanish supports overall language development and long-term academic success in both English and Spanish as students move to a more balanced approach in grades 2nd through 5th with both Spanish and English homeroom teachers. The application process occurs the year before students enter kindergarten. Students in Developmental Bilingual Education continue their immersion programming in the Spanish immersion 6-12 model. Spanish Immersion Community Network The Sioux Falls School District Spanish Immersion Community Network connects students, families, educators, and administrators across our K–12 program. Designed to foster strong connections across our schools, meetings feature student presentations, school-level highlights, and important district-wide updates regarding our Spanish Immersion program. The Community Network offers a welcoming window into our schools, ensuring our entire district community stays informed, engaged, and united in supporting our students on their journey toward bilingualism and cultural competence.
