R373 4 of 182 Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Comprehensive Education Planning (CEP) is a structured, data-driven process that guides schools in assessing their needs, identifying areas for growth, setting strategic goals and implementing targeted actions to improve student outcomes. Collaboratively developed by the School Leadership Team (SLT) in accordance with the requirements of Chancellor’s Regulation A-655, the CEP meets New York State Education Department (NYSED) requirements under the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) and aligns with NYC Public Schools’ (NYCPS) system-wide priorities. By engaging all stakeholders—school leaders, teachers, families, students and community members—schools can create a data-informed, equity- focused roadmap for student success and continuous improvement. Schools should focus their CEP on the five system-wide priorities below, connecting their planning to the data discussions and conversations with stakeholders held as part of the school’s needs assessment: Priority 1: All students learn to read well Priority 2: All students are physically and emotionally safe Priority 3: All students have a high-quality academic experience Priority 4: All students graduate college and career ready and have a strong plan and pathway to economic security Priority 5: All districts and schools are more inclusive and responsive for parents and families, including having more families choose NYC Public Schools The Education Planning Process The CEP follows an equity-driven, continuous improvement cycle, rooted in the system-wide priorities. This begins with a comprehensive needs assessment of the school’s most current data to identify priority needs and root causes. Next, the SLT establishes specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, and timely— SMART goal(s) aligned to each priority and determines targets to measure progress toward meeting the annual goals. The SLT and instructional leaders define key evidence-based key strategies, professional learning activities and parent, family and community empowerment efforts. They monitor the implementation of those strategies and activities and track progress through measurable targets. Then, the SLT aligns the school-based budget with human and instructional resources to support action plans. DBN: 75R373 School: P.S. These include evidence based instructional strategies that address the educational needs of historically underserved populations and tackle structural inequities. Schools indicate how the plan addresses the needs of Multilingual/English Language Learners (ML/ELL) through development of the school’s Language Allocation Policy (LAP) & Title III Application (if applicable) and the Language Translation and Interpretation (LTI) Plan, that are required as part of the CEP. Schools also address the needs of Students with Disabilities (SWD) through the High Quality Inclusive & Accessible Learning for Students with Disabilities section of the plan. SLTs also develop a plan to improve attendance and reduce chronic absenteeism. DBN: 75R373 School: P.S. Key Strategy 1 (Required): To support the goal of increasing K–2 students’ MAP Growth Reading scores by an average of 8 RIT points by June 2026, the school will implement a comprehensive action plan focused on phonemic awareness instruction. First, all K–2 classrooms will implement the Heggerty Phonemic Awareness Program with fidelity. Teachers will deliver daily lessons, and school leaders will conduct regular walkthroughs using fidelity checklists to ensure consistency and quality of instruction. Professional development will be a key component of the plan. In September 2025, teachers will participate in targeted training on phonemic awareness and the Heggerty routines. Ongoing support will be provided through monthly coaching sessions and in-class modeling by the Teacher Development coach. To monitor student progress, the school will administer the MAP Growth Reading assessment in Fall, Winter, and Spring. Additionally, phonics- specific tools such as the MAP Skills Checklist will be used to track foundational reading skills more closely. Grade- level teams will analyze this data regularly to inform instruction. Students who do not meet benchmarks will receive tiered interventions, including small group or one-on-one support focused on phonics and phonemic awareness. These interventions will be scheduled and delivered by interventionists and classroom teachers. Finally, the school will engage families by hosting literacy workshops and sharing strategies for supporting phonemic awareness at home. These efforts aim to build a strong home-school connection and reinforce early literacy development beyond the classroom. Root Cause Addressed: To address the root cause of inconsistent and insufficient phonemic awareness instruction in K–2 classrooms, our school is implementing the Heggerty Phonemic Awareness Program with fidelity across all early grades. This structured, research-based curriculum provides daily, explicit instruction in foundational literacy skills. In addition, we are investing in targeted professional development and ongoing coaching to build teacher capacity and ensure high-quality delivery. By aligning instructional practices and providing consistent support, we aim to close early literacy gaps and improve student outcomes in reading. DBN: 75R373 School: P.S. The Multi-tiered Systems of Support (MTSS) framework, organizes supports into three tiers: Tier 1 provides schoolwide, high-quality programming for all students, including SWDs; Tier 2 offers data informed small-group interventions for at-promise students; and Tier 3 delivers intensive, individualized support for students with persistent needs. As demonstrated by the Spring 2024 NYSESLAT, Sand/Fast, and NYSAA scores, ELL students consistently struggle with academic language acquisition, particularly in the areas of reading comprehension and written expression. As a result, targeted support in these critical areas is essential for their academic growth and success. Quantitative data from the Spring 2024 and Spring 2025 NYSESLAT assessment shows a significant number of ELL students performing below the proficiency threshold, particularly in the reading and writing components of the exam. Specifically, 93% of ELL students scored at Level 1 (beginning) and Level 2 (intermediate), indicating that they need intensive support to improve their academic language skills. Additionally, the Sand/Fast and NYSAA data further emphasize the challenges these students face in demonstrating grade-level reading and writing proficiency. Qualitative data, including teacher observations and formative assessments, indicate that students often face significant challenges in understanding complex texts, formulating written responses, and engaging in academic discussions. Teachers report that these students struggle with basic vocabulary acquisition, constructing simple sentences, and organizing their thoughts coherently in writing. These difficulties are further compounded by limited communication abilities, which hinder their participation in both individual and group activities. As a result, these students often require additional support to process language, express their ideas, and make meaningful connections to the content being taught, which further emphasizes the need for specialized, targeted instructional strategies. The professional development provided in the Title III program is designed to address the unique needs of students with severe special needs, where individualized approaches are crucial due to the wide range of abilities and challenges, including communication, sensory processing, and attention. Data from staff needs assessments, surveys, and informal/formal conversations, as well as teacher observation data, indicate a strong need for targeted strategies in these areas. In classrooms, instruction often relies on prompting techniques such as least-to-most prompting and errorless learning, along with the use of visual cues, gestures, and augmentative communication devices to support non-verbal or minimally verbal students. Additionally, teachers struggle to maintain student engagement, and use methods like interactive activities, task variation, and reinforcement systems such as token economies. The integration of technology, such as speech-to-text apps and multisensory learning tools, has also been identified as essential to support literacy development.
