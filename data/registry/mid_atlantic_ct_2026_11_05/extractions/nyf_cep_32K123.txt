Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Comprehensive Education Planning (CEP) is a structured, data-driven process that guides schools in assessing their needs, identifying areas for growth, setting strategic goals and implementing targeted actions to improve student outcomes. Collaboratively developed by the School Leadership Team (SLT) in accordance with the requirements of Chancellor’s Regulation A-655, the CEP meets New York State Education Department (NYSED) requirements under the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) and aligns with NYC Public Schools’ (NYCPS) system-wide priorities. By engaging all stakeholders—school leaders, teachers, families, students and community members—schools can create a data-informed, equity- focused roadmap for student success and continuous improvement. Schools should focus their CEP on the five system-wide priorities below, connecting their planning to the data discussions and conversations with stakeholders held as part of the school’s needs assessment: Priority 1: All students learn to read well Priority 2: All students are physically and emotionally safe Priority 3: All students have a high-quality academic experience Priority 4: All students graduate college and career ready and have a strong plan and pathway to economic security Priority 5: All districts and schools are more inclusive and responsive for parents and families, including having more families choose NYC Public Schools The Education Planning Process The CEP follows an equity-driven, continuous improvement cycle, rooted in the system-wide priorities. This begins with a comprehensive needs assessment of the school’s most current data to identify priority needs and root causes. Next, the SLT establishes specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, and timely— SMART goal(s) aligned to each priority and determines targets to measure progress toward meeting the annual goals. The SLT and instructional leaders define key evidence-based key strategies, professional learning activities and parent, family and community empowerment efforts. They monitor the implementation of those strategies and activities and track progress through measurable targets. Then, the SLT aligns the school-based budget with human and instructional resources to support action plans. DBN: 32K123 School: P.S. 123 Suydam 5 of 209 As schools set goals, develop key strategies, and plan action steps, they provide opportunities for all children to meet NYSED’s proficient and advanced levels of student academic achievement. These include evidence based instructional strategies that address the educational needs of historically underserved populations and tackle structural inequities. Schools indicate how the plan addresses the needs of Multilingual/English Language Learners (ML/ELL) through development of the school’s Language Allocation Policy (LAP) & Title III Application (if applicable) and the Language Translation and Interpretation (LTI) Plan, that are required as part of the CEP. Schools also address the needs of Students with Disabilities (SWD) through the High Quality Inclusive & Accessible Learning for Students with Disabilities section of the plan. SLTs also develop a plan to improve attendance and reduce chronic absenteeism. Public School 123, located in the Bushwick community of Brooklyn, serves students in 3K through grade 5. Our richly diverse student population is approximately 93% Hispanic, with a significant multilingual learner population, many of whom are newcomers. We pride ourselves on being a welcoming and affirming environment where every child is known, valued, and supported to succeed, regardless of immigration status. Originally designated as a federally funded Magnet School for STEAM in 2017, PS 123K continues to fully integrate Science, Technology, Engineering, Arts, and Mathematics across the curriculum for all student leaders, even though federal magnet funding has since concluded. Students engage in authentic problem-based learning experiences, applying critical thinking to real-world challenges both inside and outside the classroom. Our Kids at Hope philosophy guides our mission: All leaders are capable of success — no exceptions. We encourage every child to express their individuality, build strong relationships, and develop a lifelong love of learning. Our STEAM programming is robust and dynamic. Students benefit from our Hydroponics Science Lab, supported by NY Sun Works, and a rich music program supported through partnerships with Save the Music and Carnegie Hall, including chorus, guitar, keyboard, recorder, and music technology. We partner with Ballroom Basix to integrate ballroom dance, supporting social-emotional growth and cultural appreciation, while CHAMPS programs promote fitness through running club, soccer, and other sports. Visual arts opportunities are provided through our in-house art specialist, encouraging student creativity and self-expression. Through Engineering is Elementary, coding, robotics, and 3D printing, students engage in design-based learning, building technical skills and collaboration. Our 1:1 technology model ensures all students develop literacy and numeracy foundations within a STEAM framework, promoting rigorous, relevant, and meaningful learning. Explanation/Background: Title I Schoolwide Program schools are expected to use the flexibility available to them to integrate services and programs with the aim of enhancing the entire educational program and helping all students reach proficient and advanced levels of achievement. In addition to coordinating and integrating services, Schoolwide Program schools may combine most Federal, State and local funds to provide those services. By consolidating funds from Federal, State, and local sources, a Schoolwide Program school can address its needs using all its available resources. This gives a school more flexibility in how it uses available resources to meet the identified needs of all its students. Consolidating funds in a Schoolwide Program means that a school treats the funds it is consolidating like they are a single “pool” of funds. In other words, the funds from the contributing programs in the school lose their individual identity and the school has one flexible pool of funds. The school uses funds from this consolidated Schoolwide pool to support any activity of the Schoolwide Program without regard to which program contributed the specific funds used for a particular activity – so long as the use of funds meets the intent and purposes of the programs from which the funds originate. To consolidate funding in a Schoolwide Program, the school does not literally need to combine funds in a single account or pool with its own accounting code. Rather, the word “pool” is used conceptually to convey that a Schoolwide Program school has the use of all consolidated funds available to it for the dedicated function of operating a Schoolwide Program without regard to the identity of those funds. Consolidating Federal funds in a Schoolwide Program has the following additional advantages: Consolidating Federal funds eases the requirements for accounting for funds from each specific program separately, because a Schoolwide school is not required to distinguish among funds received from different sources when accounting for their use. A school that consolidates Federal funds in its Schoolwide Program is not required to meet most of the statutory and regulatory requirements of the specific Federal programs included in the consolidation (e.g., semi-annual time and effort reporting for Title I). However, the school must ensure that it meets the intent and purposes of the Federal programs included in the consolidation so that the needs of the intended beneficiaries are met. 2. PS 123K is committed to providing family engagement activities that address the specific needs of our multilingual learner families, most of whom are newcomers and speak little or no English. Our rationale is rooted in both qualitative and quantitative data that highlight the importance of equipping families with tools to support their children’s language acquisition and academic growth at home. DBN: 32K123 School: P.S. 123 Suydam 203 of 209 Our quantitative data shows that the majority of our ELLs are performing at Entering, Emerging, or Transitioning levels on the NYSESLAT, and over 85% are newcomers to the U.S. school system. Many of these students come from homes where English is not spoken, and families themselves are often unfamiliar with the NYC public school system, curriculum expectations, and strategies to support language development. In addition, a significant percentage of our ELL students live in temporary housing, adding to the complexity of their needs and making targeted family engagement critical. Our qualitative data sources include feedback from monthly Coffee and Conversations, in-house parent surveys, and input from our Parent Association (PA) and SLT families. Parents consistently express the need for: -Help understanding the NYC school system and their rights regarding language services -Strategies to assist with homework and language-rich home routines despite limited English proficiency -Guidance on how to support literacy and math development at home in practical, manageable ways -Clear explanations of assessments like the NYSESLAT and how to help their children prepare -Flexible meeting times that accommodate work schedules, leading to the inclusion of both morning and evening sessions We use these data points to design workshops that are practical, culturally responsive, and relevant. Sessions are offered at 9:00 am (no cost to Title III) and 6:00 pm (Title III per-session funded) to maximize family participation, based on family feedback indicating these are the most convenient times. All topics and formats are directly responsive to family voice, student data, and our schoolwide focus on supporting newcomer ELLs in both academic and language growth.
