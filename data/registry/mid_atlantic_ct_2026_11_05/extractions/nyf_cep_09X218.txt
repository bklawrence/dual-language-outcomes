Magnet School 4 of 200 Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Comprehensive Education Planning (CEP) is a structured, data-driven process that guides schools in assessing their needs, identifying areas for growth, setting strategic goals and implementing targeted actions to improve student outcomes. Collaboratively developed by the School Leadership Team (SLT) in accordance with the requirements of Chancellor’s Regulation A-655, the CEP meets New York State Education Department (NYSED) requirements under the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) and aligns with NYC Public Schools’ (NYCPS) system-wide priorities. By engaging all stakeholders—school leaders, teachers, families, students and community members—schools can create a data-informed, equity-focused roadmap for student success and continuous improvement. Schools should focus their CEP on the five system-wide priorities below, connecting their planning to the data discussions and conversations with stakeholders held as part of the school’s needs assessment: Priority 1: All students learn to read well Priority 2: All students are physically and emotionally safe Priority 3: All students have a high-quality academic experience Priority 4: All students graduate college and career ready and have a strong plan and pathway to economic security Priority 5: All districts and schools are more inclusive and responsive for parents and families, including having more families choose NYC Public Schools The Education Planning Process The CEP follows an equity-driven, continuous improvement cycle, rooted in the system-wide priorities. This begins with a comprehensive needs assessment of the school’s most current data to identify priority needs and root causes. Next, the SLT establishes specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, and timely—SMART goal(s) aligned to each priority and determines targets to measure progress toward meeting the annual goals. The SLT and instructional leaders define key evidence-based key strategies, professional learning activities and parent, family and community empowerment efforts. They monitor the implementation of those strategies and activities and track progress through measurable targets. Then, the SLT aligns the school- based budget with human and instructional resources to support action plans. These include evidence based instructional strategies that address the educational needs of historically underserved populations and tackle structural inequities. Schools indicate how the plan addresses the needs of Multilingual/English Language Learners (ML/ELL) through development of the school’s Language Allocation Policy (LAP) & Title III Application (if applicable) and the Language Translation and Interpretation (LTI) Plan, that are required as part of the CEP. Schools also address the needs of Students with Disabilities (SWD) through the High Quality Inclusive & Accessible Learning for Students with Disabilities section of the plan. SLTs also develop a plan to improve attendance and reduce chronic absenteeism. At PS/IS 218 we believe that all students can become not only bilingual but biliterate. We hold all students to the expectation that they will become fluent readers, writers, and speakers in both English and Spanish. Additionally, the 218 philosophy is that all students can take ownership of their learning experiences and can develop autonomy regarding their academic experience. Student choice and recognition of multiple learning styles are key components to fostering this self-awareness in our students. The dual language program at 218 encourages and supports all students so that they can effectively develop their biliteracy skills. From the first day of Kindergarten students are immersed in two language worlds, where they study all subjects in both languages. Following the 50/50 side-by-side model gives students the opportunity to develop their academic skills in all subjects under the umbrella of two different languages. This side-by-side approach encourages consistent collaboration with classroom teachers and gives students the opportunity to develop academic and language skills simultaneously. Over school vision is to educate students in a dual language setting through developing independence in learning. To ensure that this vision is supported, we have created several Dual Language Models in K-8: Self-Contained, 50/50 side-by-side, 60/40 and 70/30. Additionally, we hire teachers with bilingual licenses to support our school vision. And, we have an extensive Dual Language leveled Library in English and Spanish. Another schoolwide goal is to continue to improve the school culture. Through PS/IS 218’S partnership with the Positive Learning Collaborative (PLC), school culture/climate is assessed every year through the PLC staff survey. The survey, delivered to ALL school-based staff, assesses the major school culture indicators: staff-student relations, staff-administration relations, communication, response to student discipline, and physical environment. The PLC staff survey outcome data is shared with all staff members to celebrate areas of strength and strategize upon areas of need to develop measurable, actionable steps. Key Initiatives for the Academic Year 2024-2025 For the academic year 2024-2025, P.S./I.S. 218 is excited to implement several key initiatives designed to enhance student learning and success, particularly in the areas of close reading, AIS (Academic Intervention Services) mathematics, and student-centered instruction. These initiatives reflect our commitment to fostering deep, enduring learning and preparing students for success in a rapidly changing, information-rich world. 1. Close Reading We are introducing a comprehensive program focused on fostering students' close reading skills. This program will involve regular, in-depth analysis of complex texts across all disciplines, aiming to improve students' reading comprehension, critical thinking, and analytical skills. Teachers will receive professional development on guiding students through multiple readings of a text, emphasizing extracting and synthesizing key information, understanding subtleties in the text, and identifying the author's intent and perspectives. 2. AIS Mathematics A new approach to AIS Mathematics will be implemented to support struggling students. Using data-driven teaching practices, we will identify these students earlier and design personalized intervention plans. The initiative includes one-on-one tutoring, small group workshops, and online tools and resources for both in-class and at-home learning. Regular progress assessments will ensure the effectiveness of the interventions, with plans adjusted as necessary. Additionally, P.S./I.S. 218 will continue to support teachers with a highly recommended math coach three times a month. 3. Student-Centered Instruction We will promote a shift toward student-centered instruction across all grade levels and subject areas. This approach empowers students to take ownership of their learning, encouraging active participation, collaboration, and critical thinking. Teachers will facilitate learning, offering guidance and support while giving students the opportunity to explore topics of interest, solve problems, and apply their learning in practical, real-world situations. This shift will involve significant professional development for staff, the creation of flexible and dynamic learning spaces, and the integration of technology to support and enhance student learning. Explanation/Background: Title I Schoolwide Program schools are expected to use the flexibility available to them to integrate services and programs with the aim of enhancing the entire educational program and helping all students reach proficient and advanced levels of achievement. In addition to coordinating and integrating services, Schoolwide Program schools may combine most Federal, State and local funds to provide those services. By consolidating funds from Federal, State, and local sources, a Schoolwide Program school can address its needs using all its available resources. This gives a school more flexibility in how it uses available resources to meet the identified needs of all its students. Consolidating funds in a Schoolwide Program means that a school treats the funds it is consolidating like they are a single “pool” of funds. In other words, the funds from the contributing programs in the school lose their individual identity and the school has one flexible pool of funds. The school uses funds from this consolidated Schoolwide pool to support any activity of the Schoolwide Program without regard to which program contributed the specific funds used for a particular activity – so long as the use of funds meets the intent and purposes of the programs from which the funds originate. To consolidate funding in a Schoolwide Program, the school does not literally need to combine funds in a single account or pool with its own accounting code. Rather, the word “pool” is used conceptually to convey that a Schoolwide Program school has the use of all consolidated funds available to it for the dedicated function of operating a Schoolwide Program without regard to the identity of those funds. Consolidating Federal funds in a Schoolwide Program has the following additional advantages: Consolidating Federal funds eases the requirements for accounting for funds from each specific program separately, because a Schoolwide school is not required to distinguish among funds received from different sources when accounting for their use. A school that consolidates Federal funds in its Schoolwide Program is not required to meet most of the statutory and regulatory requirements of the specific Federal programs included in the consolidation (e.g., semi-annual time and effort reporting for Title I). However, the school must ensure that it meets the intent and purposes of the Federal programs included in the consolidation so that the needs of the intended beneficiaries are met. 2.
