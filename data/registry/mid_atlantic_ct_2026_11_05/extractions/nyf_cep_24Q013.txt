Moore 4 of 228 Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Comprehensive Education Planning (CEP) is a structured, data-driven process that guides schools in assessing their needs, identifying areas for growth, setting strategic goals and implementing targeted actions to improve student outcomes. Collaboratively developed by the School Leadership Team (SLT) in accordance with the requirements of Chancellor’s Regulation A-655, the CEP meets New York State Education Department (NYSED) requirements under the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) and aligns with NYC Public Schools’ (NYCPS) system-wide priorities. By engaging all stakeholders—school leaders, teachers, families, students and community members—schools can create a data-informed, equity-focused roadmap for student success and continuous improvement. Schools should focus their CEP on the five system-wide priorities below, connecting their planning to the data discussions and conversations with stakeholders held as part of the school’s needs assessment: Priority 1: All students learn to read well Priority 2: All students are physically and emotionally safe Priority 3: All students have a high-quality academic experience Priority 4: All students graduate college and career ready and have a strong plan and pathway to economic security Priority 5: All districts and schools are more inclusive and responsive for parents and families, including having more families choose NYC Public Schools The Education Planning Process The CEP follows an equity-driven, continuous improvement cycle, rooted in the system-wide priorities. This begins with a comprehensive needs assessment of the school’s most current data to identify priority needs and root causes. Next, the SLT establishes specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, and timely—SMART goal(s) aligned to each priority and determines targets to measure progress toward meeting the annual goals. The SLT and instructional leaders define key evidence-based key strategies, professional learning activities and parent, family and community empowerment efforts. They monitor the implementation of those strategies and activities and track progress through measurable targets. Then, the SLT aligns the school- based budget with human and instructional resources to support action plans. As schools set goals, develop key strategies, and plan action steps, they provide opportunities for all children to meet NYSED’s proficient and advanced levels of student academic achievement. Moore 119 of 228 Explanation/Background 1. Explanation/Background: Title I Schoolwide Program schools are expected to use the flexibility available to them to integrate services and programs with the aim of enhancing the entire educational program and helping all students reach proficient and advanced levels of achievement. In addition to coordinating and integrating services, Schoolwide Program schools may combine most Federal, State and local funds to provide those services. By consolidating funds from Federal, State, and local sources, a Schoolwide Program school can address its needs using all its available resources. This gives a school more flexibility in how it uses available resources to meet the identified needs of all its students. Consolidating funds in a Schoolwide Program means that a school treats the funds it is consolidating like they are a single “pool” of funds. In other words, the funds from the contributing programs in the school lose their individual identity and the school has one flexible pool of funds. The school uses funds from this consolidated Schoolwide pool to support any activity of the Schoolwide Program without regard to which program contributed the specific funds used for a particular activity – so long as the use of funds meets the intent and purposes of the programs from which the funds originate. To consolidate funding in a Schoolwide Program, the school does not literally need to combine funds in a single account or pool with its own accounting code. Rather, the word “pool” is used conceptually to convey that a Schoolwide Program school has the use of all consolidated funds available to it for the dedicated function of operating a Schoolwide Program without regard to the identity of those funds. Consolidating Federal funds in a Schoolwide Program has the following additional advantages: Consolidating Federal funds eases the requirements for accounting for funds from each specific program separately, because a Schoolwide school is not required to distinguish among funds received from different sources when accounting for their use. A school that consolidates Federal funds in its Schoolwide Program is not required to meet most of the statutory and regulatory requirements of the specific Federal programs included in the consolidation (e.g., semi-annual time and effort reporting for Title I). However, the school must ensure that it meets the intent and purposes of the Federal programs included in the consolidation so that the needs of the intended beneficiaries are met. 2. Instruction is specifically designed to meet the diverse language acquisition needs of students, whether they are beginners or more advanced learners. Immigrant students face a variety of challenges when adjusting to a new educational system and culture. These can include language barriers, cultural adjustments, gaps in prior schooling, family-related concerns, limited support systems, and emotional or mental health struggles. Many of these challenges may go unnoticed as students focus on learning English. Addressing the social and emotional needs of immigrant students is therefore critical to their academic success. By recognizing and responding to the full spectrum of these challenges—academic, social, cultural, and emotional—educators and communities can create a supportive environment where students can thrive. Targeted social-emotional learning programs help immigrant students develop resilience and coping skills, which are essential for navigating both school and life successfully. Moore 225 of 228 Coat and Backpack Program (September/Ongoing) Parent Coordinator Family Notification of Activities and Translation and Interpretation Supports Describe: How families will be notified of Title III Immigrant family engagement activities (e.g., phone calls, emails), How notification of activities will be translated into parent/guardians’ preferred language, and How interpretation supports will be provided at events. At P.S. 13Q, ensuring that families are fully informed and able to participate in Title III Immigrant (including ELLs) family engagement activities is a top priority. To achieve this, the school employs a comprehensive, multi-tiered communication strategy designed to remove barriers related to language and accessibility. All invitations to workshops, events, and informational sessions are translated into families’ preferred home languages to guarantee inclusivity and ensure that every parent or caregiver can fully understand the information provided. Communication is disseminated through a variety of platforms to maximize reach and accessibility. These platforms include ClassDojo, the school website, social media channels, and printed flyers sent home with students. By utilizing multiple modes of communication, the school ensures that families receive timely notifications and have multiple opportunities to access information in the format that is most convenient for them. Bilingual and multilingual staff play a key role in facilitating clear communication. Whenever possible, documents and materials are translated by qualified staff members into the home languages of the families. In cases where staff proficiency is limited, the school partners with The Office of Language Access to provide professional translation services. This office supports the translation of memos, newsletters, and other communications into a wide range of languages, and also provides over-the-phone interpretation to assist non-English speaking families in understanding important information. To further support ongoing engagement, the school leverages ClassDojo to provide automated reminders, updates, and notifications about upcoming meetings, events, and deadlines. Information is also consistently updated and maintained on the school’s social media platforms, creating multiple touchpoints for families to stay connected with the school community. By combining personalized translation support with technology-driven communication strategies, P.S. 13Q ensures that all families, regardless of language proficiency, have equitable access to information, can actively participate in their child’s education, and are empowered to support their child’s academic, social, and emotional growth.
