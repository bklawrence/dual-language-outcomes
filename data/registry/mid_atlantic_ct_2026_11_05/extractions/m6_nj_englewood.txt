EPSD Program Program Description BILINGUAL: Dual Language The Dual Language program (DLP) has been offered in the Englewood Public School district (EPSD) since 1993. The DLP is currently being offered in kindergarten through fifth grade. Our design consists of primary language maintenance and development of the second language. The program services multi-lingual learners and monolingual English speakers alike. The specific goals are: DEVELOP BILINGUAL, BI-LITERATE AND BI-COGNITIVE SKILLS IN ENGLISH LANGUAGE LEARNERS DEVELOP FUNCTIONAL BILINGUAL AND BI-LITERACY SKILLS IN MONOLINGUAL ENGLISH-SPEAKING STUDENTS PROMOTE CROSS-CULTURAL, RACIAL AND ETHNIC TOLERANCE FOSTER AN UNDERSTANDING OF AND APPRECIATION FOR DIVERSITY The goals are achieved by using a natural approach to primary and secondary language development and acquisition via content area instruction. EPSD adheres to increments of language distribution, and the student cohort is created based on newly enrolled multi-lingual learners in kindergarten and first grade. The population of students is closely paired with students whose parents choose to enroll in a yearly lottery and who are then selected via the same process. The DLP follows the EPSD scope and sequence and implements supports that focus on phonemic awareness, reading comprehension, problem-solving and higher-order critical thinking skills. BILINGUAL (Full Time) The bilingual program is made available to students in the one language group (Spanish) and who are recent arrivals to the US. It is offered to students in grades Pre- K- fifth and designed to: ENSURE THAT MULTI-LINGUAL LEARNERS RECEIVE THE OPPORTUNITY OF AN EQUITABLE EDUCATIONAL ENVIRONMENT FOR ACADEMIC ACHIEVEMENT. FOCUS ON CLOSING EXISTING LINGUISTIC OR ACADEMIC GAP PREPARE STUDENTS TO ENTER INTO A GENERAL EDUCATION OR DLP (Dual Language Program) or HIGH INTENSITY PROGRAM WITH FACILITY BILINGUAL: High Intensity The High-Intensity program is offered to students in grades 6th- 12th. It serves as a transition for students leaving the bilingual program or entering middle school and high school as identified English language learners (ELL). Students in this program receive language arts literacy, an ESL (English as a Second Language) Lab from a certified ESL teacher, as well as language support in the content area. The goal of this program is to: ASSIST WITH THE TRANSFER OF KNOWLEDGE FROM THEIR NATIVE LANGUAGE INTO ENGLISH FOR STUDENTS TO MEET LINGUISTIC AND ACADEMIC STANDARDS ESL ESL instruction is provided to students who are native of another language other than Spanish/English. ESL students in grades K-5 are serviced according to their WIDA level, while students in grades 1-6 are serviced in a pull-out model and provided instruction utilizing Balanced Literacy, WIDA strategies, and EPSD’s curriculum. Middle school and high school students receive services as a class/cohort or by their English proficiency level. World Language The World Language (WL) Program at EPSD starts at Dr. John Grieco Elementary, where students follow an immersion model in grades 1-2. They meet for instruction for 80 - 160 minutes (about 2 and a half hours) per week. Students in the third through fifth grades at Dr. Leroy McCloud are taught via a world language related arts program that integrates LAL, Math, and Science and students at the Janis Dismus Middle School (7-8) are targeted to participate in courses intended to prepare them for entrance into a second-year program at the high school level. Students who have continuously participated throughout the elementary years in the WL program and/or in the Dual Language program have the option of entering Dwight Morrow's High School level II Spanish Class (based on their language ability/final assessment score). If accepted into a Spanish II or Heritage class, they can continue language learning until reaching the Honors/AP level. Spanish is offered in grades 1-12, while French and Mandarin are only offered at the high school level. ENTRY: An Englewood Public School District home language survey is provided to all parents/guardians at enrollment. The information gathered about a student’s language background such as primary and language spoken at home, helps to identify students who could be referred for the English language proficiency assessment. All students who meet the requirements to assess are given the WIDA Screener. The assessment helps to determine whether the student should be identified as a multi-lingual learner- (English Language Learner -ELL) and therefore receive a language assistance service or not. If the student scores below an “overall 4.5” on the assessment, the information is entered into the school districts' student information system (GENESIS), where the student is then identified as an ELL. If the student is identified as an ELL, notification of identification and placement in the school district's language instruction educational program, is sent via a translated letter to the parent/guardian. Use of HLS at registration to begin the process. If the need is noted by the parent/guardian or the student’s primary/secondary language is Spanish, then the student is assessed. The WIDA Screener is used for students in grades K-12 . The student is identified and placed in a language assistance program (Bilingual, Dual Language, or ESL (English as a Second Language)- High Intensity if the student scores lower than an overall 4.5. Notice is sent to the parents. EXIT: To exit the language assistance program (Bilingual/ Dual Language or ESL-High Intensity), a student must: Pass the WIDA-ACCESS 2.0 with an overall score of 4.5 or above. Score at approaching or meeting in NJDOE (New Jersey Department of Education) or district assessments (IE-NJSLA/ Literably/ HMH Growth Measures) . Complete in all content area courses good standing. Receive Teacher recommendation. Receive stakeholders review confirmation (Parent, Teacher, Administrator(s) and if needed, the student). ASSESSMENTS: Bilingual and ESL students are provided with assessments in both English and Spanish. Assessments provided in English are the same offered to the general education population, however, the assessment provided in Spanish range from common formative assessments to Phonemic Awareness (Estrellita), EDL (Reading Comprehension), as well as other tools and resources such as: Reading A-Z and IXL.
