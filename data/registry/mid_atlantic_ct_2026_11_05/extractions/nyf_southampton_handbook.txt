This handbook is designed to serve as a comprehensive guide for families, outlining our program’s vision, instructional model, curriculum, assessments, and the important role families play in supporting bilingualism, biliteracy, and biculturalism. Program Vision and Goals Our Dual Language program values and celebrates the unique strengths of our community while fostering the curiosity, joy, and wonder of our students. The partnership between Southampton’s families and staff is central to sustaining our school’s mission and vision. This Program is built on the belief that learning in two languages is a powerful asset. Through high-quality instruction in both English and Spanish, students develop strong academic skills while also building cultural understanding and global awareness. Our core goals are to support students in becoming: ● Bilingual – able to communicate effectively in English and Spanish ● Biliterate – able to read, write, and think critically in both languages ● Bicultural – able to appreciate, respect, and navigate multiple cultures Students in the program learn rigorous academic content while developing confidence, flexibility, and pride in their linguistic and cultural identities. International Spanish Academy Southampton is an International Spanish Academy (ISA) designated district and its Spanish program meets the standards established by Spain´s Ministry of Education, Culture and Sport. The ISA program offers students a comprehensive education that will develop skills and abilities through a total immersion in the Spanish language and culture, and also through the use of an instructional model that integrates content, communication, knowledge and culture. Students receive ISA diplomas in Fourth Grade after consistent participation throughout elementary school, starting in Kindergarten. Students also receive an ISA diploma after completing ISA courses beginning fifth grade through eighth grade consecutively. Twelfth grade students will receive an ISA diploma after completing ISA courses from ninth through twelfth grade consecutively. Students who have not completed consecutive years in the ISA program will receive a local certificate. 3 Benefits of a Dual Language Program Benefits for English Proficient students, more than twenty years of research have shown: English-Proficient students develop proficiency in two languages English-proficient students score as well or better than their monolingual peers on standardized tests of math and English Language Arts. English-proficient students develop a positive sense of self and demonstrate positive cross-cultural attitudes and behaviors Benefits for English Language Learners, more than forty years of research has shown: English Language Learners (ELL) who receive Spanish language instruction were able to catch up to or surpass the achievement levels of English proficient peers who were educated in English-only mainstream classrooms. These results were found regardless of the student’s socioeconomic background. (Thomas & Collier, 2002) English learners who participated in programs that provided extended instruction in the native language (dual language programs) outperformed students who received short-term instruction through their native language (transitional bilingual education). The Process of Language Acquisition Language acquisition is the process of developing the ability to understand a language and use it to communicate within societal norms. It’s the process of going from a wordless individual into somebody who can articulate their thoughts coherently and without reservation. We learn language through two modalities. Basic Interpersonal Communication Skills (BICS) which involves language skills needed for everyday language. BICS are personal, social language with clear and concrete contexts such as “Can I go to the bathroom, do you want to play, I’m hungry” etc. Language evolves into Cognitive Academic Language Proficiency Skills (CALPS)which involves cognitive development, academic conversations, reasoning, problem solving, analysis, and synthesis, such as understanding core subjects and abstract concepts. You learn a second language the same way you learn a first language. It is a process that requires time usually taking from up to 7 years to develop. Language development is based on motivation and practice. Proficiency in the first language serves as a platform where linguistic information is transferred from the native language to make sense of the second language. (Smith, Frank, Cummins, Jim) 4 Language Allocation- Southampton Elementary School Model Our Elementary School program follows a 60/40 language allocation model: 60% of the instructional week is taught in English 40% of the instructional week is taught in Spanish This model is designed to ensure strong development in English while intentionally supporting Spanish language growth. Language allocation is carefully planned across content areas to maximize transfer of skills between languages. Elementary (K-4) Curriculum Overview English Language Arts (ELA): English Language Arts is taught using Magnetic Literacy, a comprehensive, research-based core reading program. Magnetic Literacy includes: Foundational Skills (Grades K–2) Phonological awareness, Phonics and word study, High-frequency words, and Early reading and spelling Integrated Literacy (Grades K–4) Knowledge-building, Reading comprehension, Writing, Vocabulary development, and Speaking and listening In addition, students participate in Support and Extend, a dedicated small-group instructional time. During Support and Extend, teachers: Provide targeted support based on student needs Offer enrichment and extension opportunities Differentiate instruction to help all students grow Spanish Language Arts (SLA): Spanish Language Arts is taught using Mosaico Lectoescritura, the sister program to Magnetic Literacy. Mosaico Lectoescritura includes: Foundational Skills in Spanish (Grades K–2) Phonological awareness in Spanish, Phonics aligned to Spanish orthography, Word reading and spelling Integrated Literacy (Grades K–4) Knowledge-building, Reading comprehension, Writing, Vocabulary development, and Oral language Integrated Literacy Language Rotation Integrated Literacy instruction alternates between English and Spanish 50% of Integrated Literacy instruction is in English and 50% is in Spanish This intentional rotation helps students make connections across languages and strengthens biliteracy development 5 Mathematics (K-4): Mathematics is taught using i-Ready Classroom Mathematics and is instructed in English. This curriculum: Emphasizes conceptual understanding, Builds strong problem-solving skills, Provides opportunities for practice and discussion, and Aligns with grade-level standards Although math instruction is in English, students benefit from the language skills and academic strategies they develop across both languages. S.T.E.A.M. - Science, Technology, Engineering, Art, and Mathematics: STEAM is taught in English for all students. The teacher offers translanguaging for students with limited English proficiency. Translanguaging is the ability to move fluidly between languages and a pedagogical approach to teaching in which teachers support this ability. STEAM is taught using the Science21 Curriculum. Social Studies: Social Studies is taught in Spanish in the Dual-Language program. Instruction in these content areas emphasizes: Building academic vocabulary in Spanish Developing content knowledge through reading, discussion, and hands-on learning Making connections across languages Teachers use translanguaging strategically as needed to support comprehension, deepen understanding, and allow students to draw on their full linguistic repertoire while maintaining Spanish as the primary language of instruction Conexiones All students in grades K-4 receive Conexiones once per week, a Spanish class dedicated to exposing students to various Hispanic cultures around the world. SPANISH Spanish 1 Grades 9-12; Proficiency Exam** 1 credit In this course students will become actively engaged in meaningful and authentic uses of the Spanish language. They will develop a variety of skills that will allow them to use Spanish in a myriad of authentic situations. Specifically, students will use the following modes of communication: Interpretive Mode = Comprehend reading and listening materials in Spanish, Interpersonal Mode = Engage in conversations with speakers of Spanish, Presentational Mode = Present information and ideas in both written and spoken formats of Spanish. In addition, cultural information is woven throughout this course in order to expose students to the people, geographical locations, food, music and other cultural aspects of the Spanish-speaking world. (NCAA-Approved) Spanish 2 Grades 9-12 1 credit Prerequisite: Spanish 1 This course builds upon skills developed in Spanish I, extending students’ ability to understand and express themselves in Spanish and increasing their vocabulary. Typically, students learn how to engage in discourse for informative or social purposes, write expressions or passages that show understanding of sentence construction and the rules of grammar and comprehend the language when spoken slowly. Students usually explore the customs, history and art forms of Spanish-speaking people to deepen their understanding of the culture(s). (NCAA-Approved) 14 SISA Spanish 5H Grades 10-12 1 credit Prerequisite: Spanish 4 (or demonstrated language proficiency) This course is taught in Spanish and is designed to introduce a representative body of literature and historical texts, written in Spanish from Spain, Latin America and the United States. The studies are aimed at developing the student’s analysis of poetry, short stories and novels in relation with the culture of their country and its relevance in the international setting. The thematic structure of this course is composed of the study of regional literature, avant-garde Latin American poetry, the Latin American boom and the post-boom literature. (NCAA-Approved) AP Spanish Literature ISA Grade 12; AP Exam 2 credits Prerequisite: AP Spanish Language and Hispanic Culture III (or demonstrated language proficiency) The AP Spanish Literature and Culture course is designed to develop student's critical reading and analytical writing skills. The course will present the representative texts (short stories, novels, poetry, and essays) from Peninsular Spanish, Latin American, and United States Hispanic literature. Only unabridged, full text, Spanish language versions of the required 38 readings will be used in class. Literature is examined within the context of its time and place, as students reflect on the many voices and cultures present in the required readings. This course is a double period to provide the student with the opportunity to explore cultural connections and comparisons through various media (e.g., art, film, articles, literary criticism). (Note: This course meets two periods each day). Services to ELL students are provided according to their level of proficiency. 17 Commitment By opting into the Dual Language Program, families are making a long-term commitment for their child to participate in the Dual Language program throughout their school career. This commitment supports program consistency and allows students to fully develop bilingualism and biliteracy over time. Exiting the Dual Language program will result in a classroom change. When opting in or out, that commitment is for one full year. Family Commitment The Dual Language Program is systematic in nature. The effects of education are cumulative and the full benefits are only evident over the long term of the enriched educational experience. It is important for families to commit to the program throughout the school year. Active parent involvement in the program is required to sign a commitment agreement. Families are essential partners in our Dual Language Immersion program. Research shows that long-term family involvement is critical to program success. When parents engage consistently and value bilingualism, children are more likely to thrive academically, socially, and culturally. This is a long-term journey, and the full benefits of bilingualism and biliteracy become evident over time. (Cloud, Genesee & Hamayan, 2000; Arlington Public Schools, 2023) Hence, parents of students in the program will be required to sign a commitment agreement and commit to the program. How can parents help their child at home? Families play a vital role in a child’s success in a Dual Language Program. You do not need to speak both languages to support your child. Ways families can help at home: Encourage reading in any language Talk with your child about their school day Ask your child to explain what they are learning Celebrate bilingualism and cultural identity Provide opportunities for meaningful conversation Attend Parent workshops on supporting your Dual Language child(ren) at home offered by the District The most important support you can offer is fostering a positive attitude toward learning and language. 18 Frequently Asked Questions Will my child be confused about learning in two languages? No. Research shows that children are capable of learning multiple languages and that skills transfer between languages over time. What if my child is stronger in one language? This is normal. Language development varies, and the program is designed to support growth in both languages over time. How long does it take to become bilingual and biliterate? Bilingualism and biliteracy develop gradually, usually taking up to 7 years. Consistent participation in the program through elementary school supports long-term success. Criteria for Admission and Retention: Admission to the program begins with attending an information session prior to Kindergarten and submission of a signed commitment letter. Decisions regarding admission after Kindergarten, placement, and continued participation in the program are made on an individualized basis and take into consideration each student's unique academic, linguistic, social-emotional, and educational needs. Ongoing participation is reviewed collaboratively to ensure that the program continues to support the student's growth and success. Written testimonials?
