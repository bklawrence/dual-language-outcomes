Perry 4 of 165 Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Comprehensive Education Planning (CEP) is a structured, data-driven process that guides schools in assessing their needs, identifying areas for growth, setting strategic goals and implementing targeted actions to improve student outcomes. Collaboratively developed by the School Leadership Team (SLT) in accordance with the requirements of Chancellor’s Regulation A-655, the CEP meets New York State Education Department (NYSED) requirements under the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) and aligns with NYC Public Schools’ (NYCPS) system-wide priorities. By engaging all stakeholders—school leaders, teachers, families, students and community members—schools can create a data-informed, equity- focused roadmap for student success and continuous improvement. Schools should focus their CEP on the five system-wide priorities below, connecting their planning to the data discussions and conversations with stakeholders held as part of the school’s needs assessment: Priority 1: All students learn to read well Priority 2: All students are physically and emotionally safe Priority 3: All students have a high-quality academic experience Priority 4: All students graduate college and career ready and have a strong plan and pathway to economic security Priority 5: All districts and schools are more inclusive and responsive for parents and families, including having more families choose NYC Public Schools The Education Planning Process The CEP follows an equity-driven, continuous improvement cycle, rooted in the system-wide priorities. This begins with a comprehensive needs assessment of the school’s most current data to identify priority needs and root causes. Next, the SLT establishes specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, and timely— SMART goal(s) aligned to each priority and determines targets to measure progress toward meeting the annual goals. The SLT and instructional leaders define key evidence-based key strategies, professional learning activities and parent, family and community empowerment efforts. They monitor the implementation of those strategies and activities and track progress through measurable targets. Then, the SLT aligns the school-based budget with human and instructional resources to support action plans. DBN: 14K034 School: P.S. 034 Oliver H. Key Strategy 1 (Required): To improve student reading proficiency at P.S. 34, we are implementing a year-long action plan that prioritizes personalized, differentiated instruction through daily station-teaching, AI-enhanced lesson design, and Specially Designed Instruction (SDI) for students with disabilities. Multilingual learners will also receive targeted scaffolds to support language acquisition and close learning gaps. Teachers will engage in ongoing professional development and collaborative planning to align supports across instructional teams, while student progress will be continuously monitored through formative assessments and structured data reviews. This inclusive, student-centered model allows for responsive teaching that meets learners where they are and provides the right supports at the right time. By weaving together small-group instruction, technology-driven personalization, and targeted interventions, the plan addresses key barriers to reading achievement. As a result, we anticipate measurable growth in literacy outcomes by the end of the year, particularly for historically underserved student groups. Root Cause Addressed: The action plan directly targets a root cause of low reading proficiency often observed in schools: the lack of differentiated, inclusive instruction that meets the diverse needs of all learners—especially multilingual students and those with disabilities. Here’s how the plan addresses that root issue: Root Cause: Many students fall behind in reading because instruction is delivered at a “one-size-fits-all” pace or level, without sufficient personalization. This can leave students without the targeted practice, language scaffolds, or strategic interventions they need to access grade-level content. How the Plan Addresses It: Daily Station Teaching By organizing instruction into teacher-led and independent stations, students engage in small group learning that allows teachers to meet them where they are. This structure reduces whole-class pacing pressures and adapts instruction to varied reading levels. DBN: 14K034 School: P.S. 034 Oliver H. Additionally, inconsistent systems for recognizing positive attendance and intervening early can allow absentee patterns to deepen over time. By implementing a tiered plan that promotes belonging, celebrates attendance, and connects families to tailored resources, P.S. 34 targets this underlying disconnect—creating a more proactive, inclusive approach to ensure every student is present, supported, and ready to learn.
