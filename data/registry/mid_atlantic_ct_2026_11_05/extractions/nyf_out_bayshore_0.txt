/ Academics / World Languages, ENL and Dual Language World Languages, ENL and Dual Language Overview The Bay Shore School District is committed to fostering biliteracy and cultural competency across all grade levels (K–12), recognizing that multilingual communication enhances academic performance and enriches our students' personal educational experience. Comprehensive K–12 Pathways: Language learning begins at a young age through the district’s Dual Language Program at the elementary level and typically continues at the secondary level. Students who do not participate in our dual language program, participate in formal language study beginning in grade 7, adding to their exposure and use of languages other than English. Advanced Academics: Students have the opportunity to achieve language mastery through highly rigorous high school coursework, including Advanced Placement (AP) and International Baccalaureate (IB) programs. Support for English Language Learners (ELLs): The English as a New Language (ENL) program offers multiple avenues for English acquisition. Students new to the English language have opportunities to learn English through our ENL program, which includes participation in dual language and content-based courses beginning in middle school and continuing through their high school experience. Instructional Excellence: The program focuses on providing all students with access to high-quality educators dedicated to promoting global communication and biliteracy. Our commitment to cultivating biliteracy graduates whose outstanding academic achievements are celebrated annually through prestigious school board recognition. By fostering these multilingual pathways, we continue to lead in preparing culturally responsive, globally minded citizens. Presentations: Dual Language Program Family Engagement K-5 Dual Language Program: Two-Way Spanish + Two-Way Dual Language is a Spanish dual language program where instruction is conducted in both English and Spanish. Students are taught content through both their native language and the target language. At least 30-50% of the day is spent in the target language. Our two-way program is intended for both native Spanish speakers and native English speakers who are integrated for instruction 100% of the day. These classes are heterogeneously grouped. Program Entry Requirements For Native Spanish speakers: Upon completion of the Home Language Questionnaire (HLQ), if a language other than English is spoken at home, the child will be assessed to determine their English language proficiency using the New York State Identification Test for English Language Learners (NYSITELL). Native Language speakers who require English as a New Language (ENL) services might be placed in one of our Dual Language programs (pending parent approval) or mainstreamed to receive ENL services. Entry requirements for K-5 require Spanish screening (Benchmark assessments and Observational Survey). For Native English speakers: Upon parent request, students will be screened in order to determine eligibility into one of our Dual Language programs. It is strongly recommended that these students enter the program in Kindergarten or no later than first grade. Entry requirements include screening and benchmarking assessments. These students will be chosen by way of the district’s demographically balanced Dual Language lottery. Dual Language Program at Middle School + The dual language program continues in the middle school for students who have participated at the elementary level. Students participate in a standalone class focused on the development of advanced communication skills. Grades 7-12 World Language Language Program at a Glance + Beginning in seventh grade, students begin introductory coursework in Chinese, French, Latin, or Spanish, focusing heavily on vocabulary and pronunciation. Learning is driven by interactive, high-interest activities such as songs, skits, technology integration, and cultural events (festivals, field trips, and food days). Instruction emphasizes conversational speaking, listening comprehension, vocabulary development, and specific grammatical structures. Students practice communication through cooperative learning groups and demonstrate progress through targeted projects and conversations. While eighth-grade coursework transitions to focused preparation for the FLACS A proficiency exam, the experience goes well beyond test prep. The program prioritizes active, meaningful communication and deep cultural understanding, ensuring students build literacy and confidence in real-world settings. Students continue their language journey in high school to prepare for the comprehensive FLACS B exam. Many Spanish students advance to highly rigorous Advanced Placement (AP) and International Baccalaureate (IB) level language learning, while others achieve advanced proficiency by continuing their studies in Chinese, French, or Latin.
