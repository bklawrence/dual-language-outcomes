Weber 4 of 193 Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Comprehensive Education Planning (CEP) is a structured, data-driven process that guides schools in assessing their needs, identifying areas for growth, setting strategic goals and implementing targeted actions to improve student outcomes. Collaboratively developed by the School Leadership Team (SLT) in accordance with the requirements of Chancellor’s Regulation A-655, the CEP meets New York State Education Department (NYSED) requirements under the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) and aligns with NYC Public Schools’ (NYCPS) system-wide priorities. By engaging all stakeholders—school leaders, teachers, families, students and community members—schools can create a data-informed, equity- focused roadmap for student success and continuous improvement. Schools should focus their CEP on the five system-wide priorities below, connecting their planning to the data discussions and conversations with stakeholders held as part of the school’s needs assessment: Priority 1: All students learn to read well Priority 2: All students are physically and emotionally safe Priority 3: All students have a high-quality academic experience Priority 4: All students graduate college and career ready and have a strong plan and pathway to economic security Priority 5: All districts and schools are more inclusive and responsive for parents and families, including having more families choose NYC Public Schools The Education Planning Process The CEP follows an equity-driven, continuous improvement cycle, rooted in the system-wide priorities. This begins with a comprehensive needs assessment of the school’s most current data to identify priority needs and root causes. Next, the SLT establishes specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, and timely— SMART goal(s) aligned to each priority and determines targets to measure progress toward meeting the annual goals. The SLT and instructional leaders define key evidence-based key strategies, professional learning activities and parent, family and community empowerment efforts. They monitor the implementation of those strategies and activities and track progress through measurable targets. Then, the SLT aligns the school-based budget with human and instructional resources to support action plans. These include evidence based instructional strategies that address the educational needs of historically underserved populations and tackle structural inequities. Schools indicate how the plan addresses the needs of Multilingual/English Language Learners (ML/ELL) through development of the school’s Language Allocation Policy (LAP) & Title III Application (if applicable) and the Language Translation and Interpretation (LTI) Plan, that are required as part of the CEP. Schools also address the needs of Students with Disabilities (SWD) through the High Quality Inclusive & Accessible Learning for Students with Disabilities section of the plan. SLTs also develop a plan to improve attendance and reduce chronic absenteeism. Within our ELL/MLL community our students have different needs according to their proficiency level which ties to their individual academic needs. We feel that it is important for our staff to have an understanding of the different stages of language acquisition, proficiency levels and how this understanding will support their students. Along with rigorous instruction, a teacher's understanding, skill, and ability to use different strategies, and scaffolds support the success of meeting the academic and linguistic needs of our ELL/MLLs. , We have a large community of newcomer migrant ELLs that struggle with literacy and all of our teachers must know how to use ELL strategies to support them. The rationale for the professional development activities provided through our Title III ELL program is grounded in both quantitative and qualitative data analysis that highlight instructional gaps and specific areas for teacher growth in supporting English Language Learners (ELLs). Teacher observation data, including formal and informal classroom walkthroughs conducted by the Assistant Principal, have shown a need for more consistent use of academic language scaffolds, such as sentence stems, visual aids, and structured oral language activities. Quantitative analysis of student achievement data further supports the need for targeted PD. NYSESLAT results show limited progress in speaking and writing domains, especially among students in the Entering and Emerging proficiency levels. NYS ELA and Math assessments, along with MAP Growth and Acadience data, reveal a trend of ELLs performing below grade-level expectations, with notable gaps in vocabulary, reading comprehension, and oral language fluency. Teachers have expressed in feedback sessions a desire for concrete strategies to better engage ELLs and make rigorous content more accessible. Based on these findings, professional development will focus on the following activities: Training on differentiating instruction for multilingual learners using scaffolding strategies -Workshops on translanguaging practices to support comprehension and student voice -Sessions focused on analyzing NYSESLAT data and using it to guide instruction -Hands-on training in integrating digital tools like ELL BrainPOP to support language development -Guided practice using resources such as the Sound-Spelling Transfer Kit (Benchmark) to support foundational literacy These professional learning opportunities are designed to be practical, data-driven, and immediately applicable in the classroom. The ultimate goal is to equip teachers with tools and strategies that address the specific linguistic and academic needs of our ELL students, thereby improving student outcomes and fostering more inclusive and effective classroom environments.
