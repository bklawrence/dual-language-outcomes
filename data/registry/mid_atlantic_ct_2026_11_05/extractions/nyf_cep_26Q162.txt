of 192 Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Comprehensive Education Planning (CEP) is a structured, data-driven process that guides schools in assessing their needs, identifying areas for growth, setting strategic goals and implementing targeted actions to improve student outcomes. Collaboratively developed by the School Leadership Team (SLT) in accordance with the requirements of Chancellor’s Regulation A-655, the CEP meets New York State Education Department (NYSED) requirements under the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) and aligns with NYC Public Schools’ (NYCPS) system-wide priorities. By engaging all stakeholders—school leaders, teachers, families, students and community members—schools can create a data-informed, equity- focused roadmap for student success and continuous improvement. Schools should focus their CEP on the five system-wide priorities below, connecting their planning to the data discussions and conversations with stakeholders held as part of the school’s needs assessment: Priority 1: All students learn to read well Priority 2: All students are physically and emotionally safe Priority 3: All students have a high-quality academic experience Priority 4: All students graduate college and career ready and have a strong plan and pathway to economic security Priority 5: All districts and schools are more inclusive and responsive for parents and families, including having more families choose NYC Public Schools The Education Planning Process The CEP follows an equity-driven, continuous improvement cycle, rooted in the system-wide priorities. This begins with a comprehensive needs assessment of the school’s most current data to identify priority needs and root causes. Next, the SLT establishes specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, and timely— SMART goal(s) aligned to each priority and determines targets to measure progress toward meeting the annual goals. The SLT and instructional leaders define key evidence-based key strategies, professional learning activities and parent, family and community empowerment efforts. They monitor the implementation of those strategies and activities and track progress through measurable targets. Then, the SLT aligns the school-based budget with human and instructional resources to support action plans. These include evidence based instructional strategies that address the educational needs of historically underserved populations and tackle structural inequities. Schools indicate how the plan addresses the needs of Multilingual/English Language Learners (ML/ELL) through development of the school’s Language Allocation Policy (LAP) & Title III Application (if applicable) and the Language Translation and Interpretation (LTI) Plan, that are required as part of the CEP. Schools also address the needs of Students with Disabilities (SWD) through the High Quality Inclusive & Accessible Learning for Students with Disabilities section of the plan. SLTs also develop a plan to improve attendance and reduce chronic absenteeism. The school has been experiencing a steady decrease in enrollment due to a wide range of factors such as new schools opening in the nearby neighborhood and families In the 2019-2020 school year there were 643 students enrolled; now register predictions for 2025-26 school year is approximately 325 students. Key Strategy 1 (Required): To address the gap in early identification of at-risk readers, PS 162 will implement a structured phonics progress monitoring system in grades K–2 during the 2025–26 school year. The goal is to detect reading risk within the first month of instruction and deliver targeted, data-driven interventions throughout the year—before students fall multiple grade levels behind. The first step will occur during summer 2025, when school leaders and the literacy team will use Acadience assessments to establish benchmark cut-points to flag students at risk for decoding and phonemic awareness challenges. By the end of September 2025, the screener will be administered schoolwide in K–2 classrooms. Within one week of administration, teachers and grade teams will meet to review the results and flag students who require immediate Tier 2 intervention. Beginning in October, identified students will be monitored every 2 weeks using the ALO progress monitoring protocol (e.g., letter-sound fluency, nonsense word fluency) to track their progress over time. These results will be discussed in grade-level data meetings, and groupings or interventions will be adjusted accordingly. Throughout the year, Tier 2 and Tier 3 supports will be fluid: students may enter or exit groups based on real-time data rather than static benchmarks. The MTSS team will regularly review student-level data to determine if interventions are having an impact and whether instructional intensity needs to increase. Mid-year (January 2026) and end-of-year (June 2026) reviews will take place to evaluate the overall effectiveness of the progress monitoring system. These reviews will include analysis of how many students remained “below” or “well below” in their reading progress compared to baseline levels in September. Root Cause Addressed: This plan addresses the root cause by replacing delayed, reactive MTSS identification with a proactive system that flags phonics and decoding difficulties within the first month of school. Through the use of biweekly progress monitoring and data-driven decision-making, students receive timely, targeted interventions before falling significantly behind—ensuring early reading risk is addressed during the critical K–2 window when foundational skills are most effectively developed. Explanation/Background: Title I Schoolwide Program schools are expected to use the flexibility available to them to integrate services and programs with the aim of enhancing the entire educational program and helping all students reach proficient and advanced levels of achievement. In addition to coordinating and integrating services, Schoolwide Program schools may combine most Federal, State and local funds to provide those services. By consolidating funds from Federal, State, and local sources, a Schoolwide Program school can address its needs using all its available resources. This gives a school more flexibility in how it uses available resources to meet the identified needs of all its students. Consolidating funds in a Schoolwide Program means that a school treats the funds it is consolidating like they are a single “pool” of funds. In other words, the funds from the contributing programs in the school lose their individual identity and the school has one flexible pool of funds. The school uses funds from this consolidated Schoolwide pool to support any activity of the Schoolwide Program without regard to which program contributed the specific funds used for a particular activity – so long as the use of funds meets the intent and purposes of the programs from which the funds originate. To consolidate funding in a Schoolwide Program, the school does not literally need to combine funds in a single account or pool with its own accounting code. Rather, the word “pool” is used conceptually to convey that a Schoolwide Program school has the use of all consolidated funds available to it for the dedicated function of operating a Schoolwide Program without regard to the identity of those funds. Consolidating Federal funds in a Schoolwide Program has the following additional advantages: Consolidating Federal funds eases the requirements for accounting for funds from each specific program separately, because a Schoolwide school is not required to distinguish among funds received from different sources when accounting for their use. A school that consolidates Federal funds in its Schoolwide Program is not required to meet most of the statutory and regulatory requirements of the specific Federal programs included in the consolidation (e.g., semi-annual time and effort reporting for Title I). However, the school must ensure that it meets the intent and purposes of the Federal programs included in the consolidation so that the needs of the intended beneficiaries are met. 2. P.S. 162 Q's Title III After-school program will supplement our mandated program for English Language Learners. We will invite ELLS in grades K-5. Students will be grouped according to their grade level and/or their NYSESLAT and NYSITELL proficiency levels. Three mixed grade classes, consisting of Transitioning and Expanding students, will be created: K and Grade 1, Grades 2 and 3, Grades 4 and 5. This school year, P.S. 162 Q is excited to continue an afterschool program that engages MLs/ELLs in problem-based STEM activities that elicit higher order thinking and are aligned with the school's vision statement: "To create the space where all students learn who they are and who they need to be to become the creative and innovative problem solvers of the 21st century." The goal of this program is to have students explore scientific concepts through group projects that promote communication and problem-solving skills which can transfer into real world applications to ensure the students are college and career ready. Teachers will scaffold this work using various ENL (English as a New Language) instructional strategies. The MLs/ELLs will be immersed in a wide range of activities to promote reading comprehension, written expression, listening comprehension, and speaking language skills. The Language Experience Approach will be utilized to extend listening and speaking into reading and writing and to validate the student's own language in a natural setting. Thematic picture word walls, graphic organizers, paragraph frames, pictures, sentence frames, TPR, modeling, role playing, and the use of SMART board technology are incorporated to encourage students to participate in class activities. Critical thinking skills are emphasized through the creation of group projects that vary according to the unit of study. The materials used are supplemental, distinct from those used for daily instruction, and provide students with multiple entry points. Parents will be notified in both English and their preferred languages. Records of each student's attendance will be kept by the teachers. Both copies of parent notifications and attendance records will be kept in a binder for compliance purposes. At the conclusion of the afterschool program, students will have an opportunity to share their work with the school community. As a culminating project, students and a family member will be offered an opportunity to take advantage of the rich cultural environment that surrounds our community by participating in a field trip that incorporates the science of the curriculum in the real world. During the field trip, students will be able to observe how the curriculum they learned is applied in a real world setting.
