SCHOOL 4 of 235 Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Section 1: Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Overview of Comprehensive Education Planning Comprehensive Education Planning (CEP) is a structured, data-driven process that guides schools in assessing their needs, identifying areas for growth, setting strategic goals and implementing targeted actions to improve student outcomes. Collaboratively developed by the School Leadership Team (SLT) in accordance with the requirements of Chancellor’s Regulation A-655, the CEP meets New York State Education Department (NYSED) requirements under the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) and aligns with NYC Public Schools’ (NYCPS) system-wide priorities. By engaging all stakeholders—school leaders, teachers, families, students and community members—schools can create a data-informed, equity- focused roadmap for student success and continuous improvement. Schools should focus their CEP on the five system-wide priorities below, connecting their planning to the data discussions and conversations with stakeholders held as part of the school’s needs assessment: Priority 1: All students learn to read well Priority 2: All students are physically and emotionally safe Priority 3: All students have a high-quality academic experience Priority 4: All students graduate college and career ready and have a strong plan and pathway to economic security Priority 5: All districts and schools are more inclusive and responsive for parents and families, including having more families choose NYC Public Schools The Education Planning Process The CEP follows an equity-driven, continuous improvement cycle, rooted in the system-wide priorities. This begins with a comprehensive needs assessment of the school’s most current data to identify priority needs and root causes. Next, the SLT establishes specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, and timely— SMART goal(s) aligned to each priority and determines targets to measure progress toward meeting the annual goals. The SLT and instructional leaders define key evidence-based key strategies, professional learning activities and parent, family and community empowerment efforts. They monitor the implementation of those strategies and activities and track progress through measurable targets. Then, the SLT aligns the school-based budget with human and instructional resources to support action plans. DBN: 09X443 School: THE FAMILY SCHOOL 5 of 235 As schools set goals, develop key strategies, and plan action steps, they provide opportunities for all children to meet NYSED’s proficient and advanced levels of student academic achievement. These include evidence based instructional strategies that address the educational needs of historically underserved populations and tackle structural inequities. Schools indicate how the plan addresses the needs of Multilingual/English Language Learners (ML/ELL) through development of the school’s Language Allocation Policy (LAP) & Title III Application (if applicable) and the Language Translation and Interpretation (LTI) Plan, that are required as part of the CEP. Schools also address the needs of Students with Disabilities (SWD) through the High Quality Inclusive & Accessible Learning for Students with Disabilities section of the plan. SLTs also develop a plan to improve attendance and reduce chronic absenteeism. P.S. 443 – The Family School is proudly located on the 09x90 George Meany Campus in the Morris Heights section of the Bronx. Our school serves students from 3K through Grade 5, providing a nurturing and rigorous inquiry-based learning environment. We aim to offer an academic experience modeled after a supportive home, where every child feels respected, valued, and celebrated for their unique cultural identity. We believe in the power of Culturally Responsive and Sustaining Education to honor the diversity of our students and community. Our school serves as an educational gateway for many recent arrivals to the United States, and we are deeply committed to fostering life-long learners. With a focus on both academic rigor and social-emotional development, we strive to equip our students with the skills necessary to become respectful, hard-working, and collaborative citizens of the world. The community that P.S. 443 serves is diverse and vibrant. The majority of our students come from low-income households, with 99.1% qualifying for free and reduced lunch. Our student body represents a range of ethnic backgrounds, including students from Puerto Rico, the Dominican Republic, Haiti, South Africa, Mexico, and beyond. The school’s ethnic makeup is as follows: Hispanic/Latinx (72%), Black (24%), Asian (2%), Native Hawaiian/Pacific Islander (1%), and White (1%). In addition, 32% of our students receive special education services, and 34% are English Language Learners (ELLs), further emphasizing the need for inclusive, differentiated instruction that meets every student's needs. Our core instructional programs include: HMH Into Reading (ELA): This literacy program provides a solid foundation in reading and writing, with specialized DBN: 09X443 School: THE FAMILY SCHOOL 16 of 235 scaffolds to support multilingual learners and a bi-literacy solution in both Spanish and English. Eureka Math2: A robust math program that helps students build a deep conceptual understanding of mathematics, preparing them for success in higher education and future careers. Amplify Science and Passport to Social Studies: These programs focus on knowledge building and collaborative discourse among students to deepen their understanding of science and social studies concepts. All of these programs are fully aligned with the Next Generation Learning Standards and Advanced Literacy Instruction to ensure that our students are equipped with the critical skills they need. Technology Integration Incorporating technology into the classroom is a cornerstone of our instructional philosophy. Every classroom at P.S. 443 is equipped with Smartboards, and our curriculum is supported by a range of online platforms including Teach Hub, Google Classroom, and Amplify Science websites. We have over 800 iPads available for student use and have invested in additional technology—including 300 iPads for our K-2 students, Chromebooks, and laptops for students in grades 3-5—to support programs like i-READY, ZEARN, and AMIRA. These resources empower our teachers and students, ensuring that technology is fully integrated into the learning process. Community Partnerships and Enrichment Programs P.S. 443 is proud to partner with organizations that enhance our students' educational and personal development. One of our standout initiatives is the "Music in the Brain" residency program. Offered through BUILDING FOR THE ARTS NY, this program brings music literacy and keyboard instruction to our school, empowering our music educator and fostering equity in arts education. The program ensures that all students, regardless of background, have the opportunity to develop skills that benefit both their academic success and personal growth. We also collaborate with New York Edge to provide a dynamic after-school program. This program offers students not only homework support but also a range of enrichment activities in dance, fitness, and the arts. Our students are encouraged to explore their interests and build new skills in a fun, supportive environment. In addition to academic enrichment, P.S. 443 strongly values community engagement. We regularly participate in activities such as food, clothing, and toy drives to support local families in need. These activities teach our students the importance of citizenship and social responsibility, reinforcing the value of helping others. Parental and Community Engagement Our school thrives because of the strong partnerships we build with families. We recognize that parents are essential stakeholders in the educational process, and we make it a priority to communicate openly and frequently with them. Through translated letters, our school website, Google Classrooms, PBIS, and Class Dojo, we ensure that families are well-informed about school activities, updates, and their child’s progress. This continuous communication helps to foster a collaborative and supportive school community where students can succeed academically and socially. Explanation/Background: Title I Schoolwide Program schools are expected to use the flexibility available to them to integrate services and programs with the aim of enhancing the entire educational program and helping all students reach proficient and advanced levels of achievement. In addition to coordinating and integrating services, Schoolwide Program schools may combine most Federal, State and local funds to provide those services. By consolidating funds from Federal, State, and local sources, a Schoolwide Program school can address its needs using all its available resources. This gives a school more flexibility in how it uses available resources to meet the identified needs of all its students. Consolidating funds in a Schoolwide Program means that a school treats the funds it is consolidating like they are a single “pool” of funds. In other words, the funds from the contributing programs in the school lose their individual identity and the school has one flexible pool of funds. The school uses funds from this consolidated Schoolwide pool to support any activity of the Schoolwide Program without regard to which program contributed the specific funds used for a particular activity – so long as the use of funds meets the intent and purposes of the programs from which the funds originate. To consolidate funding in a Schoolwide Program, the school does not literally need to combine funds in a single account or pool with its own accounting code. Rather, the word “pool” is used conceptually to convey that a Schoolwide Program school has the use of all consolidated funds available to it for the dedicated function of operating a Schoolwide Program without regard to the identity of those funds. Consolidating Federal funds in a Schoolwide Program has the following additional advantages: Consolidating Federal funds eases the requirements for accounting for funds from each specific program separately, because a Schoolwide school is not required to distinguish among funds received from different sources when accounting for their use. A school that consolidates Federal funds in its Schoolwide Program is not required to meet most of the statutory and regulatory requirements of the specific Federal programs included in the consolidation (e.g., semi-annual time and effort reporting for Title I). However, the school must ensure that it meets the intent and purposes of the Federal programs included in the consolidation so that the needs of the intended beneficiaries are met. 2. Funding follows similar guidelines for Title III funds established by the Office of Multilingual Learners (OML) and can only be used to provide supplementary services to immigrant students, who may or may not be English Language Learners (ELLs). Please note: Immigrant students are not necessarily English Language Learners (ELLs), and ELLs may or may not be immigrants. For the purpose of this funding, immigrant students are defined as individuals who were not born in any U.S. state (this includes the District of Columbia and the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico); and have not been attending one or more schools in any one or more states for more than three full, academic years (the months need not be consecutive).
