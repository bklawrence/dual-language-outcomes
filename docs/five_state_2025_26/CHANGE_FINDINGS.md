# Change and closure findings

Reference year: 2025–26. Rechecked November 4, 2026.

Thirty-seven district/operator scopes received targeted change/closure review,
with additional language/campus queries for the large California rosters. The
machine-readable audit retains every query, returned discovery result, document
reviewed, outcome, program scope, and limitations. Absence of a report is used as
the authorized continuity assumption, not proof that every archive was searched.

## Leads that do not establish program termination

SFUSD's 2025 Cantonese biliteracy phase-outs are consequential, but the collected
immersion campuses are different program tracks. The [Public Press investigation](https://www.sfpublicpress.org/san-francisco-schools-to-add-language-classes-while-scaling-back-programs-for-english-learners/)
distinguishes those phase-outs from the district's immersion programs. We did
not mark every Cantonese immersion school closed because Cantonese biliteracy
classrooms elsewhere were cut.

Pasadena fire damage, physical campus closures and later consolidation proposals
do not themselves establish termination of French, Mandarin or Spanish programs.
The official school/program descriptions and restoration information support
continuity, with temporary placements still needing campus-level follow-up.
Franklin-McKinley's consolidation proceedings likewise did not yield a reviewed
document terminating Meadows Spanish or Windmill Springs Vietnamese DLI; their
program page explicitly retains Fall 2025 recruitment. This was a selected-source
review, not a reading of every board packet.

The Las Vegas charter-closure report identified **Nevada Prep**, not Strong Start
Academy. Mariposa's 2024 board document concerned rejecting a proposed expansion
financing plan, not revoking its charter. Wyola's short 2023 school shutdown ended
with classes resuming; none of these leads establishes termination of the listed
continuing program.

## Remaining unconfirmed cases

**LAUSD secondary listings:** the 2025 Master Plan, pp. 49–50, explicitly permits
a secondary DLE pathway to contain only one language elective, with other
target-language content courses offered when possible. Thus no end-of-program
report does not settle academic-content eligibility. Those listings remain
candidates, except Venice Mandarin, whose official campus academy description
establishes a multisubject curriculum.

**Indigenous proposals and cultural descriptions:** St. Stephens' older proposal
has not been verified as implemented, and Weitchpec's cultural magnet description
does not establish academic instruction through Yurok. These are not cases where
the only missing information is a recent language-allocation update.

**Later schedules:** Fresno's 2025–26 roster confirms program listings, but its
July 2026 brochures cannot by themselves establish 2025–26 K percentages. Their
later 90/10 Spanish and 50/50 Hmong schedules are preserved separately. Fresno's
undated district-wide Spanish FAQ also describes preschool-through-first-grade
90/10 instruction; that policy supports the selected Spanish elementary model,
with explicit continuity/applicability notes, rather than backdating the 2026
brochure. Yu Ming's April 2025 charter gives a K–2 85–90% Mandarin range while also describing a
90/10 model; an exact point schedule remains unresolved. These allocation gaps
do not prevent operation confirmation.

The full [coverage report](COVERAGE_REPORT.md) and `change_events.csv` distinguish
these evidence gaps from actual closure, relocation, expansion, and model change.
