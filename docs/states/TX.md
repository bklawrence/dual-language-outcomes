# Texas national-model pilot

Inputs are the preserved Pass 50 language/linked-policy exports, Pass 44 structural evidence and the four saved Pass 45 TPRS observations. [Earlier research documentation](../texas/README_PASS50.md) continues to describe the verification limits.

The pilot migrates 1,610 campus identities and 1,863 **provisional evidence groups**. Candidate groups retain their original IDs in the crosswalk; they are not asserted to be 1,863 distinct programs. Language scope and dates come from Pass 50. District policy history is not backdated to a particular campus, and later Wooten language evidence remains later evidence. Unknown pairings receive no invented language rows.

All 970 original kindergarten policy observations remain in `allocation_schedules`, including published model labels, raw source periods, historical-entry-year notes and unresolved language links. The migration does not turn a model label into verified named-language instructional minutes or tested-cohort exposure. Schoolwide structural claims remain school-level evidence; whole-school DL does not imply a whole-school Spanish track.

Four TPRS reporting groups produce eight subject observations: ELA and mathematics, 2023–24, all tested grades, **BE-Dual One-Way current emergent bilingual/EL**. Three reporting units are campuses and one is Round Rock ISD. Numeric denominators are absent in the saved extract. Grade-specific, partner-language-specific and non-EL DL participant results cannot be recovered from these values.

Reporting-group/candidate links remain unresolved or combined; none establishes direct track attribution. The district aggregate has no individual-campus links. Original public report URLs, source references and saved-extract hashes remain available in provenance.

The first assessment layer is therefore a small, explicitly scoped pilot. Statewide academic/context data, English-language proficiency results, EL exit criteria/rates and SEDA have not yet been collected into the shared Texas tables. This status does not mean Texas fails to publish them.
