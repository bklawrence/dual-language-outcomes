# First national structural gap pilot

November 6, 2026. The [nine-district dataset guide](../../data/registry/structural_gaps_pilot_2026_11_06/README.md) documents the sources, decisions, comparison cohorts and reproduction commands.

The pilot adds **257 observations across 93 existing school research identities**. In the fixed 2025–26 comparison, numerical primary-grade model availability increases from **37 to 59 of 222 elementary-containing identities (16.7% to 26.6%)**. These are selected research cases, including candidates; this is not a national or district census estimate. Texas's older source-year cohort remains separate.

There are **63 newly resolved programme-language classifications, 26 one-way/two-way classifications, 25 grade scopes and one whole-school/strand classification**, across the fixed 348 programme-site evidence groups. Published labels, named-language grade schedules and core-only percentages remain distinct. Coverage at some primary grade does not imply kindergarten coverage or coverage of every language strand.

The first review was useful: Alief's model labels were already available for 2025–26, so its new support is counted as verification/detail rather than 18 newly filled model gaps. Garland's dated Vietnamese story supports Hickman and the matching Lister programme. Union County's school-specific English-start grades support core-only 100/0 phases and later 50/50 phases, with the inference and denominator retained. Coronado explicitly states that its 80/20 programme is the sole option for enrolled pupils. Las Cruces's published 50/50 descriptions preserve native-language kindergarten literacy exceptions.

The [nine review cases](../../data/reports/structural_gaps_pilot_2026_11_06_review_cases.csv) include Vial's conflicting model descriptions, Doss's historical 50/50 versus current unassigned alternatives, candidate TAPR associations, Kensington's Mandarin listing, Buncombe's variable allocations, SAIL's untimed language-specific English lessons, Las Cruces's K–1 literacy exception, Gadsden's missing campus plans and Tres Volcanes's unresolved Navajo immersion scope. No roster omission is treated as proof of closure.

The main lesson for the next batch is to collect admissions and implementation documents for whole-school/strand participation, and approved campus BMEP plans for New Mexico allocation. Generic programme descriptions yielded much more language/type evidence than participation evidence. The pilot retains source counts as an effort measure without inventing research-hour estimates.

All original structural rows and identities remain intact; the additions are separate observations and review metadata. The frozen national baseline, earlier reports and twelve assessment/context outputs remain unchanged. Recomputed derived reports are versioned under the pilot's report directory. No new proficiency, demographic, EL-exit or SEDA observations were collected.
