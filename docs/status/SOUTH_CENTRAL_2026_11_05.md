# Oklahoma, Arkansas, Louisiana, Alabama and Mississippi

The structural pass adds **101 programme records at 86 provisional school/campus units**, with **79 saved public sources**. Research now covers **30 states**: 29 have school/programme entries, including candidates and later starts, and Mississippi has screening records only. Twenty states remain without an integrated structural pass. This is a partial inventory, not a statewide census.

The operation reference remains **2025–26**. Published policies, grade context and later events retain their actual source dates. New proficiency, demographic, EL-exit and SEDA collection remains deferred.

## Oklahoma

Tulsa explicitly publishes **90/10 in K–2** at Eisenhower's French and Spanish strands and Zarrow's Spanish programme, reaching 50/50 by grade 5. Its one-way/two-way programmes describe subject allocation and equal literacy time, which do not establish a total-day percentage. Those models remain unfilled. Different population strands at Kendall-Whittier, Springdale and Skelly remain separate programme rows at shared schools. Will Rogers Middle is a named continuation with no K allocation. Dual Language Academy is retained as an older name of Felicitas Mendez, without another school count. Chart references to 2027–28 at Cooper and Springdale concern grade-5 rollout rather than initial opening.

Norman's September 2025 ribbon-cutting account confirms **Truman Primary's PK/K/1 50/50 programme**. The article appears under Monroe's website path but names Truman as the host. The 2026–27 page adds grade 2 and retains a future Truman Elementary continuation. Adelaide Lee's official Oklahoma City page explicitly announces a fall-2026 start; it is not a 2025–26 confirmation.

Western Heights publishes a clear two-way Spanish 90/10 entry policy. Its current saved department page does not name host schools, so the Council Grove/John Glenn mappings remain review cases. A Jenks parent-council account reports Mandarin intake phaseout since 2021–22; primary confirmation and the final continuation year remain unresolved. These cases do not inflate confirmed coverage.

The Cherokee Nation's academic immersion provision is distinct from a language elective. An official May 2026 graduation account confirms K pupils at both Tahlequah and Greasy during 2025–26. Greasy's wider grades are not copied from Tahlequah; Kenwood expansion remains screening. The chief's May 6 memorandum confirms state charter support through June 30, 2026, followed by independent tribal governance for 2026–27. This is a governance change rather than a programme closure.

Osage Nation's Daposka Ahnkodapi has documented K–8 immersion and a 2025–26 calendar. The government's August 2026 closure statement documents tribal-government funding exceeding $3 million annually, so a private-school description does not imply privately funded provision. Its later closure and October reopening appropriation remain distinct events; actual reopening is unverified. One provisional school unit spans two age-band locations. Yuchi's academic-medium provision is documented, but reference-year implementation and public funding remain review questions. Qualitative exclusive/full immersion descriptions are not assigned invented daily shares or English partners.

The low numerical coverage reflects Tulsa's unquantified all-day models and Indigenous schools' qualitative descriptions, plus excluded mapping/phaseout candidates. It does not mean only three elementary schools have confirmed programmes.

## Arkansas

Little Rock's official April 2026 announcement names **Watson and Chicot kindergarten starts for 2026–27**, adding one grade yearly. The district-republished board report differentiates Watson's one-way heritage-Spanish cohort from Chicot's two-way cohort. Majority-Spanish instruction does not provide a numerical K allocation. Both later starts are retained outside the 2025–26 denominator. Lacey is an eventual expansion proposal, not another implemented school.

The provider's Casa Castillo network describes preschool/childcare and school-age summer provision, which is retained in screening. No claim is made that Arkansas lacked all other programmes statewide.

## Louisiana

CODOFIL's map page claims 36 schools, while its public endpoint returns **35 actual objects with no additional pages**. All 35 source rows and names are preserved. The **24 Fall-2025 certified site labels** are a different selection, and include Spanish sites missing from a French-only map. Calcasieu's two language columns reconcile to **13 school/language rows**; Jefferson's current two-way roster names **11 Spanish hosts**.

FLAIM and Westdale retain French, Spanish and Mandarin separately. FLAIM publishes **60–70%** immersion, so a single point model remains unfilled. The school's three K language offerings do not become three schools. Hynes' 2025–26 handbook explicitly maps approximately 60% French immersion to Lakeview and UNO K–5, separately from weekly language classes and middle-school advanced French. The model is not assigned to Parkview. Hynes' later LSU-New Orleans website naming does not create another school.

Jefferson's two-way Spanish roster directly maps its **90/10 PK/K policy**, reaching 50/50 by grade 4. Ellis' older French/Spanish immersion evidence remains separate: the 2025 policy explicitly notes French phaseout, and the final active French grades/end year need review. Ruppel is excluded from DL programmes because the district explicitly says its core subjects are English with French taught as a subject.

Audubon's state profile gives 70% French, while its school page describes a 70–90% lower-elementary range and 70/30 from grade 3. The state label is retained with grade qualifications, without claiming an exact K timetable. Mandarin enrichment is not another immersion strand. ISL's elementary policies are approximately 90% target language in K–1 and 80% in grades 2–5; Uptown's upper-elementary grades receive the latter label. Its secondary instruction and additional language classes do not inherit a K model. Dixon/St. Roch names remain one provisional lower-campus reporting unit pending a dated official campus crosswalk, avoiding duplicate lower-school counts.

Glen View's school-linked February 2025 registration flyer states **60% of the day in French**. Fairfield's 2023 K launch report also states 60%. Qualified 60/40 labels preserve the arithmetic remainder without manufacturing named English-share rows. LeBlanc's brochure has 2025 in its filename but explicitly recruits for **2026–27**; its 60% French/40% English plan remains in that later year and does not fill a 2025–26 gap.

Longitudinal changes include Middlebrook's Mandarin closure after 2023–24 and the French/Spanish move from Paul Breaux to Scott Middle for 2024–25. The schools themselves are not labelled closed. Lafayette Renaissance's World Languages Academy announces Mandarin K–5 and Spanish K–1 for 2025, but implementation, host identity and inconsistent enrollment dates need follow-up. ASL enrichment is not another immersion strand. Lycée's French Quarter primary move has an August-2025 reopening report; Priestley is a secondary host. The proposed Acadiana campus starts in 2027 and remains screening.

Saved CCD grade context includes **Mamou Junior High (5–8)** and **Teche Elementary (3–5)** in elementary coverage. Cecilia Junior High and Evangeline Reimagine are 6–8 and excluded. These source-dated school grades do not establish programme-specific exposure or durable identity crosswalks. Several Lafayette Parish pages returned 503, leaving additional Spanish discovery and allocation work open. Rougon’s older operator newsletter explicitly describes French academic subjects and English literacy at PK/K/1. With no ending indication found in the checked sources, it is confirmed through the user-authorized continuity rule; its source date and missing numerical model remain unchanged. Douglass retains a historical directory follow-up question.

## Alabama

Birmingham's Booker T. Washington K–8 programme has official implementation reports in November and December 2025. The programme starts with PK/K/1 strands within the larger school. Its half-English-speaker/half-Spanish-speaker statement describes **pupil composition**, while its tentative timetable and variable specials do not establish a full-day numerical ratio. It is confirmed with allocation missing.

Huntsville's Academy for Science and Foreign Language describes daily French classes in elementary grades and language choices later; academic-medium immersion is not established. Other directory entries lack programme details and remain screening. An operator's claim that Birmingham is the first of its kind is retained without treating it as proof of statewide completeness.

## Mississippi

No qualifying DL programme was verified in this pass. The directory's Clarkdale entry supplies Mississippi contact details but no instructional-language/model evidence; the similarly named Georgia DLI school is not transferred into Mississippi's inventory. Choctaw Tribal Schools describes 30–60-minute language/culture periods, classroom integration and after-school activities at its eight named schools. These valuable language provisions remain distinct from verified academic immersion.

The state has preserved screening rows, source links and an open discovery question. Its empty school/programme CSVs contain headers intentionally. N/A allocation coverage means no verified qualifying denominator, not zero percent coverage or a finding of no statewide programmes.

## Preservation and validation

All earlier school/programme identities, 114 dated reports, earlier registry inputs/exports and 12 assessment/context/EL/SEDA outputs are guarded by preservation checks. Source checks reconcile original CODOFIL fields, named selections, programme languages, public funding, source dates and grade contexts. Tests cover later starts, closures, pupil versus instructional ratios, language enrichment, partial grade models and screened-only states. Offline rebuilds and separate DuckDB/Parquet exports validate the combined data. These checks establish recorded scope and preservation, not statewide completeness or independent certification of every research claim.
