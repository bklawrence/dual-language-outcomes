# Southeast structural inventory — November 5, 2026

This pass integrates **596 programme records at 552 provisional school/campus units**, supported by **155 saved public-source documents**, for Florida, Georgia, South Carolina, North Carolina and Tennessee. The national project now has structural research for **35 states**, with programme-level records in **34** and Mississippi screened separately. These are partial inventories, not estimates of statewide census coverage. No new proficiency, demographic, EL-exit or SEDA observations were collected.

Coverage is numerical instructional-model availability across the cited source periods, among confirmed qualifying 2025–26 elementary hosts in the reviewed working set. It includes any school serving K–5, including K–8, 3–5 and 5–8; secondary-only hosts are retained but excluded. It does not mean exact kindergarten schedules, every strand, each campus’s 2025–26 policy, or observed student exposure. Later policies count as available information with their actual dates visible. ## Source reconciliation and findings

- **North Carolina:** all 316 original live-directory CSV rows and every field are saved. Preschool and unnamed future placeholders remain screening entries; multiple languages and K–5/6–8 rows at the same named campus are linked rather than counted as new schools. The qualitative Full/Partial/Continuing categories alone do not establish 100/0 or another numerical ratio. District sources provide 50/50, 80/20 and 90/10 policies where grade and language scope are documented. Many remaining gaps are qualitative directory entries or inaccessible/outdated district links. Intermediate schools are retained in the elementary denominator.
- **Georgia:** all 87 school rows / 93 language-school cells in the FY25 state appendix are reconciled. Five preschool-only entries are screened separately, and Sandra Dunagan Deal’s reference-year K rollout remains unresolved. State guidance says 50/50 is typical and permits local design; local sources and explicitly flagged matching-programme inferences supply model assignments. Fulton’s programme for English Learners remains **one-way**. Japanese, Korean, Mandarin, German and French are preserved alongside Spanish. The current APS articulation page’s middle-grade text under North Atlanta High School is flagged rather than copied as its school grade.
- **Florida:** two distinct Broward 2025–26 roster versions conserve 33 and 34 cells, with Gulfstream K–8 counted once. Three elementary names appear only in the earlier version; omission is not declared a closure. The parent guide’s 1.5–2.5 daily Spanish hours are not rounded into a day ratio. Palm Beach has 40 named rows despite a different introductory total, including Haitian Creole at three elementary schools. Its 2025 Spanish guidance allows 50:50 or 90:10; the saved **2026** elementary guidance specifies 50/50. That later policy is not backdated. Miami-Dade’s BISO and International Studies lists are reconciled, including French at Arch Creek and Portuguese at Ada Merritt; one BISO partner identity remains unresolved. All **47 EFL schools** are preserved as leads because the generic language-arts requirement does not guarantee academic content immersion. Osceola’s schools are “working toward” 50/50, so implemented allocation remains unknown. Seminole’s 2022 eight-school brochure remains linked from the current district page and is accepted under bounded continuity.
- **South Carolina:** Lexington One’s elementary half-day model and named secondary pathways, Richland programmes, Beaufort’s Spanish/Mandarin strands, Rock Hill’s French/Spanish pathway, Florence, Riverbank and public charters are retained. Blythe’s pupil/magnet percentages are not instructional ratios. Its proposed French phaseout was **rejected by the board in November 2023**; the current operator still lists French. Riverbank’s September 2026 retrospective dates its K launch to 2024–25 and later grade expansion separately. Operator sources for East Point and Belvedere were inaccessible; public school context and university-listed academic immersion support bounded continuity, with direct operator verification in the review queue.
- **Tennessee:** Treadwell’s K model is **90/10**; its 50/50 home-language balance is a different measure. Nashville’s October 2026 retrospective confirms K launches at Glenview, Paragon Mills and Stratton in 2025–26 and first grade in 2026–27. Glendale’s timetable lacks a complete language assignment, and Barksdale’s “at least 50%” is a lower bound, so neither becomes an exact 50/50 model. Richview and ChattAcademy secondary provision is retained outside the elementary denominator.

## Longitudinal changes and Indigenous provision

The official Harnett parent hub names **14 historical hosts**. Eight were missing from the live state-directory selection and are restored from the historical operator list and retrospective closure reporting. All 14 retain 2025–26 operation; 12 have separately dated **2026–27 closure/consolidation events**. June 2026 board-review reporting and autumn reporting support the later outcome. Glenwood’s Mandarin academic immersion is planned to move to Rashkis in **Fall 2027**; Rashkis is a future host, and Mandarin world-language specials at Scroggs are not another DL programme.

**New Kituwah Academy is academic Cherokee immersion**, with a 2025–26 school handbook and a 2025 tribal-government annual report documenting its budget and expenditures. Adult KPEP classes are separate. No numerical allocation or unsupported English partner share is assigned. Private North Carolina academic programmes remain in the inventory with public funding unconfirmed and outside the confirmed-public reference denominator.

Park Street’s undated operator letter stops new K intake “next year” while preserving existing cohorts; no effective calendar year is invented. City Park’s FY25 state entry is missing from Dalton’s newer two-host description; no explicit ending was found, and that discrepancy remains a review case. Historical Hillsborough handbook selections absent from its newer roster are preserved as screening reconciliation cases rather than asserted closures.
