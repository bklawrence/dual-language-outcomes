# Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Rhode Island and Vermont

Reviewed November 6, 2026. **All 50 states now have a first structural research pass.** Partial programme evidence is integrated for 48 states, including candidates, historical hosts, continuations and plans; Mississippi and West Virginia have screening-only research. This milestone does not establish complete statewide school lists or full allocation coverage.

This batch adds **101 programme records across 97 provisional school units**, supported by **85 archived public documents**. Of these, **85 school units** qualify for the 2025–26 public K–12 reference working set; 65 are elementary-containing campuses and 20 are secondary-only. Programme records and school counts differ where languages share a school. Future plans, preschool-only phases and unverified funding are retained outside the qualifying reference set.

Coverage means at least one numerical academic language-allocation model at a primary grade. It does not mean the same model in every grade/strand, a complete K progression, measured student exposure or a statewide census. Some models have unresolved source conflicts or an explicitly flagged estimate.

**Maine:** Portland’s 2025 Lyseth presentation reports a former 90/10 one-way model, current 60/40 one-way model and future consideration of two-way immersion. Current reference coverage selects 60/40 without discarding the historical claim or inventing the transition year. The slide does not explicitly label each percentage by language, so named-share order remains unresolved. L’École Française du Maine is an academic French immersion candidate with public funding unverified. Ocean Avenue’s IB language exposure and limited Indigenous-school homepage checks do not independently establish academic DLI. Those leads remain available for curriculum follow-up.

**Massachusetts:** All 75 original DESE Word school paragraphs are conserved, including three Brockton language strands within one row and the composite Framingham high-school row. Current Framingham evidence specifically identifies Portuguese TBE and Spanish DL extension at high school; Portuguese DLI is not inferred from TBE. New Bedford’s June 2026 district spotlight independently verifies Spanish/English at Roosevelt, whose DESE row omits the language. Gomes explicitly documents 50/50 instruction.

Boston’s **K1 is preschool; K2 is kindergarten**. Blackstone and Sarah Roberts launched only K1 in 2025–26 and stay outside the K–12 reference set. Sarah Roberts explicitly plans kindergarten 80/20 in 2026–27; its preschool 90/10 is not a K model. Lila G. Frederick’s Cabo Verdean Creole preschool launch and Everett’s Spanish/Portuguese debuts are later September 2026 phases. Everett’s erroneous Washington hyperlink supplies no Massachusetts allocation.

Amherst’s actual 2025–26 contract gives K–1 90/10, grade 2 80/20 and grades 3–6 50/50. Brockton explicitly gives 50/50 academic instruction separately for French, Spanish and Portuguese. Maynard’s older linked presentation gives K–2 90/10, grade 3 80/20 and grades 4–5 50/50. Mendon-Upton gives K–2 100% Spanish core instruction, grade 3 80/20 and grade 4 60/40; its later grade-5/6 and elective phases remain distinct. Millis and Milton start immersion in grade 1; their 100/0 figures describe academic core instruction, excluding English specials where specified.

At Hernández, K2 lists 220 Spanish core minutes and 45 English phonics minutes. The dataset retains a **researcher estimate of approximately 83/17**, calculated as 220/265 and 45/265, with bilingual bridging and unlisted time explicitly noted. It is not a published whole-day ratio. Grades 2–6 are explicitly 50/50. The estimate has a separate classification method, exact source passage and inference type.

Open cases include Lynn’s Connery one-way district description versus the DESE two-way code; Cambridge Mandarin’s reported allocation dispute and rename; Fall River’s earlier 50/50 versus current K 80/20 and Viveiros-to-Greene transfer with unresolved effective dates; Worcester’s narrative “50/50 by third” versus grade-table 60/40 in grade 3; calendar-2026 middle-school plans without an exact school-year implementation confirmation; and the mismatched Millis/Pierce directory row. Worcester’s 2026–27 grade-7/8 expansion and explicit Burncoat High continuation during redesign are retained as later events, not programme closure.

**New Hampshire:** District and March/July 2026 reports confirm Bakersville Spanish operating since 2024 and Weston French starting in fall 2025. A district retrospective release explicitly supports 90% target-language kindergarten instruction for both languages; French does not borrow an unverified Spanish allocation. Sources differ on the future 50/50 endpoint, grade 4 versus grade 5, so no intermediate progression or upper-grade implementation is invented. The two campuses’ 2024–25 grade metadata remains separate from their actual programme cohorts.

**Rhode Island:** Providence’s district list and March 2026 brochure name eight Spanish/English hosts, but do not establish exact instructional shares at each school. A generic “at least 50%” definition and pupil language balance are not universal 50/50 models. International Charter School explicitly gives 50/50 for both Spanish and Portuguese; South Kingstown explicitly gives 50/50 at Peace Dale and West Kingston. Matunuck/Wakefield feeder communities are not extra instructional hosts. Greene’s own academic description independently verifies Spanish/English, rather than borrowing a language from district demographics. Nuestro Mundo’s academic English/Spanish two-way model is verified without inventing an allocation from “100% dual-language students.” Raíces programme continuity is retained, while its explicitly 2026–27 50/50 policy is not backdated. Spaziano’s differing grade spans and the 2027 De La Comunidad charter proposal remain separate review/planning evidence.

**Vermont:** MMUUSD’s handbook documents the 2024 English-phonics change to K–2 80/20 and grades 3–4 60/40 at Jericho and Richmond. Earlier “up to 90%” is a bound, not an exact historical model. Its May 23, 2025 post-immersion guide says all core classes are English from grade 5; custom Spanish/world-language/AP options remain pathway screening evidence, without being presented as continued core academic immersion at additional campuses.
