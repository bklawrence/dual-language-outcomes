# New York allocation follow-up

Reviewed November 6, 2026, against the **2025–26 operating reference**. All **200 elementary-containing schools in the existing NY inventory** received a follow-up, including the **187 NYC hosts**. Their official 2025–26 Comprehensive Educational Plans include a Language Allocation Policy that frequently supplies details absent from admissions pages. This remains a partial state inventory.

| Scope | Schools with an elementary numerical model | Coverage |
|---|---:|---:|
| Original inventory before follow-up | 8 / 200 | 4.0% |
| Same 200 schools after follow-up | 176 / 200 | 88.0% |
| Current inventory, including newly verified hosts | 178 / 203 | 87.7% |

The current NY inventory contains **273 school-language programme records at 259 school units**. The **56 secondary-only hosts** remain in the data and outside the elementary denominator. Coverage means at least one elementary strand/grade has a numerical model; it does not mean every strand or kindergarten has an exact timetable.

**162 elementary schools have source-recorded model evidence. Sixteen additional schools depend solely on researcher inference.** Twenty schools have some inferred evidence, including four with both source-recorded and inferred models. The inferred schedules comprise **two kindergarten subject-distribution estimates** and **18 language-cycle estimates** across strands/grades. None represents measured student exposure.

School plans yield several important corrections. **Anna Silver (01M020)** explicitly describes 50/50 for both Mandarin/Chinese and Spanish. **PS 163 Manhattan (03M163)** uses 80% Spanish in kindergarten and 50/50 in grades 1–5. **PS 89 Queens (24Q089)** reports a change to Spanish-heavy early grades: 80/20 in K, 70/30 in grade 1, 60/40 in grade 2, and 50/50 from grade 3. **PS 101 Queens (21K101)** specifies Mandarin/English 40/60 in K–1 and 30/70 in grades 2–5. English-first descriptions are normalized carefully: PS 770 Brooklyn’s 90% English / 10% Spanish is recorded as **10/90 Spanish/English**, with an academic-scope review flag.

The user authorized approximate 50/50 when a kindergarten subject distribution strongly suggests balanced instruction. **PS 124 Brooklyn** is recorded as an explicitly flagged K estimate from English mathematics/science and Spanish literacy/humanities. Its detailed subject description and generic wording differ, so that qualification stays attached. A separate bilingual special-education/ICT 60/40 label remains a distinct service-specific observation with language ordering unresolved. **PS 253 Queens** receives a K estimate from explicit 50/50 mathematics, science and social studies together with alternate-language ELA. The source passages, subject maps and assumptions are retained. Merely listing subjects in two languages does not trigger an estimate.

The full plans explicitly state 50/50 for both PS 145 programmes and PS 94’s Spanish and Chinese programmes; these are source-recorded models despite English-only components in PS 94’s timetable. PS 896 remains unclassified because substantial fixed English periods prevent a reliable 50/50 inference. PS 319’s six-day cycle and stated within-day percentages instead support an approximate 40% Spanish / 60% English estimate.

Balanced alternate-day/week cycles support additional approximate models, with qualifications for English-only supplements or cross-language days. These carry `researcher_inference` evidence and `inferred_plan` schedule status. They are distinguished from published numerical models in CSV, canonical tables and coverage summaries. Some plans contradict themselves: PS 30 Bronx, PS 311 Bronx, PS 5 Brooklyn’s French programme, and PS 97 Queens retain alternative documented models with `conflicting_documented_plan` status. No single resolved schedule is asserted.

Outside NYC, explicit district sources now verify **English/Spanish** for the four Rochester programmes and Seymour in Syracuse. The five research track labels now identify Spanish, with their earlier unverified labels preserved in dated correction records. Rochester’s original school names and IDs remain; current names for schools 12 and 33 are retained as aliases. No. 33 has an unresolved one-way/two-way difference between district pages. Neither Rochester, Syracuse nor Newburgh receives a time split merely because bilingual instruction is confirmed. Newburgh’s January 2025 curriculum-revision document confirms Fostertown and Vails Gate as K–5 English/Spanish DL hosts but does not disclose their overall allocation.

Newly verified hosts include **John M. Marshall Elementary in East Hampton**, with an explicit 50/50 model and dated 2025 continuity evidence, and **Southampton Elementary, Intermediate and High School**. Southampton’s undated district webpage reports 50/50 in K–4, while its **2026–27 handbook specifies 40% Spanish / 60% English**. Both remain separate observations; the actual date of change is unresolved. The intermediate school begins in grade 5 and therefore belongs in the elementary denominator, but its minimum two periods / 25% is not converted into an exact split. The high school retains formal continuation without an elementary allocation.

Remaining gaps include ASL/English programmes where modalities are used together, special-class programmes without time shares, subject-only ratios, and incomplete rotation schedules. PS 18 Brooklyn and PS 9 Queens have programme-type/presence conflicts between the city roster and school plan; no closure date is fabricated and neither is removed from the original denominator. Public 2026–27 leads in Bay Shore and Suffern, and a September 2026 Schenectady launch, remain separate from confirmed 2025–26 hosts. Ossining and Brentwood still require school-specific follow-up.

The pass adds **215 archived public sources** to the six-state source index, for **314 total documents**. The original 99 snapshots and metadata, 269 NY programme identities, 255 school identities, raw NYC service observations, other five state inventories, earlier reports and 12 assessment/context/EL/SEDA outputs are preserved. The original NY inputs and first-pass exports are also [frozen for comparison](../../data/registry/ny_allocation_followup_2026_11_06/baseline/). Proficiency collection remains deferred.
