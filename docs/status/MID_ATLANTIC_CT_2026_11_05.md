# Pennsylvania, Maryland, Delaware, New Jersey, New York and Connecticut

Initial pass November 5, 2026, with a Herricks correction November 6, against a **2025–26 operating reference**. The initial inventory contained **433 programme records across 404 provisional school units**, including separately flagged plans, candidates and historical hosts. It is not a statewide census. The initial pass archived **99 public documents** with URL, retrieval timestamp, content type and checksum metadata.

Structural research now covers **45 states**: **43 have programme records**, and Mississippi and West Virginia have screening-only research. **Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Rhode Island and Vermont** remain without an integrated structural pass. Existing assessment, context, EL-exit and SEDA outputs are preserved; additional proficiency collection remains deferred.

Coverage means a numerical language-allocation model is available for an elementary-containing school in the reviewed reference working set. Any K–5 grade qualifies, including grades 3–6 and 5–6. Grade 6+ only schools remain in the dataset and are excluded from this denominator. Undated and older explicit policies can support continuity after bounded review; they are not newly dated 2025–26 observations. Explicitly later policies remain separate. A model at one primary grade does not establish the full kindergarten progression, every strand, a complete bell schedule or individual/cohort exposure.

Pennsylvania includes Philadelphia’s nine district DL hosts and public charters, plus Allentown’s Sonia Sotomayor academy. Independence Charter’s explicit 2025–26 schedule is Spanish 90/10 in K–1, 70/30 in grade 2 and 50/50 in grades 3–5. Pan American reports 50/50. Most remaining checked pages describe content instruction without numerical time allocation. Independence Charter West’s Spanish subject curriculum and Pittsburgh Liberty’s Spanish enrichment remain screening cases; a similar name or magnet label does not establish academic immersion. Allentown’s dated February 2026 report confirms existing PK–2 operation and separately describes future expansion.

Maryland includes Montgomery’s six 50/50 two-way elementary schools, distinct foreign-language immersion strands and formal continuations; PGCPS’s eleven host schools and multiple Largo languages; Charles County’s dated PK/K launch and grade-1 rollout; and Baltimore’s two public international academies. Qualitative “total” or “partial” labels are not converted to numerical ratios. Baltimore East’s five implemented languages differ from West’s three; West’s proposed Russian/Arabic choices are not recorded as implemented. Their explicit all-target-language academic core in K–1 is retained on that basis, without claiming a measured whole-day schedule. The Bilingual Global Citizens charter’s planned fall 2025 opening still needs actual implementation proof and remains outside the reference denominator. Montgomery’s 2027–28 receiving hubs are future records.

Delaware preserves all **57 original school rows** from the **2021–22 state map**, representing 60 language strands because three hosts list two languages. A numeric typo (“2107”) remains raw source data, not a future launch. The state initiative’s elementary half-day policy supplies 50/50 for its Spanish/Chinese schools; it is not transferred to independent charters. Academia Antonia Alonso has its own K–4 50/50 policy and grade-5 20% Spanish/80% English policy. Odyssey’s Greek academic immersion is 50/50, separate from Greek FLES. ASPIRA’s numerical allocation remains unverified. Capital’s current seven-host pathway and grade changes are distinct from old Central/William Henry middle-school names; those historical identities are excluded from reference confirmation pending reconciliation. Shields/Lewes identity history remains flagged.

New Jersey includes HoLa, North Plainfield, West Windsor–Plainsboro, Princeton, Jersey City, Englewood and Hoboken district programmes. HoLa’s 90/10-to-50/50 progression is numeric but lacks exact transition grades. North Plainfield’s Somerset 5–6 host counts as elementary-containing; its grade-5 allocation is retained. WW-P preserves Spanish/Mandarin strands and the 2024 new-entrant transfer from Dutch Neck to Wicoff, without treating continuing Dutch Neck cohorts as closed. Hoboken’s proposed 90/10 model was revised to 50/50 before implementation; the old proposal is not an implemented schedule. Jersey City’s Spanish/Arabic transitional and other language supports remain distinct from its three named Spanish DL hosts. Englewood’s world-language staff columns do not become Mandarin/French immersion strands.

New York preserves the full official **579-row NYC 2025–26 bilingual CSV**: **300 DLBE service cells**, **279 TBE cells**, **254 distinct DL school-language strands** and **240 schools**. General/special education cells remain observations, not extra programmes. All eleven listed partner-language labels are retained, including ASL; “Chinese” is not silently changed to Mandarin. TBE rows remain screening records. The official DBNs are preserved separately from provisional research keys and CCD context. Most city rows do not report allocation; only matched school-specific policies supply ratios. Outside NYC, reviewed programmes include Buffalo, Port Chester, Herricks, Newburgh, Rochester and Syracuse. A November 6 follow-up to the user-identified Herricks FAQ documents Denton Avenue as a one-way 50/50 elementary programme. The FAQ mentions September 2023 kindergarten intake; it is retained at that source vintage, with a separate 2026–27 recruitment flyer corroborating the half-day model. Middle/high school continuation receives no elementary percentage. A supplementary parent presentation is archived for source review; its comparative Utah targets and proposed assessment schedule are not treated as implemented Herricks allocations or imported outcomes. The later school-specific follow-up verifies English/Spanish for Rochester’s four programmes and Seymour using explicit district sources, while preserving the earlier unverified observations. The inventory covers only a subset of non-NYC districts.

Connecticut includes public district and magnet programmes plus formal Windham continuation. Windham’s official 2025–26 fifth-grade move makes its middle school a 5–8 host and North Windham K–4; these dated claims replace older CCD grade context for this audit. “At least 50% Spanish” is not exact 50/50. DiLoreto’s “eventually working toward 50:50” remains a review case. Bridgeport’s linked handbook documents 50/50 instruction; its original image PDF is archived, with OCR and the critical page visually verified. Its PDF creation timestamp is not a publication or operation year. The linked two-host brochure differs from the current landing page’s one-host description, so Luis Muñoz Marín remains confirmed under the continuity rule with an explicit discrepancy case. New Haven’s sustained one-way biliteracy model is kept separate from two-way programmes and TBE; Fair Haven’s current page explicitly distinguishes its one-way DL and transitional programmes. The “FAME” two-way host identity remains unresolved, and Stamford’s launch notice has no verified host. RMMS Puentes supplies academic bilingual scope but no numerical allocation.
