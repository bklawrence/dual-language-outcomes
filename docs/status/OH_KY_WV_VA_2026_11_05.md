# Ohio, Kentucky, West Virginia and Virginia

The November 5, 2026 structural pass adds **110 programme records across 108 provisional school units**, with **105 archived public documents**. These totals include separately flagged candidates, historical hosts and later starts. The 2025–26 working set contains **99 qualifying school units**. No qualifying academic DL programme was verified in West Virginia; its leads and screening decisions are preserved without a statewide absence claim.

Structural research now covers **39 states**: **37 have programme records**, and Mississippi and West Virginia have screening-only research. Eleven states remain without an integrated pass: Connecticut, Delaware, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island and Vermont. All represented states still have discovery/verification gaps. Additional proficiency collection remains deferred until the structural state passes are finished.

Coverage is within the reviewed working inventory, not the proportion of all DL schools statewide. It means a numerical model is available in an undated or no-later-than-reference source for an elementary-containing host. It does not establish a complete 2025 school timetable, every language strand, kindergarten eligibility or observed student exposure. Explicitly later models remain visible in the broader all-recorded view. Any K–5 grade qualifies, including 3–5 and 5–6 campuses. Schools beginning grade 6 or later remain in the data and are excluded from the elementary denominator.

Ohio includes GALA's separate Spanish and Mandarin Chinese one-way strands, Columbus's Spanish and French elementary programmes and secondary continuations, Cincinnati AMIS Spanish-English immersion, Cleveland Buhrer and Luis Muñoz Marín, and Mansfield's public Spanish immersion magnet. The January 2026 Mansfield report confirms operation despite staffing/budget cuts and quotes all-Spanish academic instruction in K–2, with English introduced in grade 3. No later-grade percentages or precise date for its reported building move are invented.

Ohio's 100% is qualified by source ambiguity: Columbus Spanish Immersion's About page says 50/50 K–5, while Academics says 80/20 K, 70/30 grade 1, 60/40 grade 2 and 50/50 grades 3–5. Both undated policies are preserved and flagged; the dated 2025–26 handbook confirms operation without resolving the allocation. Luis Muñoz Marín's whole-school expansion notice is also undated. Columbus Bilingual Academy North remains an academic-scope candidate: the homepage claims bilingual core content, but detailed curriculum lists Spanish among electives. Fairview's intensive German FLES, Columbus Bilingual West's daily language curriculum, Escuela SMART and Bond Hill directory leads are screening cases rather than verified academic immersion hosts.

Kentucky's current Fayette roster has Maxwell, Liberty, Cardinal Valley and the Bryan Station middle/high pathway. Older explicit ratios are retained where current descriptions confirm operation, especially Maxwell and Hawthorne. Northern and Goldsmith have historical academic immersion evidence but incomplete current descriptions; no explicit ending was found in the bounded checked school/district/news sources, so both remain confirmed under the user's continuity rule and carry review cases. Cardinal Valley's one-way heritage-Spanish classification is explicitly historical. Highland Middle's Spanish-course cut, effective September 2024, interrupts the older pathway: no reinstatement of academic immersion was verified, so it is excluded from the reference working set. Atherton's undated district resource listing is distinct from its earlier planned start and needs current course-level confirmation. The state page's “at least 50%” definition is not an exact universal 50/50 allocation.

Virginia sources support hosts in sixteen divisions. Fairfax's dated 2025–26 admissions letter and current school/pathway table distinguish Spanish, French, German, Japanese and Korean; Ravensworth is Spanish. Ravensworth's new kindergarten intake is a 2026–27 entry-grade expansion, not a new Spanish programme. Bailey's Upper is a 3–5 host and remains in the elementary denominator. Designated Fairfax 7–8 transition courses are retained without assigning primary-school allocation to them.

Arlington's original dated rollout image gives **2025–26 Spanish/English shares of 80/20 K–2, 70/30 grade 3 and 50/50 grades 4–5**. Its previous 50/50 model and 2023–24 transition start remain separate. Alexandria's 2022 handbook assigns Mount Vernon 50/50 and John Adams a 70/30 entry model. The current district-linked handbook assigns both schools 70/30 K–1 and 50/50 grades 2–5. Both source vintages remain, with effective change dates unresolved. Its inconsistent preschool “90:30” table cell is not converted to a K–12 allocation.

Virginia Beach has three one-way 50/50 programmes beginning in grade 1, two two-way 80/20 entry programmes progressing to 50/50, two established secondary continuations and two secondary starts anticipated for 2027–28. Winchester includes Daniel Morgan Intermediate (grades 5–6) in the elementary denominator; only its grade-5 allocation is assigned as elementary. Quarles has conflicting grade-rollout descriptions, retained for review. John Kerr's 2026–27 start and Mountain View Upper's July 2026 opening remain outside the reference set. Mountain View's original K–5 host changed from strands to whole-school provision in 2024–25. Palmer's historical Newport News programme consolidated into Saunders in 2017–18; current transportation eligibility does not make Palmer a current host. Newport News's two early-childhood centres are preschool-only leads.

Virginia's nine reference allocation gaps comprise five hosts without a verified numerical instructional model in the checked sources—Loudoun's three hosts, Broad Rock in Richmond and Holladay in Henrico—and four Prince William hosts with numerical policies explicitly dated **2026–27**. The later Prince William models are preserved without backdating them into the reference numerator. Loudoun's pupil-language balance and out-of-zone seat quota are not time allocations. Harrisonburg's legacy district language resource supplies an explicit 50/50 policy applied to current same-district Spanish elementary strands with a provenance/current-effective-date review flag. Widewater's ratio comes from a school-staff-authored directory entry with an explicit 2024–25 scope, supported by the current official district pilot description.

West Virginia's North Elementary directory lead and current Chinese enrichment description do not establish academic content immersion. Cheat Lake's 2018 STARTALK programme is explicitly a summer camp. State-operated Schools for the Deaf and the Blind provide core academics with ASL, spoken English and individualized access, but the checked documents do not establish a structured ASL-English academic bilingual immersion programme. Braille is not recorded as a separate language. These are scope questions for future investigation, not negative findings about all programmes in the state.
