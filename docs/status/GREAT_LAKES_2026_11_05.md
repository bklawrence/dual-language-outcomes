# Wisconsin, Michigan, Illinois and Indiana

First structural pass researched November 5, 2026: **306 programme records at 294 provisional school/campus units**, supported by **177 saved public documents**. Wisconsin, Michigan and Indiana are new represented states; Illinois expands its earlier seed. The national dataset now represents **25 states**, with 25 remaining. This is a partial inventory, not a statewide census.

Operation is assessed against **2025–26**. Each configuration, language and allocation keeps its source period. Undated or older descriptions accepted by bounded continuity do not become newly observed annual configurations. Future entry-cohort lists do not establish earlier school operation.

Coverage measures numerical allocation information within the confirmed qualifying working inventory. It includes schools/programme hosts serving any K–5 grade, including 3–5 and 5–6. It excludes hosts starting at grade 6+. A numerical model at one strand establishes school-level information availability; it does not mean every strand has a supported model. School-grade proxies and conflicts remain flagged. Published policies and model labels do not establish observed exposure or exact daily minutes.

## Findings and remaining gaps

**Wisconsin:** Milwaukee publishes Spanish bilingual K5 90/10, while Waukesha publishes K–1 80/20 and Verona Spanish and Mandarin each have independent 50/50 support. Madison’s source percentages and Sheboygan’s 50/50 describe pupil backgrounds, so their time models remain unfilled. French immersion has a published 90/10 label; German and Spanish full-immersion descriptions do not supply a quantified K day. Madison includes upper-elementary Lincoln (3–5), plus named secondary continuations. The Carey rename is recorded as school history, not a second school. Lowell’s placement before the 2026–27 Waukesha reorganization needs review.

Waadookodaading is documented Ojibwe academic-medium K–8 education with BIE affiliation and English ELA from grade 4, rather than an elective. Its old half-day pilot is not a current ratio. Hmong American Immersion School has verified public-charter status and a fall-2025 opening, but available descriptions do not establish how academic content is taught through Hmong; it remains a medium-of-instruction screening question, not a funding question. Menasha’s operator names Banta, Maplewood and Menasha High. Victory Italian immersion closed in June 2025 and continued as an Italian language class; the proposed Hi-Mount relaunch is 2027–28. Doty, Savanna Oaks and Central Heights also have explicit later starts. Delavan-Darien’s K 50/50 policy is saved; host mapping remains in the discovery queue.

**Michigan:** Forest Hills has distinct Spanish early-total and Mandarin 50/50 models. Northern Trails 5/6 stays in the elementary denominator; middle/high continuation shares are not assigned its kindergarten model. FLICS has four language strands at one school: Mandarin, French, Japanese and Spanish. Niji-Iro is Japanese/English two-way immersion; its saved page does not quantify time. El Sol explicitly publishes 50/50 and Los Lagos K 90/10, corroborated by dated December 2025 school news. Jenison, Zeeland, Rockford and Holland have academic immersion and continuation evidence, but qualitative full immersion and English specials do not justify an invented numerical split. Neinas, directory-only schools and private-school funding/academic-scope checks remain screening work. Academy of the Americas’ Cub/Tiger campuses remain one provisional reporting unit pending official school-ID reconciliation; high-school 50/50 is not its K 90/10 model.

**Illinois:** All 16 original registry schools, 12 programme IDs, sites and original claims are preserved. Current evidence now establishes DLE at formerly excluded Darwin and Talcott and bilingual programmes at Yankee Ridge, without duplicate school identities. Lozano remains an unresolved legacy programme; absence from a directory does not prove an ending. Chicago’s current 49-host roster reconciles to 45 confirmed K–12 hosts, three explicit 2026–27 new hosts, and Thomas preschool in screening. The 2025 release totals include that preschool; the 2026 release contains inconsistent aggregate counts, so no unlisted schools are invented. Bateman’s K 80/20 and AGC’s 80/20 label are school-specific.

U-46’s PDF page 10 names 27 incoming-K/1 hosts for 2025–26 under an 80:20 heading and documents the change from separate OW/TW entry structures to a unified programme. The nine-host 2024–25 diagram and its feeder schools are not substituted for that roster. North Shore’s Red Oak operated in the reference year; closure/consolidation is planned for after 2026–27. Evanston’s Bessie Rhodes DLI was grades 6/7 in 2025–26, followed by closure and relocation; the school’s historical K–8 range is not assigned to the DLI strand. Barrington’s location chart is an entry-cohort chart: later rows do not resolve older-cohort hosts in 2025–26. Spanish 80/20 and Chinese 50/50 remain distinct. IPA’s school/2025 plan 90/10 conflicts with the district’s undated 50/50 overview; both are retained for review. Woodstock publishes two models without a verified school assignment, and Crystal Lake quantifies literacy only, so their day models are held.

**Indiana:** All 44 named IDOE directory rows retain their original school, district, language/type and grade cells. The directory includes enrolment-only 2023–24 notes and is not a 2025 grade census. The 2022 grant announcement’s minimum 50% target-language criterion does not label every programme 50/50. Current sources add Clifty Creek/Legado 80/20, Potter 50/50, Lawrenceburg and Warren 50/50, and Lawrence Township’s 80/20 entry progression. Two ELC K models use the user-authorized same-district/language/type inference rule and retain named donors. Grade-1-only two-way strands do not acquire K allocations. Sunnyside’s older one-way/current two-way descriptions retain their separate source periods. Stonybrook includes grade 5 and remains elementary; Westlane starts at grade 6 and is excluded. Warsaw has named K–6 hosts and secondary continuations, with grade 10 explicitly later. Benjamin Harrison implementation and Global Prep’s campus dates/identities remain review questions.

## Preservation and validation

The new batch checks exact school/programme sets and retains the original Illinois rows. Historical identity checks permit additions only when the saved original IDs reproduce the independently frozen earlier checksum; the new audit separately rejects unreviewed additions. Full source hashes, the Indiana directory, CPS roster and U-46 chart reconcile offline. Portable rebuilds include supporting screening sources as well as programme citations.

All 12 existing assessment/context/EL/SEDA outputs and 78 earlier dated reports are guarded by checksums. New proficiency, demographic, EL-exit and SEDA observations remain deferred until the structural inventory phase is complete. Validation verifies preservation, grain and recorded source boundaries; it does not independently certify every programme statement or statewide completeness.
