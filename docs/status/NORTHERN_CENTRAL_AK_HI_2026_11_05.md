# North Dakota, South Dakota, Minnesota, Iowa, Missouri, Alaska and Hawaiʻi

First structural pass, researched November 5, 2026. **187 programme records at 172 provisional school/campus units**, supported by **115 saved public source documents**. These are reviewed evidence groups, including candidates, historical hosts and future starts; they are not a statewide census. The national registry now represents 22 states, with 28 states remaining.

Operation is assessed against **2025–26**. Each configuration, language and allocation retains its actual source period. An undated description does not become a 2025–26 annual observation. Continuity confirmations have saved, bounded reviews; they do not assert that every board archive was searched.

Published classroom-only allocations are labelled as such. They do not establish a full-day clock-time split or a named-language minute plan. Hmong International Academy’s single “50%” description and 120 literacy minutes remain unresolved rather than being synthesized into 50/50.

## Findings and boundaries

**Hawaiʻi:** Every one of the state Kaiapuni directory’s 34 named rows is reconciled: 27 HIDOE and seven charter hosts. The two satellites under Nāwahī Iki remain attached to their listed school. The directory states that classes use Hawaiian exclusively, with English introduced as a subject in grade 5. This supports 100/0 in the stated primary grades for the Kaiapuni strand. It does not label English-medium strands or secondary programmes 100/0. The Prince Jonah Kūhiō Kalanianaʻole launch is explicitly 2026–27 and is excluded from 2025–26 confirmation. Expanded later grades are not backdated. HSDB is separately retained because its own description explicitly establishes ASL-English bilingual academic instruction; no clock-time split is invented.

**Alaska:** Anchorage’s directory reconciles to 18 school-language cells at 16 school units; Romig and West each have Spanish and Russian records. Dated brochures and school descriptions preserve half-day models and source-vintage grade differences. Ayaprun’s 2024 building opening and earlier addition of grades 7–8 are distinct from programme start. TCLL’s academic Tlingit medium and May 2025 district-funding discussion are retained. The current district page still identifies Harborview; no speculative campus move is made. Preschool-only Indigenous programmes and planned secondary destinations remain screening leads.

**Minnesota:** Minneapolis publishes an 80/20 Spanish entry policy, Robbinsdale 85/15, Richfield 90/10, and St. Cloud distinct Chinese 90/10 and Spanish 80/20 models. Northfield starts in grade 1. Spring Lake Park and Moorhead include grade-5/6 hosts in elementary coverage. The 2023 Ellen Hopkins-to-Probstfield move and Rochester’s Gage-to-Riverside/Willow Creek transition prevent old host names from inflating current counts. Academia Cesar Chavez/In Lak’ech remains one provisional school identity. Kennedy’s 2026–27 Spanish start is separate. Bdote remains a historical discovery lead because the operator pages returned 404 and no reliable ending report was found; failed retrieval is not evidence of closure. Discovery outside the represented districts and tribal-school funding checks remain incomplete.

**Iowa:** The West Liberty 2025–26 kindergarten notice explicitly supports 50/50. Its matching elementary programme uses a flagged same-programme inference under the user’s rule. Sioux City names Irving and both East/West middle continuations. West Des Moines identifies Western Hills and Stilwell, not unrelated elementary campuses. Woodbury’s dated principal explanation gives K 70/30 and grades 1–4 50/50. St. Anthony and Pella Christian remain reference-year funding candidates: later 2026–27 ESA participation information is saved without asserting 2025–26 programme funding. Preschool subsidies do not establish K–12 support.

**Missouri:** Foreign Language Academy has distinct Spanish and Mandarin Chinese strands. SLLIS has French, Spanish and Chinese strands at one school; its source says Chinese without independently verifying a variety. Language of the Day and Gomez & Gomez branding are not converted into numerical instructional ratios. Carthage’s 2024–25 packet supports 50/50 elementary instruction and named continuations. Grade-six-only hosts stay outside elementary coverage. Académie Lafayette’s two elementary campuses and Armour middle campus remain separate; the high school is a screening lead until its immersion scope is established.

**South Dakota:** Sonia Sotomayor’s classroom-only Spanish entry description is distinct from Rosa Parks/Hayward’s documented equal-language strands. Edison and Lincoln retain their continuation roles. Ben Reifel and George McGovern’s announced fall-2025 expansions still need implementation evidence. Community/nonprofit Lakota academic immersion is retained as funding candidates, distinct from Rapid City’s historical public-school pilot and from preschool nests.

**North Dakota:** The July 2023 college board-approved policy identifies school-age Wičhákini Owáyawa within the merged Wahóȟpi Kiŋ programme. It independently describes the shared Dakota/Lakota immersion environment; the earlier preschool-only 100% statement is not used as kindergarten evidence. Operation is accepted by bounded continuity, but public funding for the nonpublic school remains unresolved. A zero qualifying denominator therefore means **coverage is not established**, not 0% coverage or no DL schools statewide. Dakota/Lakota wording is not turned into two independent strands. NDSD interpreter/IEP services alone do not establish a separate dual-language academic model.
