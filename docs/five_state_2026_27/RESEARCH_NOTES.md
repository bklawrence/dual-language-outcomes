# Research notes and verification priorities

Reference year: 2026–27. Research date: November 3, 2026, America/Chicago.

## Source coverage and limits

All five states received a roster pass and a second pass for kindergarten
allocation, structure, and current operation. State education-agency resources
were checked before district and campus sources. California supplied a usable
statewide historical school roster; the other states required district,
school-operator, tribal-language, and state-report research. No statewide
coverage percentage is established, including for the smaller states.

Official public district/campus materials supply most program claims. Montana
also uses a state-official-authored report hosted by Education Northwest and a
September 2026 OPI report. Clearly labeled external reporting, a professional
association, and funder case studies supplement specific claims. Search results
were discovery leads, never evidence-ledger substitutes. An official site's
copyright year, crawl date, or current accessibility does not confirm 2026–27
operation. Dated enrollment materials establish announced current-year offerings;
they do not independently audit enrollment or classroom implementation.

The source register contains 71 documents: 61 official district/school-operator
sources, four state-agency sources, one state-official-authored external report,
two independent news reports, two funder reports, and one professional-association
directory. Sixty-one documents directly support program claims; the remaining
registered documents support screening, context, or unresolved leads.

Source excerpts are concise paraphrases unless identified as quotations. The
source register stores retrieval URLs, access times, and response hashes. Most
HTML/PDF originals remain in the working research cache outside the repository;
the CDE workbook and sanitized LAUSD directory rows are retained in the deliverable.
This is an evidence-linked inventory, not a complete archived copy of every website.

## Wyoming

The 12 records cover Natrona, Campbell, Teton, Laramie County 1, and a St. Stephens
Indigenous-language candidate. Natrona explicitly names Paradise Valley
(Mandarin), Park (Spanish), and Verda James (Spanish) in 2026–27 kindergarten
enrollment materials. Their kindergarten ratios remain unknown: general
immersion terminology does not establish 50/50. Secondary Natrona continuations
are retained separately without invented kindergarten allocations.

Stocktrail's school-linked program page documents kindergarten 50/50 and a
completed 2026–27 lottery. Its waitlist paragraph contains an inconsistent
January 19, 2025 cutoff; the inconsistency is noted. Munger Mountain has explicit
2026–27 admission evidence plus district documentation of a dedicated K–5
English–Spanish two-way 50/50 school. It is the only record with all five essential
fields resolved and explicit current-year evidence.

Hobbs documents Spanish 50/50 and the coexistence of immersion and traditional
classrooms, supporting strand. A district page under Afflerbach repeats Hobbs'
name; this is an unresolved campus-assignment lead, not a second assumed school.
Campbell County High School's continuation requires content-course verification.

St. Stephens' Arapaho source describes a proposal with an optimistic August 2020
start. Current implementation was not established. Tribal heritage is not
evidence of an additional Shoshone instructional track. Priorities are current
confirmation of Hobbs and secondary pathways, Natrona starting ratios and
structures, and the status of St. Stephens' proposal.

## Montana

The seven records deliberately preserve Indigenous-language provision rather
than treating Spanish–English as the sole model. Historical state-official
reporting documents KW Bergen's Blackfeet kindergarten 50/50 class, Wyola's Crow
program, and Crow Agency. A single documented classroom does not itself prove
that other classrooms coexist, so KW Bergen's structure remains unknown.

The September 2026 state Indian Language Preservation report describes content
work involving Crow Agency and Pretty Eagle Catholic Academy, including
field/lab learning and public support. Fiscal-year reporting is not explicit
2026–27 school-year evidence; both remain current-year unconfirmed.

Nk̓ʷusm's Salish school and the Piegan Institute's Cuts Wood Blackfeet school
remain candidates pending public-funding and/or multilingual content-scope
verification. English academic instruction is not assumed in Indigenous
immersion. Historic Moccasin Flat and Lost Children names are not invented as
additional active campuses.

Paxson's Spanish immersion ended after a June 2022 board decision; ongoing
Spanish language classes do not establish continued content immersion. Its
record is retained as `confirmed_discontinued`, excluded from operating counts.

No Montana record has explicit 2026–27 confirmation. Highest priorities are
current tribal-school and public-funding rosters, Crow and Blackfeet program
continuation, kindergarten schedules, and school structure. Further screening
should include additional state-funded tribal projects rather than assuming the
small collected roster is exhaustive.

## Idaho

The 18 records cover Boise, Jerome, Kuna, Jefferson Joint 251, Blaine, Nampa,
Teton 401, Twin Falls, American Falls, and Chief Tahgee. Spanish and Mandarin
programs are separate; Chief Tahgee's Shoshoni mission is retained as a candidate
because sustained multilingual academic content remains insufficiently specified.
A Shoshone–Bannock community affiliation does not establish a separate Bannock
instructional track.

Driggs and Bickel have explicit 2026–27 kindergarten enrollment evidence and
supported 50/50 models. Bickel's intake includes kindergarten and limited first
grade; a full operating K–5 immersion span is not assumed from a new launch.
Jefferson's campus/model documentation supports Spanish at Jefferson, Roberts,
and Harwood and Mandarin at South Fork. A district-wide 2026 agreement that does
not name participating campuses is insufficient to confirm every campus in
2026–27.

Alturas explicitly serves all students through English and Spanish for half
the day, supporting whole-school 50/50. New Horizons documents schoolwide
English–Spanish instruction, but its kindergarten starting ratio remains
unknown. Jerome's middle-school and Kuna's secondary content continuations have
separate records; elementary ratios are not automatically copied to them.

Hillcrest's official kindergarten registration page explicitly offers dual
immersion for 2026–27. It does not name the languages. October 2022 Idaho
Education News reporting associates American Falls with Spanish–English
immersion. The current campus language track is consequently a flagged candidate,
excluded from qualifying confirmed-program counts. The report's general
multi-district half-day description is not used to infer Hillcrest's K ratio.

Priorities are Hillcrest's current track/model, current-year campus rosters in
Boise, Jerome, Kuna and Jefferson, and school-structure documentation across
the state. Secondary listings need exact content courses and grade spans.

## Nevada

Six records cover Washoe, Clark County, and public charter provision. Mount Rose
documents kindergarten **90/10**, despite later convergence to 50/50. Its school
title and association descriptions are insufficient to establish whole-school
structure from the official source. Jessie Beck is documented in a 2024 Washoe
board presentation; target-year continuation remains unconfirmed.

Strong Start Academy has explicit 2026–27 calendar/enrollment context and
schoolwide English–Spanish academic provision. No supported kindergarten ratio
was found. A handbook's 90% attendance provision is not a language allocation.
C. C. Ronnow's opt-in program and 2024–25 brochure support strand 50/50, but the
source year is not silently promoted to 2026–27.

Robert E. Lake is retained using a clearly labeled Nevada bilingual-education
association directory and an official school registration link; detailed
official program content was not accessible. An alternating second-grade day
description does not establish the kindergarten starting ratio. Mariposa remains
a candidate: bilingual literacy and a historical public charter agreement do
not alone establish the sustained academic-content model or a current K ratio.

The state agency page did not enumerate schools. No public Mandarin content
program was established, which does not mean none exists. Commercial search
guides using “official” in a title are not state rosters. Priorities are current
Washoe/Clark rosters, charter model and structure evidence, and non-Spanish leads.

## California

The 271 curated records are a substantial district sample, not a statewide
census. The CDE 2024–25 workbook supplies **1,310 unique school CDS IDs across
302 districts**. It generally does not establish language tracks, starting
ratios, structure, or 2026–27 operation. Twenty-five schools report only
transitional bilingual education; all source schools remain separately labeled
historical screening leads. The curated and screening counts overlap and must
not be added together.

LAUSD's official page embeds a public directory. Its 211 rows resolve to **209
school–program records**: Castelar's Mandarin ES/MS rows and Reseda's Spanish
one-way MS/HS rows are consolidated as grade segments of the same program.
Original row IDs and grade descriptions remain traceable. Different languages,
and explicitly distinct one-way/two-way programs at one campus, remain separate.
The directory's school-year applicability is undated; row creation timestamps
are not current-year verification. Secondary rows are candidates until actual
content instruction is established.

Officially listed LAUSD 50/50, 90/10, and 70/30 models are accepted as K starting
designations. “N/A” is not converted into a ratio. Sixteen elementary program
records have a strand classification because the official directory establishes
parallel distinct-language tracks in overlapping grades. This describes the
individual program's coexistence with another program, not an assumed
English-only track. Benjamin Franklin's French, German, Italian and Spanish
programs similarly coexist; each has its own record and evidence.

Other collected districts/programs include:

- **SFUSD:** Cantonese, Mandarin, Korean and Spanish, including Alice Fong Yu.
  The detailed allocation page is historically dated 2019. Its explicit
  kindergarten 80/20 schedules are retained; a Cantonese Pre-K 70% schedule is
  not substituted. A generic 2026–27 application without a campus roster does
  not confirm every historical campus. Alice Fong Yu's secondary Mandarin
  language class is not a separate Mandarin immersion program.
- **Pasadena:** five elementary French, Mandarin and Spanish programs at 90/10;
  explicit alternatives support strands at Altadena, Jackson and Longfellow.
  A January 2026 guide does not explicitly identify SY2026–27. Webster Armenian,
  Washington Spanish and Willard Spanish language courses are screened separately.
- **Glendale:** elementary Eastern Armenian, French, German, Italian, Japanese,
  Korean and Spanish; separate secondary language-elective candidates are flagged.
  Spanish middle-school content pathways have stronger historical evidence.
  Glenoaks' Western Armenian opening is **2027–28**, retained as planned future.
- **Westminster:** explicit 2026–27 campus rosters establish six Spanish or
  Vietnamese pathways. Four elementary programs document 50/50. Middle-school
  K allocations and school structures remain unknown.
- **Franklin-McKinley:** Windmill Springs Vietnamese 50/50 and Meadows Spanish
  90/10. The web page combines a Fall 2026 heading with older 2025 FAQs, so the
  ambiguity is retained rather than treated as campus-specific current proof.
- **Fresno:** a July 2026 brochure explicitly lists 2026–27 Hmong DLI at Vang Pao,
  Balderas and Sequoia Middle. The elementary K schedule is 140 minutes Hmong
  and 140 English, supporting 50/50. Sequoia's content scope remains a candidate.
  Separately listed high-school Hmong Heritage Speaker courses are not assumed
  to be DLI. Older one-way terminology versus newer balanced-enrollment wording
  leaves program organization unresolved.
- **Sacramento:** Spanish at Bowling Green Chacon and Cesar Chavez, Cantonese at
  Elder Creek, Hmong at Susan B. Anthony, and Mandarin at William Land. Ethel
  Phillips is categorized as early-exit transitional bilingual despite the parent
  page's “Dual Immersion” heading and is screened out.

**Yu Ming conflict:** the official school page presents K–2 as 90% Mandarin and
10% English; a 2025 NewSchools case study explicitly describes kindergarten as
85%/15%. Both claims are retained. The selected K allocation and percentages
remain unknown pending clarification; 90/10 is preserved as the published label.
The external source supports schoolwide participation. The school's multiple
campus locations have not been independently enumerated as distinct programs.

**Indigenous-language leads:** Weitchpec's Yurok magnet description establishes
cultural emphasis but not the actual language of academic instruction; it is a
candidate with instructional languages unconfirmed. Hupa Xine:wh-ding reporting
describes a toddler language nest and plans for a fuller school. It does not
establish a current publicly funded K program and is retained as a research
lead rather than an invented operating-school record.

California's largest gap is converting the statewide leads into current
campus/language-track records outside the sampled districts. Next priorities
are current-year LAUSD and SFUSD confirmation; secondary content-course checks;
structure evidence; Yu Ming's schedule; and charters, Indigenous-language
programs, and uncollected large districts. Do not interpret 271 records divided
by 1,310 historical schools as a statewide coverage rate.
